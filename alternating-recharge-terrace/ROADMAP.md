# Alternating Recharge Terrace — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Research concept; advancement requires permits, safety evidence and a defensible comparator, not a calendar date.

## Concept

Survey candidate soils, native permeability, aquifer depth, flood hydraulics, water rights and source chemistry with local authorities. Draw the two 25 m² beds, 1 m³ forebay and positively identifiable bypass and price the full installed and operating system, including surveys and testing. Gate: identify a lawful site and a site-specific flood-safe bypass and contamination stop plan; otherwise retire the concept there.

## Prototype

Construct one instrumented, approved two-bed unit and a same-footprint single-bed comparator where feasible. Measure inlet, bypass, bed drawdown, sand replacement hours, embankment and overflow behavior over at least three qualifying wet/dry cycles; observe water quality and groundwater separately. Gate: show repeatable operation without unsafe ponding or groundwater-quality deterioration, not merely the 0.03 m/h unverified flux target.

## Pilot

Run 20 permitted dual-bed sites versus 20 matched single-bed controls for two wet seasons. Pre-register the ≥20% cumulative qualified infiltration per 50 m² footprint target; independently audit labor, costs, downstream allocation, mounding and observation-well chemistry. Gate: no material safety or downstream-flow deterioration and a demonstrable incremental benefit after matching site conditions; a failed comparison stops scale-up.

## Scale

If pilot gates pass, publish open fabrication and monitoring specifications and train local crews; acquire site-by-site permits rather than applying blanket approval. At an illustrative 1,000 installations/year, revisit the unquoted US$1,300 hardware/installation goal and US$100–200 annual maintenance budget against actual all-in lifecycle costs. Gate: audited aquifer and downstream outcomes, affordable paid operation and proven safe bypasses across varied sites.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, consider adoption only in hydrogeologically suitable and legally approved catchments, with public water accounting and independent quality checks. A 10,000-site network at 15 eligible six-hour events/year has a 675,000 m³/year surface-infiltration planning ceiling, not assured net recharge. Gate: recurrent verification of aquifer benefit, water rights, maintenance funding and transparent retirement of underperforming sites.
