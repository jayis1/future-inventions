# Altitude-Qualified Lake Drain — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1** · Conceptual scenario arithmetic, not field measurements, an approved intervention, or an estimate of lives saved. The hydraulic failure of the 2025 Thorthormi siphon is a cautionary comparator, not evidence that this module works.[2]

## Assumptions and method

| Variable | Illustrative value | Status / uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating discharge | 5 L/s = 0.005 m³/s | Unverified 72 h bench target; actual field discharge may be zero or below permitted rate. |
| Operating period | 30 days, 24 h/day | Illustrative continuous operation; outages and seasonal ice reduce it. |
| Constant lake surface area | 1 ha = 10,000 m² | Hypothetical small site; real area varies with level. |
| Concurrent inflow | 1, 3 or 5 L/s | Assumed sustained cases, not forecast hydrology; precipitation, evaporation and groundwater ignored. |
| Net lowering | max(0, discharge − inflow) × seconds / area | Volume balance only; not a stability or flood-risk model. Negative values would mean lake rise, not drain benefit. |
| Hardware | US$3,000 per 100 m module in 2038 at 1,000 kits/year | Unquoted production target from README; excludes all site survey, works, logistics, approvals and operations. |

At the target 5 L/s, gross transfer is 432 m³/day or 12,960 m³ in 30 days. A nominal 0.5 m drawdown of a constant 1 ha lake needs 5,000 m³ *net* removal. These are arithmetic checks only; water level depends on measured inflows and geometry.

## Thirty-day single-site sensitivity

| Case (assumed inflow) | Net outflow | Net 30-day volume | Modeled lowering on 1 ha | Time to 0.5 m, if conditions hold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Favorable (1 L/s) | 4 L/s | 10,368 m³ | 1.04 m | at least 15 days |
| Base illustration (3 L/s) | 2 L/s | 5,184 m³ | 0.52 m | at least 29 days |
| Unfavorable (5 L/s) | 0 L/s | 0 m³ | 0 m | never under these conditions |

These cases assume the entire 30 days meet hydraulic and permitting conditions. For the base 5,184 m³ net volume, a 0.5–2 ha constant surface area would imply approximately 1.04–0.26 m of lowering; an actual stage–storage curve must replace this shortcut. A permitted rate below 5 L/s or any loss of prime reduces these estimates further; if inflow exceeds outflow, the lake can rise. None of these numbers demonstrates dam stability or reduced outburst probability.

## Ten-site conditional illustration

If ten *independently qualified* 1 ha sites all met the base illustration for 30 days, aggregate net removal would be 51,840 m³, with each site's modeled reduction around 0.52 m—not 5.2 m at any one lake. Ten modules would cost US$30,000 at the **unquoted future hardware target**; this is not a pilot budget. Site rejection, installed civil works, transport, field staffing, permits, warnings, maintenance and removal could dominate total cost. Compare actual full lifecycle costs per site with an engineered channel or monitoring-only plan before claiming any economic advantage.

Potential benefit is a documented, bounded reduction in stored water at an eligible site. Translating that volume to avoided damage requires a site-specific breach/outburst model, exposed-asset inventory and uncertainty distribution; **avoided lives and US$ losses are not estimable from these assumptions**. The published global exposure estimate of about 15 million people is context, not this device's addressable market or beneficiaries.[1]

Potential downside includes induced moraine instability from drawdown, local downstream scour or flooding during operation, disturbed habitat, crew exposure at altitude, transport emissions and unrecovered HDPE. Geotechnical stop criteria, approved receiving-channel capacity, seasonal shutdown, local consent and a funded retrieval plan are prerequisites, not benefits already delivered. If any fails, do not deploy; track both benefits and incidents in an independently reviewed pilot.
