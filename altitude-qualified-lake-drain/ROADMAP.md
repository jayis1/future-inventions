# Altitude-Qualified Lake Drain — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Concept-stage gates, not a deployment authorization. Each phase stops if a qualified civil/geotechnical authority finds unacceptable downstream or moraine risk.

## Concept

Years 0–3: assemble surveyed candidate profiles, pressure and temperature bounds, hypsometry, receiving-channel capacity and a rejection checklist; peer-review the siphon pressure/loss model against the published failed Thorthormi configuration. Exit only with an independently checked no-go envelope and a documented comparison to conventional channeling and no intervention.

## Prototype

Years 3–7: build a 100 mm-bore, ≤100 m HDPE test line in a closed flume, then trial it at a noncritical reservoir. At a 5 m surface-to-outlet drop, ≤3 m crest lift and ≥60 kPa equivalent pressure, seek ≥5 L/s for 72 h without unplanned loss of prime; measure apex pressure and water balance, then repeat with air leaks, debris and temperature extremes. Exit only on reproducible performance, safe manual isolation and an independent safety review; failed targets trigger redesign or cancellation.

## Pilot

Years 7–12: seek site-specific regulator and community approval for a small moraine-dammed lake with safe access, verified pressure margin and a rated downstream channel. Fund bathymetry, geotechnical stability, ecological assessment, warning/evacuation plans and daily monitoring before installation. Exit only after a regulator-supervised seasonal campaign documents net drawdown, inflow, seepage, scour, incidents and a full installed-cost comparison; suspend immediately on predefined movement or outlet thresholds.

## Scale

Years 12–20: compare multiple independently reviewed pilots with channels, monitoring-only and nonintervention at matched site types. Standardize a reusable hardware kit and site-rejection protocol only if field reliability and adverse effects are credibly measured; independently audit maintenance, insurance, retrieval and total lifecycle costs. No automatic deployment to unassessed lakes.

## Ubiquity

Beyond year 20, a validated qualification protocol—not necessarily this siphon—could be routine in glacial-lake hazard plans. A regional agency would maintain an audited register of suitable and unsuitable sites, train local inspectors, retain independent warnings and budget for replacement/retrieval. Exit criterion is sustained institutional oversight and evidence of net benefit across sites; a future failure of those conditions reverses any scale-up decision.
