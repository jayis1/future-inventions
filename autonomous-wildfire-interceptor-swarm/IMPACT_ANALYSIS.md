## Limitations of This Analysis

1. **Covered vs. uncovered zones:** Projections apply to areas within AWIS coverage. Global wildfire reduction depends on deployment scale. Uncovered regions continue to experience current wildfire rates.
2. **Climate feedback nonlinearity:** As AWIS reduces emissions and protects carbon sinks, the climate benefit compounds — but quantifying this requires coupled climate-fire models beyond this analysis.
3. **Adaptation lag:** Forests and ecosystems damaged by decades of catastrophic fire will take decades to recover. AWIS stops the bleeding but does not instantly restore ecosystem health.
4. **Human factors:** Arson, utility negligence, and accidental ignitions will continue. AWIS suppresses fires regardless of cause, but addressing root causes (grid hardening, education) remains necessary.
5. **Excluded benefits:** Reduced fire insurance premiums, restored property values, avoided mental health costs, preserved biodiversity, and maintained ecosystem services are not fully monetized here — actual total benefit is likely 1.5–3× higher than stated.
