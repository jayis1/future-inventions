# AWIS Development Roadmap

## Phase 1 — Concept & Component Validation (2026–2028)

The foundational phase focuses on de-risking the three core technical innovations individually. Acoustic flame extinction is validated in outdoor field tests against 0.1–1 m flames using PVDF transducer prototypes, scaling up from DARPA IFE lab results. The metamaterial suppression blanket is prototyped via roll-to-roll lamination and tested against ASTM E84 (surface burning) and E108 (fire-resistance) standards, with iterative optimization of the aerogel/intumescent/PCM layer ratios. The interceptor drone airframe is built from COTS components with a custom VTOL quad-plane design, achieving 25+ minute endurance with full payload. A stratospheric glider prototype demonstrates 7-day continuous flight at 18+ km altitude using perovskite-silicon tandem solar cells. Multi-modal detection AI (SWIR + LWIR + VOC fusion) is trained on controlled-burn datasets. Estimated cost: $15–30M. Exit criteria: all four components independently validated at TRL 5.

## Phase 2 — Regional Pilot (2028–2031)

Five regional AWIS installations are deployed in high-risk fire zones: California (USA), Attica (Greece), Victoria (Australia), central Portugal, and central Chile. Each installation includes 10 stratospheric gliders, ~200 interceptor drones, and 10–25 solar charging docks — a deliberately over-instrumented pilot to validate the full detection-to-suppression pipeline under real wildfire conditions. BVLOS regulatory approvals are secured per region (FAA Part 108, EASA U-space, CASA, ANAC). The pilot measures: detection latency, false-positive rate, suppression success rate, cost per event, and integration with existing fire management agencies. Blanket supply chain is established with Aspen Aerogels or equivalent. Estimated cost: $25–50M. Exit criteria: ≥70% of detected ignitions suppressed at <0.1 ha, <2% false-positive rate, full-night and high-wind operation demonstrated.

## Phase 3 — Scale-Up (2031–2035)

Deployment expands to 30 regional installations globally, covering the highest-risk fire zones in North America, the Mediterranean, Australia, South America, and Southeast Asia. Component costs are driven down through volume manufacturing: interceptor drones <$2,000 (from $3,000), blankets <$10 each (from $35), docks <$10K (from $15K). Local manufacturing partnerships are established for blanket production (regional roll-to-roll lines) and dock assembly, reducing shipping costs and creating local jobs. Stratospheric glider endurance reaches 3+ months. The detection AI is refined with 5+ years of real-fire data, improving accuracy and reducing false positives to <0.5%. Integration protocols with national fire agencies are standardized. Estimated cost: $100–200M. Exit criteria: 30 regions operational, component cost targets met, documented suppression of ≥1,000 real fires.

## Phase 4 — Global Deployment (2035–2040)

100 regional installations cover all major fire-prone areas worldwide, protecting 5–20 Mha. The global fleet comprises 5,000+ stratospheric gliders, 100,000+ interceptor drones, and 2,500+ docks. AWIS is integrated into national fire management frameworks — agencies treat it as primary first response, with human crews focused on mop-up, prescribed burning, and large-fire-front management (the rare events that exceed AWIS's design envelope). An international AWIS coordination body (analogous to ICANN or the IPCC) manages cross-border deployment, data sharing, and equitable access for developing nations. Cost per hectare drops to $200–500/ha/year. The 2036–2040 fire seasons show the first measurable global decline in burned area and wildfire emissions in the satellite record. Estimated annual cost: $300–660M — less than 20% of current global suppression spending. Exit criteria: 100 regions operational, wildfire emissions declining year-over-year, system availability >99% during Red Flag conditions.

## Phase 5 — Ubiquity (2040–2045)

200+ regional installations achieve near-global coverage of fire-prone landscapes. Cost drops below $200/ha/year at full scale, making AWIS affordable for all fire-prone nations including low-income countries in Sub-Saharan Africa, Southeast Asia, and Latin America. The system evolves from reactive to predictive: AI models integrate lightning forecasting, grid-fault monitoring, vegetation moisture indices, and arson pattern detection to pre-position interceptors before ignitions occur, shortening the kill chain from 2–8 minutes to 30–60 seconds. AWIS interfaces with power utilities for automatic line de-energization before fault-induced ignitions, and with forest managers for fuel-load-aware deployment. Wildfire catastrophes — the megafires that defined the 2020s — become rare historical events rather than annual disasters. Cultural and prescribed burning is safely reintroduced in designated zones, with AWIS providing perimeter protection. The fire-climate feedback loop is broken. By 2045, the question is no longer "will this year's fire season be catastrophic?" but "which species' habitat are we managing with fire this year?"
