# Barn Urine Ammonia Recovery Panel — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**

**Status: proposed milestones, not completed field results**

## Concept

Survey barns with existing urine-only drainage, confirm a physically unobstructed bypass, and measure seasonal flow, TAN, total N, pH and channel NH₃-N emissions. Select only sites with a lawful acid-cartridge service and disposal route. Gate: an independent safety review accepts the channel interface and the baseline monitoring plan; otherwise stop before installing chemicals.

## Prototype

Build a removable PP cover, suspended ePTFE cassette, isolated recirculation loop and 20 L bunded receiver; bench-test leaks, acid reserve, pump shutdown and overflow with deliberate screen blockage. Measure membrane flux and total-N mass-balance closure at representative temperatures and pH, with uncertainty reported. Gate: no chemical-to-drain escape, intact drainage in all fault tests, and enough measured flux to make the 30 g N/day illustrative target plausible at 100 L/day and 1 g TAN/L.

## Pilot

Randomize matched uncovered, covered-only and covered-plus-panel channels over warm and cold seasons; keep barn ventilation unchanged. Track receiver N, paired NH₃-N flux, reagent use, energy, service labor, exposure and failed cartridges for at least 30 days per comparison. Gate: at least 30 g N/day receiver recovery on valid design-load days, at least 30% lower channel NH₃-N emissions than covered-only controls, and no unacceptable leaks or worker exposure; failures trigger redesign, not rollout.

## Scale

Run a ten-channel cooperative service route with weekly sealed cartridge exchange and batch assays; report actual availability against an 80% planning case and full cost per verified kg N, including the US$450 unquoted workshop target, installation, reagent, labor, replacement and disposal. Gate: independent life-cycle and safety review shows net benefit versus simple covers and current manure handling; product use requires local approval, otherwise qualified disposal.

## Ubiquity

Only after repeated multi-region seasonal trials, supplier quality controls, trained servicing, and enforceable product and drainage standards should new-build urine-separated housing offer the panel as an option. Keep an opt-out where flux, economics or safety fail; 2050 adoption is a conditional vision, not an installation target or claim that all barns can use it.
