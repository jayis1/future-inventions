# Batch-Locked Gasifier Cooktop — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**

**Status: conditional research plan, not a deployment claim.** Each phase proceeds only if its safety and usability gate passes. See [README](README.md) for the unverified design envelope and sources.

## Concept

Characterize three eligible local pellet feedstocks, moisture ranges, size distributions, pot/recipe demands and real household fuel prices. Build a hazard analysis for CO, PM₂.₅, blocked exhaust, power loss, burns and both latch failure directions; commission an independent review of ventilation and emergency-response assumptions. Exit only when cooking-energy and fuel-supply budgets and a testable gated-versus-ungated protocol are agreed. If basic safe-state design cannot be demonstrated, stop.

## Prototype

Build instrumented 0.5 kg basket prototypes with 304-stainless steel, a refractory liner, a 12 V fan and a serviceable mechanical latch. Independently measure full-cycle efficiency, ISO 19867-1 PM₂.₅ and CO, startup/shutdown and fan-failure transients for each qualified fuel; test at least 1,000 latch cycles without stuck-open failure and validate a safe cooldown indication. A 25% full-cycle efficiency and ≤3 Wh per nominal one-hour batch are targets, not results. Do not advance on a good steady-state burn alone; establish an acceptable exposure and fire-safety case against locally applicable requirements.

## Pilot

Only after safety approval, recruit 100 consenting households for the randomized crossover in the README, comparing the gated and otherwise identical ungated cooktop alongside actual household cooking. Monitor personal and kitchen PM₂.₅/CO, burns, hot-refuelling attempts, stacked-stove use, repair events, meal completion and delivered fuel costs across seasons. Gate: ≥80% fewer hot-refuelling events, ≥30% lower personal PM₂.₅ versus household baseline without higher CO, and ≥90% of intended meals completed without bypass; independently audit harms and withdraw units on unsafe trends. These are proposed thresholds, not predicted outcomes.

## Scale

If the pilot and independent regulatory review support deployment, establish repair training, batch-traceable manufacturing and locally qualified pellet supply. Validate the unquoted US$85 ex-factory goal only at 100,000 units/year, with estimated US$30–50 battery/charger separate; document installed cost, alarms, ventilation, parts and recurring fuel before financing decisions. Gate on repeatable factory emission/safety checks, replacement-part availability and audited sustained use rather than sales volume. Never market an unqualified biomass appliance as clean.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, conditional adoption could mean open, repairable fuel and spare-part standards wherever verified household exposure improves and cleaner fuels remain unavailable. Publish longitudinal comparisons against electric, solar, LPG and biogas alternatives and retire the cooktop wherever those options become affordable and safer. The exit criterion is sustained measured health-relevant exposure reduction with no CO penalty, not a device-count target or an assumed carbon-credit stream.
