# Batch-Witness Chlorination Kiosk — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Research concept, not an approved drinking-water device. Every gate requires qualified water-treatment oversight and an independently safe fallback supply.

## Concept

Map local source hazards, regulatory release criteria and ordinary batch-chlorinator practice; prototype the batch-ID log and normally closed latch on a dry rig. Exit only with a documented operating envelope, identified chemical/protozoal exclusions and a testable failure-state specification. Stop if the ordinary protected-storage process already provides equivalent assurance more simply.

## Prototype

Build the food-contact HDPE wet path and mechanically interlocked 40 L tank; bench-test fill-volume error, leakage, bypass, timer resets, power loss, mixing coverage, DPD control failures and repeated valve cycles with qualified staff. Exit when every injected fault holds the outlet closed and material compatibility, cleaning and waste protocols are demonstrated; redesign on any false-safe release.

## Pilot

With local authority approval, compare at least 200 paired batches against ordinary staffed batch chlorination on characterized source water across seasons. Independently assay outlet and stored-water microbial indicators, residual, pH, turbidity, byproducts, premature draws, false lockouts, and delivered cost per compliant litre. Exit only if treatment meets local limits and the interlock measurably improves verified compliance without delaying access; otherwise stop.

## Scale

Validate manufacturing inspection at the proposed 10,000-units/year *target* volume only if pilot evidence and service capacity support it. Train operators, secure reagent/filter/calibration supply, audit batch logs and maintain a backup safe source. Release sites in stages with independent water-quality surveillance and recall capability; suspend rollout for unexplained failures or missing consumables.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, offer the design as an optional auditable layer wherever local regulators and operators find it preferable to conventional protected storage; publish lifetime costs, failures and retirements. Expand only in source-water envelopes with sustained qualified support. Replace or remove units when safer piped water or better validated treatment becomes available.
