# Binder-Mapped Sand Reclaimer — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1.** Scenario model, not measured impact or a market forecast. Reference design: one nominal 1 t/hour module, eligible segregated silica sand with a laboratory-qualified organic binder. Annual accounting period: 2,000 actual operating hours; compare with the same foundries' current sand sourcing and disposal in that year. The 2050 vision is not an adoption forecast.

## Transparent material and power model

| Input or outcome | Illustrative ten-module calculation | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible feed treated | 10 modules × 1 t/h × 2,000 h/year = **20,000 t/year** | Hypothetical uptime and throughput, not demonstrated. |
| Qualified reclaimed sand | 20,000 t × 0.90 accepted yield = **18,000 t/year** | Proposed pilot target, conditional on receiving-foundry LOI, sieve and trial-casting approval. |
| Other inlet mass | 20,000 − 18,000 = **2,000 t/year** maximum *illustrative balance* at exactly 90% yield | Includes metals, fines, rejected sand and any unaccounted mass; binder combustion also sends mass to controlled off-gas, so this is **not** a prediction of 2,000 t of solid waste. Weigh solids and characterize exhaust separately. |
| Virgin sand actually displaced | 18,000 t × 0.80 substitution fraction = **14,400 t/year** | Illustrative assumption; verify with purchase records and matched production volumes. At 0% substitution, displacement is zero. |
| Purchased electricity | 20,000 t × 180 kWh/t = **3,600,000 kWh/year** | Upper design-target consumption at the assumed feed, including heater, fans and exhaust treatment; actual use may exceed this. |
| Electricity spending | 3,600,000 kWh × US$0.12/kWh = **US$432,000/year** | Illustrative energy-only price; excludes demand charges, labor, transport and residues. |

The 90% target gives **18,000 t of foundry-qualified output**, not automatically 18,000 t of avoided mining or landfill. At a 50–100% substitution range, the same hypothetical output displaces **9,000–18,000 t/year** of virgin purchases; both endpoints are assumptions to test, not forecasts. Ten modules' unquoted ex-works capital goal totals US$2.5 million, excluding installation and operating costs. Do not infer payback from electricity price alone.

## Net-effect decision gate

For each matched lot, report feed composition and moisture; purchased kWh (heater, pretreatment, fans and abatement); fresh-sand purchases; outbound residual masses and treatment; transport tonne-kilometres; worker exposure; stack emissions; and casting rejects. Calculate net CO₂e only after obtaining measured site energy and transport plus documented emission factors for the grid, incumbent reclaimer, virgin sand and residue treatment. Net CO₂e = project energy + project transport + project treatment − displaced incumbent energy/transport/treatment − verified displaced virgin-sand supply, using identical production and quality boundaries. A positive result means this design has *higher* emissions and should not claim a climate benefit. Avoid counting the same displaced mass as both avoided disposal and virgin extraction without separate evidence for each flow.

## Uncertainties and validation

Eligibility share and utilization are independent: 2,000 operating hours cannot be assumed at a foundry with insufficient segregated feed. Moisture, binder composition and bypass fraction change energy; local casting tolerances change accepted yield; residual classifications and air permits vary by site. The published equipment-specific mechanical (~11 kWh/t) and thermal (~242 kWh/t) examples in [2] are context, **not** directly comparable lifecycle baselines. A 500-hour pilot must report distributions by lot rather than only pooled averages and independently confirm product, exposure and emissions limits. No emissions reduction, disposal avoidance, or net cost saving is quantified without that evidence.
