# Biogas Breakthrough Guard — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Research concept; all milestones are proposed, not achieved.

## Concept

Specify the qualified gas-flow envelope, site-independent pressure protection, cartridge chemistry, sensor architecture and outlet quality requirement with a licensed gas-systems engineer. Exit when a hazard analysis, bill of materials and comparison protocol against a single-bed iron-sponge control are reviewed; no field operation is authorized.

## Prototype

Build an enclosed bench rig with qualified media and representative wet biogas or safely managed surrogate challenges. Log inlet, interstage and outlet H₂S, pressure drop, sample transport delay, blocked drains, power loss, valve proof-of-closure and calibration drift at 0.5–1.0 m³/h. Exit only after repeatable mass balance and measured false-trip and missed-breakthrough rates against predeclared acceptance limits, plus independently reviewed gas-safety design.

## Pilot

At up to ten professionally operated sites with existing compliant digester relief, diversion and leak-protection systems, obtain local approvals and train service crews before installation. Record every trip, breakthrough, near miss, cartridge change, downtime and spent-media route for at least a full seasonal cycle. Exit only if outlet limits and independently defined safety/maintenance thresholds hold under variable loads; otherwise redesign or stop.

## Scale

Qualify corrosion-compatible supply chains, inspection and leak-test fixtures, cartridge traceability and return logistics; publish independent lifecycle and complete-site cost comparisons against existing treatment. Exit on audited reliability, safe media handling, local conformity assessment and a service model that does not depend on untrained bypasses.

## Ubiquity

Make interoperable replacement cartridges and technician training available where a site has verified safety infrastructure, with periodic third-party sampling and transparent failure reporting. Exit is sustained measured outlet quality and economically supportable maintenance across different climates—not a target device count or a claim that all digesters should use this design.
