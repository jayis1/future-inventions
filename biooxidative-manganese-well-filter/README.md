# Biooxidative Manganese Well Filter

**Author: jayis1**

**Category:** Drinking Water & Universal Basic Services / Manganese-Exposed Wells

**Status:** TRL 2 research concept; not a certified drinking-water device

## Problem

Dissolved manganese in reducing groundwater can be invisible at the tap. WHO's **provisional** guideline for *total* manganese is 0.08 mg/L, selected to protect bottle-fed infants; WHO also notes that microbial safety and other chemical hazards must be assessed independently.[1] A household cannot infer manganese safety from clarity, a generic filter label, or a running pump. Biofilters can take weeks or months to mature, while manganese bound to a young bed may initially disappear and then break through.[4][6]

## Solution

A locally serviceable, shared-well **batch treatment and verification station** uses a screened, non-chlorinated well source and four physically separated paths: raw-water inlet → splash aerator → two independently sampleable, removable granular beds → covered 20 L quarantine tank. The upstream bed catches iron and particles; the downstream bed uses manganese-oxide-coated silica sand with a naturally established, site-specific biofilm. Adsorption onto manganese oxides and oxygen-dependent regeneration/oxidation are established mechanisms, but the fraction attributable to microbes varies by site; one mature-filter study found predominantly abiotic removal.[4][5] A downstream fine-particle barrier retains oxide fines. No genetically engineered organism or routine permanganate dosing is proposed.

A mechanical **normally closed draw valve** opens for an individual batch only when a trained attendant records a valid *total-manganese* outlet test from that batch and a matching, current laboratory cross-check schedule. A keyed sample port and batch-ID tag prevent a previous result from releasing the next batch. A water-quality failure, overdue or invalid test, unsampled bypass, low flow from clogging, or unqualified source closes the outlet and shows **HOLD—USE AN INDEPENDENTLY SAFE SOURCE**. The gate is a procedure and hardware hypothesis, not a substitute for a water utility's certified treatment system.

**Proposed trial envelope, not demonstrated performance:** influent total Mn 0.08–0.5 mg/L, total Fe ≤0.3 mg/L, ammonium ≤0.2 mg/L, pH 6.5–8.0, water 10–30 °C, and filtered-water dissolved oxygen ≥4 mg/L at commissioning. At a capped 0.5 L/min through a ≥7.5 L working bed, nominal empty-bed contact time is ≥15 minutes; the actual hydraulic distribution and biofilm start-up must be measured. Trial outlet target: **total Mn <0.05 mg/L** (below WHO's provisional 0.08 mg/L), verified by a validated method with reporting limit ≤0.02 mg/L. The numbers are deliberately narrow engineering gates, not a universal operating recipe; source assays must also check microbial quality and locally relevant arsenic, fluoride, nitrate, lead and other hazards.[1][4]

## Key Innovation

Aeration, oxide-coated media, biological manganese filtration and outlet testing already exist.[1][4] The proposed distinction is **batch-custodied treatment with a mechanical release gate tied to measured *total* manganese**, not merely bed age, a color-change indicator, or dissolved-Mn removal. Separate iron and manganese beds expose each stage to sampling and maintenance, while the batch identifier invalidates an old pass after startup, a backwash, a power interruption, or media exchange. This addresses a specific reliability gap in small intermittently operated wells; whether it improves safety or cost over maintained reverse osmosis, established biofilters or a safer alternative well remains unproven. Unlike the repository's arsenic and fluoride kiosks, its contaminant is manganese and its treatment is oxygen/oxide-mediated filtration; unlike the incubation-witness kit, its gate concerns chemical breakthrough, not microbial-test validity. No worldwide novelty or patent claim is asserted.

## Target Cost

**Unquoted 2038 US-dollar ex-workshop goal at 10,000 units/year: US$225/unit:** US$45 food-contact-qualified tank, lid and sample port; US$35 splash aerator and screened housing; US$30 two removable bed housings; US$25 graded sand/oxide-coated media and retained polishing layer; US$35 keyed batch valve, witness and labels; US$20 tubing, seals and spare screen; US$35 assembly and wet-path inspection. This is a design budget, not a vendor quote. Excludes installation, certified analytical meter and reagents, source and confirmatory laboratory assays, operator labor, seasonal maintenance, power if pumped, safe backup water, taxes, shipping, certification and regulated waste disposal. Media life and all-in cost per compliant litre require field measurement. Prefer locally replaceable polypropylene/HDPE and standard fasteners; characterize spent media/backwash for co-adsorbed contaminants before managed disposal, never scatter it onto soil or into a well.

## Impact

Five *passing* 20 L batches per day would offer at most **100 L/day** or **36,500 L/year at perfect availability** per unit; neither throughput nor the number of wells is a health-outcome claim. A first paired pilot should compare 50 eligible wells over a full seasonal cycle with maintained conventional treatment or an independently safe source. Measure inlet and each bed's dissolved/total Mn, outlet total Mn by independent laboratory method, dissolved oxygen, pH, iron, ammonium, turbidity, microbial indicators, false releases, false holds, service labor, waste mass and delivered cost. Simulate oxide exhaustion, start-up transients, cold water, high iron, organic loading, power loss and unauthorized draw. Require zero observed out-of-spec *released* batches in the pilot as a provisional gate, while acknowledging that this does not prove zero risk. Biofilm may take months to mature even with inoculated media; during maturation the system stays on HOLD unless each batch independently meets the release criteria.[4][6]

No treated water is called potable without a local water-safety plan, independent pathogen/chemical testing, approval and protected storage. Backwashing sends washwater to a contained waste tank, never the clean-water tank. High iron/organic loading, low oxygen, clogged media or failed regeneration trigger a service visit and alternative supply; cold-weather sites must be independently qualified. The engineering goal is fewer silent manganese breakthroughs, with actual exposure reduction contingent on verified performance, equitable staffing and affordable safe backup water.
