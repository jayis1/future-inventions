# Biooxidative Manganese Well Filter — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Research concept; not certified for drinking water. Every phase requires an independently safe backup supply.

## 1. Concept

Characterize candidate wells across seasons for total/dissolved Mn, Fe, ammonium, pH, oxygen, temperature, microbes and other local contaminants. Specify the two removable beds, 20 L quarantine tank, batch-ID valve and a validated total-Mn assay with ≤0.02 mg/L reporting limit. Gate: independent review of the water-safety plan, materials and a laboratory-comparator test protocol.

## 2. Prototype

Build a food-contact-suitable HDPE/polypropylene wet path and oxide-coated silica-sand bed. Bench-test at ≤0.5 L/min with ≥7.5 L working Mn-bed volume, including tracer residence, adsorption exhaustion, maturation, backwash-to-waste, valve tampering, missed assays, stagnation and restart. Gate: reproducible batch-specific HOLD on every missing or failed test; no claim of safe drinking water.

## 3. Pilot

Run 50 eligible wells over a full seasonal cycle against matched, maintained conventional treatment or independently safe alternative supplies. Independently assay inlet, inter-bed and outlet water, false releases/holds, pathogens, service hours, costs and contained waste. Gate: zero observed out-of-spec released batches, sustained total Mn <0.05 mg/L on passing batches, no uncontained waste and local regulator/operator approval for the next trial; this sample cannot prove zero risk.

## 4. Scale

If pilots pass, qualify media suppliers, batch-ID hardware, local assay laboratories and operator training; verify cost per compliant delivered litre rather than the US$225 ex-workshop target alone. Introduce monitored multi-site procurement only where backup water and residuals management are funded. Gate: independently audited reliability and affordability across diverse chemistries and operators.

## 5. Ubiquity

By 2050, conditional on demonstrated comparative benefit, integrate batch records and service intervals into local water utilities' water-safety plans. Repair locally, publish failed-batch rates and independently measured delivered volumes, and retire installations that cannot maintain testing or safe fallback. Ubiquity is a scenario, not an adoption forecast or a replacement for pathogen control.
