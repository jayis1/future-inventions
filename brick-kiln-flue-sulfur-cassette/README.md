# Brick-Kiln Flue Sulfur Cassette

**Author: jayis1**

**Status: Research concept (TRL 2)**

**Category: Clean Air & Sustainable Manufacturing / Brick Kilns**

## Problem

Coal-fired brick kilns can emit sulfur dioxide (SO₂) from sulfur in fuel and clay. A World Bank review of Bangladesh's brick sector identifies SO₂ alongside particulates and CO and notes that some zigzag kilns already use water-filled ducts for particulate scrubbing; dirty scrubbing water limits their effectiveness.[1] SO₂ exposure and secondary sulfate pollution matter to workers and surrounding communities, but the sulfur burden differs by fuel and site. This proposal is **only for kilns whose measured stack SO₂ justifies treatment**; cleaner fuels, kiln replacement and existing operating improvements take priority. It is not a claim that every kiln needs a scrubber.

## Solution

A **removable wet limestone contact cassette** for an eligible induced-draft kiln's cool, accessible exhaust section, *downstream of an existing particulate collector or serviced dust drop-out*. A qualified engineer surveys gas temperature, flow, sulfur load, existing water scrubber, fan curve, available draft, discharge permit and maintenance capacity before specifying the retrofit. A corrosion-resistant fiberglass-reinforced-plastic or polypropylene vessel carries a serviceable spray header, open contact zone and washable mist eliminator. A sealed HDPE sump recirculates a metered calcium-carbonate slurry; a removable settling/dewatering bag captures spent solids. Differential-pressure taps across the cassette, sump pH and flow checks, and paired **calibrated inlet/outlet SO₂ measurements** distinguish actual removal from a merely healthy-looking pH indicator. Limestone absorption of SO₂ and oxidation to sulfate are established flue-gas-desulfurization chemistry, not new chemistry.[2]

The cassette is fitted only where the existing fan and emissions permit can accommodate its resistance. A **full-size engineered clean-side bypass** and visible bypass-position flag protect exhaust flow on pump failure, high pressure drop or power loss; bypassed gas is untreated and its duration is counted as emissions, never called a safe emissions mode. Loss of draft, positive pressure at worker openings, or CO alarms require stopping firing under the site's approved emergency procedure, not continued operation on bypass. No camera, worker tracking or remote control is included.

## Key Innovation

Wet limestone scrubbing and water-duct brick-kiln scrubbers are existing art.[1][2] The testable integration is a **cold-service, exchangeable contact-and-solids module with a mechanically verifiable exhaust-relief path**, designed to expose the trade-off between SO₂ removal, fan headroom, water quality and maintenance rather than hide it behind a single removal number. Relative to [Kiln Draft Balancing Retrofit](../kiln-draft-balancing-retrofit/), which adjusts hot-gas flow to test fuel and particulate outcomes, this is an external *sulfur capture and residue-containment* device; it does not rebalance the firing path. It also differs from the [Ventilation-Safe Nitrogen Scrubber](../ventilation-safe-nitrogen-scrubber/), which uses acid to capture ammonia in barn air: this uses alkaline limestone against SO₂ in a hot, dusty combustion exhaust. Whether a small removable cassette can beat an ordinary maintained scrubber on lifecycle cost is unproven.

**Provisional engineering envelope, not field performance:** qualify one kiln at 1,000–3,000 actual m³/h after cooling below the certified vessel-temperature limit; require ≥60% *seasonal mass-flow-weighted* SO₂ removal during treated operation, ≥95% accounted operating hours including bypass, and ≤400 Pa additional clean pressure drop at the specified flow. These are design gates, not measured results or universal operating instructions. If the fan cannot maintain approved draft through the dirty cassette, reject the site or engineer a compliant exhaust upgrade; do not compensate by starving combustion. EPA describes limestone slurry contact, demisting, possible fan upgrades, scaling, and contaminated wastewater as material design constraints for wet flue-gas desulfurization.[2]

## Target Cost

**Unquoted 2038 ex-workshop target:** US$8,000 per 2,000-actual-m³/h module at 1,000 units/year: US$1,800 vessel/bypass, US$1,200 spray/demister, US$900 sump/pump, US$1,000 pressure/pH/flow instrumentation, US$1,100 solids handling and secondary containment, and US$2,000 fabrication/leak testing. Allow a separate, unvalidated US$4,000–10,000 per site for engineering survey, fan or duct alterations, installation, stack tests, permits and operator training; sites needing major fan upgrades may be uneconomic. Limestone, makeup water, electricity, waste assays, treatment and disposal, testing and replacement parts are recurring costs to measure, not included in the unit target. No payback is asserted.

## Impact

**First pilot, not an impact forecast:** commission five eligible kilns alongside five comparable sites using the best practicable existing controls, then independently compare inlet/outlet SO₂ mass rates, whole-season emitted SO₂ (including startup and bypass), PM, CO, fan and pump kWh, fuel per saleable brick, water makeup, wastewater contaminants, solids mass and maintenance hours. Target ≥60% treated-stream SO₂ removal *without* increased whole-season CO, PM, worker-area CO, or fuel per brick; report confidence intervals and every outage. The number of people protected and health benefit cannot be inferred from capture efficiency alone; exposure and dispersion must be evaluated separately. A credible 10–20-year path is bench hydrodynamics and residue testing, supervised kiln pilots, then site-qualified production if it outperforms maintaining existing controls.

## Validation, waste and stop conditions

- **Material balance:** for each mole SO₂ removed, allow at least one mole CaCO₃ reacted in the idealized chemistry; measure excess limestone demand and sulfate/sulfite split rather than assuming pure gypsum. Balance inlet/outlet sulfur, spent solids, dissolved sulfur and bypassed gas. No recovered solid is automatically safe as construction material or fertilizer.[2]
- **No uncontrolled effluent:** recirculate slurry in a bunded sump; collect purge water and dust-rich solids in labeled containers. Have an accredited laboratory test applicable metals (including arsenic, lead and mercury), pH and dissolved salts. Route to locally permitted treatment/disposal; reuse only after product-specific contaminant testing and approval. If no lawful receiver exists, do not install. Wet-scrubber wastewater can carry toxic metals and other pollutants.[2]
- **Fault-to-maintenance:** spray clogging, scaling, frozen lines, failed pump, high differential pressure, mist carryover, leak or exhausted alkalinity trigger a local alarm and bypass/stop according to the engineered kiln procedure. Survey the existing fan/stack for wet-acid corrosion and condensate; add a permitted corrosion-resistant downstream liner or reject the site rather than exporting damage past the cassette.[2] A pH reading alone is **not** proof of SO₂ control. Never let a bypass silently satisfy an emissions permit; log and notify the operator for any permitted exception.
- **Go/no-go:** independent engineer confirms intact original draft and safe combustion across startup, full fire and dirty-cassette tests. Stop and investigate on positive pressure, CO alarm, unsafe residue leakage or permit exceedance. Reject the design if the measured whole-season benefit is erased by bypass, energy, waste handling or maintenance burden. No unsupervised field deployment or implied regulatory certification.
