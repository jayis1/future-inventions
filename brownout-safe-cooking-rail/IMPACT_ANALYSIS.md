# Brownout-Safe Cooking Rail — Conditional Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1.** All numbers below are illustrative arithmetic, not measured performance, a market forecast, or a claim of avoided illness. The README describes a TRL 2 concept without a demonstrated meal-completion advantage.

## Boundary and baseline

Compare one rail plus three insulated pots with three protected conventional electric pots on the **same qualified 48 V supply**, battery, solar input and cooking schedule. Count outcomes only in supervised kitchens with adequate available energy and verified recipe-specific safe food temperatures. No claim applies to unelectrified homes; no health, carbon or PM₂.₅ benefit can be credited from equipment counts alone.

## Explicit scenario assumptions (one operating year)

- 6 attempted meals per kitchen per operating day × 300 days = **1,800 attempted meals/kitchen-year** (assumed utilization, not guaranteed by three ports).
- **5 percentage points** more fully completed meals than the protected comparator (hypothesis requiring crossover evidence, not the README's ≥90% absolute target). Thus 1,800 × 0.05 = **90 additional completed meals/kitchen-year**; if the measured difference is zero, this impact is zero.
- Half of those 90 additional meals would otherwise have fallen back to a polluting fuel and are not simply delayed electric meals: **45 potentially displaced fallback meals/kitchen-year** (assumption to test by fuel logs).
- Up to **0.5 kWh** input per additional meal is an illustrative energy *allowance* based on one 250 W port for two hours; actual recipes, converter losses and standby use must be metered. This implies an illustrative upper allowance of **45 kWh/kitchen-year** for 90 meals, not a prediction of net grid demand: the comparator may already consume energy on incomplete attempts. Extra supply must be available; scheduling cannot manufacture energy.

| Illustrative deployment | Attempted meals/year | Extra completed meals/year at +5 pp | Potential fallback meals displaced/year at 50% | Additional-meal electricity allowance/year |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 kitchens (pilot) | 18,000 | 900 | 450 | 450 kWh |
| 100 kitchens | 180,000 | 9,000 | 4,500 | 4,500 kWh |
| 1,000 kitchens | 1,800,000 | 90,000 | 45,000 | 45,000 kWh |

The final column is **extra meals × 0.5 kWh**, not measured incremental energy and not added to any avoided-fuel energy figure. Using the README's unquoted equipment-only goal of US$495/rail plus three pots gives hypothetical equipment totals of US$4,950, US$49,500 and US$495,000 respectively, *excluding* supply, battery, installation, approvals, maintenance, finance and replacement parts; it is not an installed-cost forecast.

## Sensitivity, evidence and stop conditions

At zero measured completion advantage, incremental completed and displaced meals are zero at every scale. At the same +5 pp completion advantage but zero real fallback substitution, potential polluting-fuel displacement is also zero. Halving operating days or meals per day halves each meal-based estimate; doubling them doubles it **only** if supply, throughput and safe-food checks still qualify. A pilot should record actual cooking time and input kWh, incomplete and discarded meals, fuel purchases/use, emissions and battery cycling on both arms. Convert displaced fuel to emissions or exposure only with locally measured fuel quantities, emission factors and exposure data; no lives saved, CO₂ savings or economic payback is presently calculable. Stop scale-up on unsafe restart, persistent connector heating, greater nuisance-trip burden, food-safety failures or unaffordable installed service.
