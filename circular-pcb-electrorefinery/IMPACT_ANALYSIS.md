# Impact Analysis — Circular PCB Electrorefinery

## Scope and Method

This prospective analysis describes a mature **100 kg PCB/day** module operating **300 days/year**, or **30 tonnes/year** of accepted PCB feed. Results are scenario estimates, not measured performance. Every deployment must replace assumptions with feed assays, local electricity factors, transport distances, component test yields, and independently reviewed lifecycle data.

The functional unit is **one tonne of accepted PCB feed processed**. The boundary includes on-site electricity, reagent makeup, water, replacement filters and electrodes, direct emissions, and outbound products/residue. Avoided primary production and transport are reported separately to prevent double counting.

## Per-Module Annual Material Case

Illustrative mixed-board feed composition:

| Flow | Assumption | Annual quantity at 30 t feed |
|---|---:|---:|
| Copper in feed | 20% | **6.0 t** |
| Tin in feed | 3% | **0.90 t** |
| Gold in feed | 250 g/t | **7.5 kg** |
| Silver in feed | 1.0 kg/t | **30 kg** |
| Palladium in feed | 80 g/t | **2.4 kg** |
| Glass/resin/other metals | balance | **23.1 t** |

Applying design targets yields approximately **5.82 t Cu**, **0.855 t Sn**, **7.1 kg Au**, **28.5 kg Ag**, and **2.2 kg Pd** recovered each year. These values are sensitive to feed grade; XRF plus laboratory assays, not this reference mix, determine actual inventory and payment.

If 30% of input boards are populated, accessible packages average 250 parts/kg populated board, 80% are removed before fragmentation, and 60% of removed parts pass electrical screening, the theoretical output is **1.08 million screened parts/year**. This is an upper planning case, not guaranteed saleable reuse: provenance, obsolescence, destructive authentication, and market demand can reduce the usable total sharply.

## Resource Demand

| Input or output | Design range per tonne PCB | Annual range per module |
|---|---:|---:|
| Electricity | **1.5–3.5 MWh** | **45–105 MWh** |
| Fresh-water makeup | **<0.5 m³** | **<15 m³** |
| Net reagent makeup | **20–80 kg** | **0.6–2.4 t** |
| Non-recoverable hazardous residue | **<30 kg** | **<0.9 t** |
| Routine liquid effluent | **0** | **0** |

Zero routine liquid discharge does not mean zero waste. Spent electrolyte, membranes, filters, scrubber media, failed organic products, and residues remain regulated until regenerated, refined, or destroyed by licensed facilities.

## Climate and Energy

At **1.5–3.5 kWh/kg**, a grid with lifecycle intensity of **50–600 g CO2e/kWh** contributes roughly **0.08–2.1 t CO2e per tonne PCB** before reagents, equipment, and logistics. The broad range makes clean electricity and heat recovery material design requirements.

The project target is **60–85% lower cradle-to-gate greenhouse emissions** than supplying an equivalent basket of recovered metals from primary ores. This target can only be credited after substitution factors are constrained: secondary output may displace less than one kilogram of primary material per kilogram recovered, and component reuse receives avoided-production credit only when a tested part demonstrably replaces a new one.

For a mature module, a conservative validation protocol should report three totals independently:

1. gross refinery emissions;
2. avoided primary-material emissions under low, central, and high substitution cases; and
3. avoided product manufacture from verified component reuse.

## Health and Environmental Outcomes

The primary benefit is controlled handling rather than a speculative monetary value. For each tonne diverted from unsafe processing, the system aims to eliminate open combustion of PCB resin, uncontrolled acidic wastewater, and uncontained metal-rich dust. Performance evidence must include personal and fence-line monitoring for lead, cadmium, nickel, acid mist, particulate matter, brominated compounds, noise, and thermal stress.

Release gates are:

- **>99.9%** capture or destruction of brominated organics entering thermal finishing;
- **>99%** corrected batch mass closure;
- glass filler below applicable leachable-metal and bromine limits;
- headspace below **10% of hydrogen's lower explosive limit**;
- no product designation for liquids or solids that fail independent specifications.

Informal workers should not lose livelihoods through equipment substitution. Deployment contracts should reserve paid sorting, repair, operation, maintenance, and governance roles for existing worker organizations, provide PPE and certified training, and guarantee income floors during commissioning.

## Economics and Distribution

At 30 tonnes/year and **$0.80–1.80/kg**, direct operation costs are approximately **$24,000–54,000/year** before financing, taxes, feed purchase, regional-hub charges, and recovered-material revenue. A nominal **$1–5/kg** margin would imply **$30,000–150,000/year**, but it must not be generalized: precious-metal grade, component resale, commodity prices, labor, compliance, and residue fees dominate.

Each operator should publish a feed-grade sensitivity table and maintain three revenue categories:

- service or producer-responsibility fees for safe treatment;
- assayed commodity revenue;
- verified component-reuse revenue.

Separating these categories prevents high-grade telecom boards from concealing the true cost of treating low-grade consumer boards. It also allows producer fees to fund socially necessary treatment that commodity value cannot support.

## Scale Scenarios

| Scenario | 100 kg/day equivalents | Annual PCB capacity | Annual electricity | Maximum hazardous residue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Demonstration network | 10 | **300 t** | **0.45–1.05 GWh** | **<9 t** |
| Regional rollout | 1,000 | **30,000 t** | **45–105 GWh** | **<900 t** |
| Broad global access | 10,000 | **300,000 t** | **0.45–1.05 TWh** | **<9,000 t** |

These are capacity scenarios, not forecasts. Deployment must follow audited collection and may use combinations of 20 and 100 kg/day units. Building excess capacity could encourage premature scrapping, weaken repair markets, and strand hazardous inventory.

## Rebound Risks and Safeguards

- **Premature disposal:** require repair-first triage and prohibit incentives tied only to processed mass.
- **Waste trafficking:** geofence approved feed jurisdictions contractually, record custody transfers, and audit sealed residue destinations.
- **False circularity claims:** publish mass balances and count outputs as recycled only after they meet a buyer's specification.
- **Pollution relocation:** include regional regeneration, refining, and final destruction in certification boundaries.
- **Commodity volatility:** rely on transparent treatment fees and producer responsibility, not precious-metal speculation.
- **Digital privacy:** accept boards only after authorized data handling; do not extract or retain device content.

## 2050 Outcome Metrics

A successful program is measured by outcomes rather than module sales:

- share of collected boards routed to repair and component harvesting before materials recovery;
- tonnes processed without open burning or uncontrolled liquid discharge;
- independently verified recovery and mass-balance rates;
- primary metal displaced under conservative substitution accounting;
- worker exposure and incident rates relative to incumbent pathways;
- percentage of residues with verified final destinations;
- stable, formally compensated jobs created for current repair and waste-worker communities.

The invention merits scale only if pilots show lower lifecycle harm than both local informal processing and shipment to a well-run centralized refinery. Where centralized treatment is safer or more efficient, CPE should act as a component-harvesting and concentration front end—or not be deployed.
