# Coastal Lens Recharge Gate — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1** · Scenario arithmetic, not field measurements or a claim of potable yield.

## Boundary and baseline

One eligible 100 m² roof feeds a 5 m³ covered tank and shallow gallery in an approved unconfined sandy coastal aquifer. The comparison is a hydrogeologically matched, permitted conventional roof/pond MAR site, **not** no recharge. Existing community MAR is described by [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/documents/managed-aquifer-recharge-mar-protecting-communities-saline-intrusion-groundwater-costal); neither its benefit nor a universal yield transfers to this design. All figures below are illustrative annual *per-site* scenarios, conditional on water quality, land access, flood clearance, soil permeability and protected downstream flow.

A 100 mm event on 100 m² supplies 10 m³ rain; assumed roof runoff coefficient 0.8 gives 8 m³ before first flush and overflow, while the empty tank holds at most 5 m³. A full fill need not occur at every storm. No event is admitted without the approved laboratory/source check; no pump is designed into the gravity path.

## Water-balance scenarios

The assumed annual full-equivalent eligible fills (8/15/20), infiltration fractions (0.50/0.70/0.80) and potentially recoverable fractions of infiltrated water (0.25/0.40/0.50) are **scenario inputs**, not measured coefficients. The last fraction includes conceptual allowance for mixing and lateral leakage; it does not establish sustainable abstraction. No rainfall climatology is assumed; site records must show enough admissible events and source-water volume for the selected case.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 8 | 40 | 20 | 5 |
| Base | 15 | 75 | 52.5 | 21 |
| High (planning ceiling) | 20 | 100 | 80 | 40 |

Formula: `tank-to-gallery = fills × 5 m³`; `infiltrated = tank-to-gallery × infiltration fraction`; `potentially recoverable = infiltrated × recoverable fraction`. Measure overflow, tank drawdown and aquifer response separately. The remaining water is not assumed lost entirely: it may drain, mix or remain stored outside the monitored/recoverable zone. No row is drinking water; source tests and independent well certification remain mandatory.

A 20-site pilot at the base-case assumptions would admit **1,500 m³/year**, with modeled **1,050 m³/year infiltrated** and **420 m³/year potentially recoverable**; the matched 20 control sites also recharge water and must be measured independently. If 1,000 sites independently qualified at the high bound, **100,000 m³/year** could leave their tanks, but this is a *gross planning ceiling*, not a forecast of storage, freshwater-front movement or beneficiaries. Never add the pilot and scale cases together as if they occurred in separate populations.

## Outcomes and economic sensitivity

The pre-registered research outcome is **≥10% lower dry-season inland observation-point chloride concentration versus matched controls** after two wet seasons, adjusted for tide, rainfall and pumping, with no increased microbial/chemical exceedance, neighbor-well harm or protected downstream-flow loss. This is a **target**, not evidence of attainment or proof of potable supply. Report confidence intervals and absolute chloride values; if seasonal variability dominates the estimated difference, call the outcome inconclusive. No avoided desalination energy, emissions or drinking-water beneficiaries are booked before a measured *additional* safe yield and a documented displaced alternative exist.

The README's unquoted hardware/local-assembly goal is **US$2,500/site** at a hypothetical 1,000/year production rate; paid service is **US$250–600/site-year plus lab fees**. For an illustrative ten-year lifetime with zero discount and no replacement, this yields `(2,500 / 10) + (250 to 600) = US$500–850/site-year`, **excluding** surveys, permits, land, substantial civil works, accredited tests and alternate safe supply. Even dividing by the base-case *hypothetical* 21 m³/year gives roughly **US$24–40/m³** before those omissions—not a market price or a potable-water cost. If recoverable yield is zero, unit cost per recoverable cubic metre is undefined. Quote and compare complete lifecycle costs before any procurement decision.

## Validation and stop conditions

The dominant sensitivities are eligible fill frequency, source-water rejection, soil infiltration, tidal/pumping dispersion and recoverability. Survey high water table and contamination pathways; meter fill and bypass events; check gallery clogging; compare paired groundwater heads and lab chloride against matched sites; log neighboring abstraction and downstream flow. Suspend recharge on contamination, flood warning, overtopping, harmful mounding or an unmaintainable outlet. Scale only after independently reviewed replication and local permits; otherwise retain conventional MAR, demand management or protected alternative supply.
