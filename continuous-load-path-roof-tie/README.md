# Continuous-Load-Path Roof Tie

**Author: jayis1**

**Category:** Disaster Resilience & Accessible Housing / Wind Retrofit

**Stage:** TRL 2 — unvalidated concept

## Problem

High winds can separate a roof from its walls or pull the walls off their foundation. A stronger roof-to-wall clip alone can simply move the failure into a weak top plate, stud, sill or anchor: FEMA describes the required path from roof sheathing through framing and walls into the foundation, and requires connectors and fasteners matched to the structure.[1] Existing retrofits can require opening finishes, which increases cost and makes hidden missing connections hard to audit. This is a problem for exposed housing and community buildings, not a promise that every vulnerable building can be retrofitted.

## Solution

The **Continuous-Load-Path Roof Tie** is a proposed *engineer-designed family of inspectable retrofit connection assemblies* for eligible one-story timber-framed dwellings with an engineer-verified sound foundation. A galvanized-steel roof-truss/rafter strap couples to a reinforced stud path; a separate rated sill hold-down connects that path to an assessed concrete foundation. Captive, numbered steel inspection covers expose each attachment without removing finished wall cladding. A non-load-bearing, replaceable displacement witness spanning each critical joint records movement beyond a bench-calibrated gap; a red witness calls for inspection, but a green witness never certifies strength. The designer selects rated connectors, fastener schedules, edge distances, corrosion class and foundation anchor system for *each* building; this concept supplies no universal installation pattern.

Before fitting, a qualified structural engineer surveys roof sheathing attachment, framing, load-bearing studs, wall bracing, openings, sill, concrete condition and existing anchors. Failed sheathing, deteriorated timber, unreinforced masonry, unknown foundations and unsafe access must be repaired or rejected, not hidden by stronger straps. Shear and overturning paths must also be checked. Preserve weather and fire barriers around any opened finish; keep connections inspectable and maintainable. No occupied-building installation is authorized on the strength of this concept note.

## Key Innovation

Hurricane clips, hold-downs and continuous load paths are **existing practice**, not inventions claimed here.[1][2] The research delta is a modular, *end-to-end, joint-by-joint auditable retrofit*: compatible roof, stud and foundation interfaces plus a replaceable displacement witness and accessible fastener inspection, all acceptance-tested on the *assembled structure* rather than assigning the roof clip's catalog capacity to the whole building. Unlike [Passive Storm-Stow Solar Mount](../passive-storm-stow-solar-mount/), this does not move a PV panel or respond to a gust; unlike [Seismic Metamaterial Isolation Foundation](../seismic-metamaterial-isolation-foundation/), it does not attenuate seismic waves. Whether this integrated approach improves actual installation completeness or lifecycle economics over conventional engineered retrofits is unproven.

**Unvalidated design gates:** in a specified sound one-story timber-wall/concrete-foundation test assembly, target a minimum **3 kN characteristic uplift resistance per connection path** under both monotonic and repeated combined uplift/lateral loading, *without* timber splitting, anchor pullout, brittle cover detachment or loss of continuity. This is a laboratory research target, **not an allowable load or wind rating**. Design demand and safety factors are site-specific. Target witness activation for **2–4 mm relative joint displacement** after verified loading, with <1 mm drift during 1,000 benign thermal/humidity cycles; test false green/red outcomes independently. A witness sees only the joint it spans and may fail silently. Record full load–displacement curves and all weak-link failures, not only peak strap strength.

A rough force balance illustrates the limit: with an *assumed* 1.5 m² tributary roof area and 2 kPa net uplift pressure, the demand is **3 kN before** local pressure variation, internal pressurization, load combinations or code factors. The target must be revised or rejected against actual site design pressures and every element in the chain; multiplying a clip rating by the number of clips cannot establish whole-building capacity.

## Target Cost

**Unquoted 2038 planning target**, US$85 ex-workshop per complete *one-path* hardware set at 50,000 sets/year: roof/stud galvanized steel connectors US$30, foundation-compatible hold-down and bolts US$25, numbered access cover and displacement witness US$12, fabrication/coating/inspection US$18. Foundation anchor chemistry, substrate repair and reinforcement, roof/sheathing work, installation, engineer assessment, permits, finish restoration and tax are **excluded**; a fully installed building cost is unknown and may greatly exceed the hardware budget. Budget a further **US$10 per path-year** as an unverified inspection/parts allowance, excluding access labor. Specify corrosion-compatible fasteners, replaceable covers and locally recyclable steel; used anchors and contaminated finishes follow local disposal rules. Avoid committing to proprietary sensors, batteries or cloud service.

## Impact

A plausible 10–20-year pathway is connector/substrate characterization, full-wall cyclic and combined-load tests, then a permitted, independently assessed multi-site retrofit comparison with standard code-compliant connections. At 100 eligible buildings, publish screened-out counts, missing-path findings, as-installed capacity, witness false indications, corrosion, cost, roof detachment and water intrusion after actual storms. No reduction in fatalities, roof losses or insurance premiums is established. Affordable, auditable strengthening could benefit exposed communities *if* qualified installers and affordable building-wide upgrades exist; households with unsafe substrates must receive repair or safer-housing alternatives, not a false assurance.

**Safety and failure boundary:** Wind debris, roof covering failure, opening breach, flood and extreme tornado conditions remain hazards. The witness is a maintenance prompt, **not a structural interlock**. On missing fasteners, loosened anchors, corrosion, joint movement, broken cover or damaged wall material, mark the path unverified and refer to a qualified engineer; do not simply tighten or replace the witness. Power loss has no effect on this passive device, but unseen deterioration remains possible. Inspect before storm season and after damaging events; require independent structural testing, local-code approval and licensed design before any deployment. FEMA explicitly cautions that only compliant shelters/safe rooms provide tornado life-safety protection.[1]
