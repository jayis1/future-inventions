# Continuous-Load-Path Roof Tie — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Research concept, not an approved structural product. Phase gates require independent evidence and jurisdiction-specific engineering; dates are not commitments.

## Concept
Map the complete deck-to-foundation uplift path in candidate one-story timber homes and identify incompatible substrates, bracing gaps and inspection access. Exit when a licensed structural reviewer approves a test matrix, a conventional engineered comparator and explicit reject/repair criteria; document the assumed 3 kN laboratory gate without calling it a building rating.

## Prototype
Fabricate galvanized-steel interface and removable witness prototypes; test matched complete wall/roof/foundation assemblies under monotonic and repeated combined uplift/lateral loading. Exit only with published load–displacement and weak-link records, witness 2–4 mm activation and <1 mm drift over 1,000 benign cycles, and a comparison against the conventional engineered assembly. Failure of the 3 kN characteristic-resistance target sends the design back for revision.

## Pilot
Obtain permits and site-specific signed designs for up to 100 screened eligible buildings, with an independently assessed comparison to conventional code-compliant retrofits. Record exclusions, as-built joint photos, missing paths, inspection access, installed costs and false witness indications; stop or repair any unsafe installation. Exit after a pre-defined independent review of safety, usability and any observed storm outcomes—not an extrapolated damage claim.

## Scale
Only after approvals and pilot results, qualify fabrication, coating, compatible fasteners, installers and local repair/inspection partners. Audit production lots and installations; compare full installed lifecycle costs with the comparator. The US$85/path ex-workshop figure at 50,000 sets/year remains a planning target until supplier quotes and field costs validate it.

## Ubiquity
Make inspectable, documented connections a routine option for eligible housing by 2050, contingent on local code acceptance, access and affordable substrate repairs. Maintain joint histories, publish post-event outcomes and revise designs when failures appear; never represent a roof tie as a safe-room certification or a cure for flood, debris or inadequate foundations.
