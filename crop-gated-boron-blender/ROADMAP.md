# Crop-Gated Boron Blender — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Research plan; phase gates are proposed, not achieved.

## Concept

Confirm the local crop, soil and seasonal source-water envelope with an agronomist and a qualified laboratory. Draw a closed hydraulic and custody diagram, identify an authorized regional route for spent resin and regenerant, and cost assays and labor alongside the US$850 hardware aspiration. Exit only when a field-specific limit, legal waste route and credible comparator are documented.

## Prototype

Build a contained 1 m³/day HDPE/polypropylene bench module with NMDG cartridge, split-flow meters, covered tank and keyed normally closed outlet. Measure inlet, column outlet and mixed-batch boron with laboratory duplicates over breakthrough and changing feed chemistry; inject wrong-label, stale-result, power-loss, leak and blocked-flow faults. Exit only if no failed batch escapes the gate in the defined fault campaign and flow accounting meets the proposed 5% tolerance; independent safety review precedes field use.

## Pilot

With local permissions, compare the blender and a maintained full-flow boron-resin control on matched source water across a season. Log lab turnaround, rejected volumes, EC/SAR, soil boron, crop response, energy, cartridge replacements, labor and lawful waste handling. Exit only if every released batch satisfies the agronomist's complete irrigation specification and independent reviewers find no unacceptable soil trend or uncontained release; yield benefit is not assumed.

## Scale

If pilot gates pass, establish regional cartridge exchange and independently audited batch custody, train cooperative operators, and test multi-site reliability under changing crops and water chemistry. Price installed equipment, recurring assays and waste per qualified cubic metre against full-flow treatment and alternative sources. Expand only where safety and audited total cost justify deployment.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, conditional adoption could make the module an optional, locally repairable part of cooperative irrigation systems rather than a universal fix. Publish field-qualified limits and failure data, maintain independent laboratory and soil surveillance, and retire or redesign units when source chemistry, disposal capacity or economics change. Ubiquity means verifiable suitability where appropriate, not automatic release everywhere.
