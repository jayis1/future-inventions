# Dialysis Reject Isolation Dock — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Research concept, not approved for clinical installation. Each phase is gated by evidence, not a calendar promise.

## Concept

Map reject-side outlets, cleaning states, peak flush flows, local nonpotable rules and actual toilet demand at candidate facilities. Draw and independently review the air-gap, full-flow drain, dual-valve isolation and segregated plumbing boundary. Exit when clinical engineering and licensed plumbing reviewers agree on a testable hazard analysis and site-specific sampling plan; a site with no permitted endpoint does not advance.

## Prototype

Build a 316 stainless air-gap funnel, spring-to-drain diverter, separate normally closed tank inlet, HDPE tank and metered test loop using dyed water only. Challenge reverse pressure, power loss, stuck valves, blocked drain, full tank and false cleaning-state signals at the measured site peak flow and 5–35 °C. Exit only when single-fault trials show no pressure/tracer return toward RO, all normal faults divert to drain within the proposed 5 s target, and a blocked drain triggers the manufacturer's approved contingency rather than backpressure; redesign on any failure.

## Pilot

Following licensed-plumber, clinical-engineering, infection-prevention and local-authority approvals, operate drain-only shadow monitoring before releasing any qualified nonpotable batch. A proposed 10-site, one-year supervised comparison meters eligible reject, tank admission, overflow, actual toilet delivery, matched mains displacement, assays, kWh and maintenance. Exit if every site's approved water-quality criteria hold, no cross-connection is observed, ≥50% of eligible reject is captured at qualified sites and independently verified net water benefit survives losses; zero observed incidents alone is not proof of safety.

## Scale

Standardize non-interchangeable fittings, inspection access, calibration records and training while retaining site-specific plumbing and water-quality decisions. Compare each installation with its existing approved reuse arrangement or direct-to-drain baseline, including installed cost, laboratory and labor expense, pump energy and service downtime. Exit when repeat installations pass the same fault and endpoint tests and audited operating savings justify local upkeep without weakening treatment continuity.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, offer the dock as an optional, repairable facilities component where reject quality, code, staffing and nonpotable demand align—not as a universal dialysis appliance. Publish aggregated, de-identified rejection reasons and actual delivered-water performance so sites with efficient RO plants or no approved reuse route can simply stay drain-only. Exit is sustained audited operation across differing sites with no erosion of clinical water segregation; withdraw any configuration that fails its safety gates.
