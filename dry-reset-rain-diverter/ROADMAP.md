# Dry-Reset Rain Diverter — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**

**Status:** Proposed gates, not achieved milestones. No potable-water approval is implied.

## Concept

Freeze a hydraulic state diagram, 25 L reference geometry, waste/overflow routing rules and a comparison protocol against a maintained conventional first-flush diverter. Exit only when an independent design review can trace empty → full → flow-held tank admission → flow-stop waste return and identify every single-fault path to the tank.

## Prototype

Build an accessible transparent bench unit with replaceable screen, restrictor and sealed polypropylene cassette. Run at 0.8–8 m³/h simulated roof flow, interrupted storms, blocked drains, stuck floats and 1,000 wet–dry cycles; proceed only if ≥99% tested return-to-waste transitions, zero tested admissions after a missed empty reset, and an independently sized overflow with no unsafe backpressure are documented. Failures trigger redesign, not a relaxed safety claim.

## Pilot

With local plumbing and drainage approval, compare 100 matched roofs over two rainy seasons against equally maintained conventional diverters. Log event volume, empty-state timing, overflow, maintenance minutes and paired tank-inlet *E. coli*, turbidity and site-specific chemical samples. Exit only if reliability and net utility exceed the baseline without creating unsafe discharge or cross-connections; no collected water is presumed potable.

## Scale

Qualify UV/temperature aging, seal compatibility and repair access, then obtain supplier quotations and local product approvals. A proposed 10,000-unit/year manufacturing gate is ≤US$42 ex-workshop per cassette plus ≤US$8/year service parts, excluding installation, tank, treatment, testing and labor; expand only if audited field failures and full installed cost justify added complexity.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, *if* multiple regions replicate the pilot outcomes, publish open maintenance and incident statistics and standardize replaceable parts for suitable non-potable catchments. Keep the option to retire the design wherever a simpler serviced diverter has equal safety and lower lifecycle burden; any drinking-water use still requires separate treatment, testing and legal approval.
