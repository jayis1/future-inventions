# Electroferrate Arsenic Remediation Network — Impact Analysis

Quantitative projections for deployment in arsenic-affected groundwater regions.

---

## 1. Deployment Scale Scenarios

| Scenario | Units | Water delivered (m³/day) | People served* | CapEx ($M) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pilot | 100 community nodes | 100 | 25,000 | 0.12–0.18 |
| District scale | 10,000 mixed nodes | 20,000 | 5,000,000 | 30–90 |
| National program | 250,000 mixed nodes | 500,000 | 125,000,000 | 750–2,250 |
| Multi-country scale | 500,000 mixed nodes | 1,000,000 | 250,000,000 | 1,500–4,500 |

*Assumes ~4 L/person/day of drinking and cooking water equivalent served through a mix of household and shared-well systems.*

---

## 2. Water Quality Impact

| Metric | Baseline exposure range | EARN target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Influent arsenic | 50–1000 µg/L | — |
| Effluent arsenic | — | <5 µg/L |
| Arsenic removal | — | 99–99.7% |
| As(III) oxidation | Often incomplete in low-cost systems | >95% in <2 min |
| Service uptime | 50–80% common in poorly maintained rural systems | >95% target for community nodes |

At 500,000 community-equivalent nodes treating 1,000,000 m³/day, EARN would prevent roughly **50–1,000 kg of dissolved arsenic per day** from reaching drinking-water users, depending on source concentration.

---

## 3. Public Health Impact

| Scale | People moved to WHO-safe water | Expected long-term effect |
|---|---:|---|
| 25,000 served | Demonstrates compliance in high-risk pilot zones | Early biomarker studies, behavior change |
| 5M served | Population-scale reduction in chronic exposure | Lower skin lesions, improved pregnancy outcomes |
| 125M served | Majority coverage in a heavily affected nation | Large future decline in cancer/CVD burden |
| 250M served | Multi-country transformation | Arsenic poisoning no longer a mass rural norm |

Because arsenic diseases have long latency, the fastest measurable gains would likely be in **urinary arsenic reduction, child-development protection, pregnancy outcomes, and skin lesion incidence**, followed by later declines in cancers and cardiovascular disease.

---

## 4. Cost Benchmarks

| Metric | EARN target | Common alternative range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Household unit CapEx | $120–220 | $50–300 |
| Community unit CapEx | $600–1,800 | $1,000–5,000 |
| Water treatment cost | $0.02–0.10/m³ | $0.05–0.50/m³ |
| Energy use | 0.05–0.20 kWh/m³ | 0–0.6 kWh/m³ |
| Sorbent/media replacement burden | Low | Medium to high |

The economic wedge is not just cheaper treatment; it is **lower failure-driven replacement and safer waste handling**.

---

## 5. Waste and Environmental Impact

| Metric | Target |
|---|---:|
| Stabilized waste generated | <5–20 g/m³ treated |
| Regeneration cycles before scaffold replacement | 100+ |
| Solar share of operation | 80–100% in off-grid modes |
| Loose arsenic sludge generation | Near-zero by design |

At **1,000,000 m³/day treated**, stabilized residue would total roughly **5–20 tonnes/day**, but in a dense, auditable ceramic form rather than dispersed wet sludge or spent household cartridges. That radically improves the odds of safe long-term custody.

## 7. Sensitivity Analysis

| Variable | Conservative | Base case | Optimistic | Main effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Raw-water phosphate | High | Moderate | Low | Higher phosphate increases iron demand and lowers bed life |
| Ferrate generation efficiency | 40% | 60% | 75% | Better efficiency lowers energy and electrode wear |
| Regeneration capacity recovery | 60% | 80% | 90% | Higher recovery reduces OPEX sharply |
| Local manufacturing share | 25% | 50% | 75% | More local sourcing reduces CapEx and repair times |
| Verification frequency | Monthly | Weekly | Continuous proxy + weekly assay | Higher verification improves trust but raises service cost |

Even in conservative conditions, the concept remains compelling where the alternative is lifelong toxic exposure.

---

## 8. 2050 Systems-Level Outcome

If EARN-class systems reached **250M people by 2050**, the world would gain:

- a credible path to collapse one of the largest chronic groundwater poisoning crises on Earth,
- a standardized hazardous-waste pathway for arsenic that is far safer than dispersed spent media,
- a solar-native rural water-treatment model compatible with schools, clinics, and private wells,
- and a new norm: groundwater safety measured not by whether a well exists, but by whether the delivered water is actually verified safe.
