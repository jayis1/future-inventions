# ROADMAP — Electrogenic Denitrification Drainage Array (EDDA)

**Invention #:** 042
**Created:** 2026-08-17

---

## Phase 1: Concept (2026–2027)

Literature synthesis, thermodynamic modeling, and component-level feasibility analysis. Validate the N₂O suppression mechanism by reviewing bioelectrochemical denitrification literature (Pous et al. 2014; Vilar-Santiago et al. 2023) and confirm that the −0.3 to −0.6 V vs. SHE cathodic potential window consistently yields <1% N₂O. Model the PCM thermal buffer against US Midwest and Nordic winter soil temperature profiles to confirm ≥12°C interior sustained for ≥90 days. Complete bill-of-materials costing at 10,000-unit and 100,000-unit production scales. Publish the concept as a whitepaper or preprint to invite community critique. **Exit criteria:** peer-reviewed confirmation that all three core innovations (autotrophic denitrification at NosZ potential, BMED sidestream recovery, PCM thermal buffering) are individually demonstrated in published literature and the novel integration is physically and economically plausible.

## Phase 2: Prototype (2027–2029)

Build a bench-scale single-module prototype (0.1 m³ reactor volume) in a laboratory setting using synthetic tile-drain water (8 mg N/L NaNO₃ in buffer). Demonstrate: (a) 85%+ nitrate removal at 18°C, (b) N₂O <0.3% of influent N as measured by GC-ECD or TDLAS, (c) 15–35% ammonium sulfate recovery in the BMED sidestream, (d) stable biofilm on graphite/PEDOT:PSS biocathode for ≥90 days continuous operation. Test PCM thermal buffering in an environmental chamber cycling between −20°C and +30°C. Iterate on biofilm inoculum consortia (*Thiobacillus*, *Pseudomonas*, *Paracoccus* spp.) and Cu²⁺ dosing protocol. Optimize Pd catalyst loading on Ti cathode for NH₄⁺ selectivity. **Exit criteria:** prototype meets all four performance targets in lab conditions for ≥90 days continuous; TDLAS N₂O data validates the suppression mechanism; BMED membrane fouling tested with real tile-drain water samples.

## Phase 3: Pilot (2029–2032)

Deploy 10–20 field modules on 2–3 working farms in Iowa and Denmark — representing US Midwest and Nordic climate regimes. Install along active tile-drain outlets under USDA-NRCS Code 604 and EU CAP frameworks. Run for 2 full annual cycles including winter freeze–thaw. Validate: PCM thermal endurance under real soil conditions, biofilm stability across seasons, BMED membrane fouling rates with real drainage water (sediment, dissolved organics, Fe/Mn), LoRaWAN telemetry reliability, and farmer usability of the dashboard. Collect 16S rRNA microbial community data at quarterly intervals to characterize biofilm evolution. Begin Pd-free catalyst (Ni-Mo, Co-P) parallel testing for cost reduction. **Exit criteria:** field modules achieve ≥70% winter removal and ≥85% growing-season removal over 2 annual cycles; N₂O <0.5% verified by TDLAS in field; farmer dashboard rated "usable without training" by ≥80% of pilot farmers; cost-verified at ≤$4,200/module at 1,000-unit pilot production.

## Phase 4: Scale (2032–2038)

Ramp production to 10,000–100,000 modules/year via contract manufacturing. Expand deployment to 500,000+ ha across the US Midwest (Iowa, Illinois, Minnesota, Indiana, Ohio), Baltic catchment (Denmark, Sweden, Poland), and Indo-Gangetic Plain (India, Pakistan). Establish watershed cooperatives for bulk (NH₄)₂SO₄ recovery and resale. Integrate with USDA EQIP cost-share and EU CAP eco-scheme payments. Develop verified N-removal and N₂O-abatement credit protocols using sensor telemetry data, enabling water-quality trading markets. Validate the low-cost variant ($900–2,000) for smallholder deployment in South/SE Asia via agricultural extension partnerships. **Exit criteria:** 500,000+ ha deployed and verified; Gulf of Mexico N load reduction measurable in USGS monitoring data; N₂O abatement verified at watershed scale; net operating profit demonstrated for farmers via recovered fertilizer + credit revenue.

## Phase 5: Ubiquity (2038–2050)

Achieve global deployment across 120M ha of treatable drained cropland. EDDA becomes a standard edge-of-field practice equivalent to terracing or buffer strips — installed by default on new tile-drain systems and retrofitted on existing outlets. Coastal dead zones shrink by 50–80% as agricultural N flux drops 60–90%. Agricultural N₂O emissions fall 50–70% globally. Fresh Haber–Bosch ammonia demand reduced 5–15% via recovered (NH₄)₂SO₄ circular flow. Modules are manufactured from recycled graphite and bio-based PCM, with Pd catalysts replaced by earth-abundant alloys. The dashboard evolves into a planetary nutrient-flow monitoring network, providing real-time data on agricultural nitrogen cycling to researchers, policymakers, and farmers. The ocean's 500+ dead zones are in active recovery. **Exit criteria:** 120M ha deployed; global agricultural N₂O emissions down ≥50%; Gulf of Mexico dead zone <5,000 km²; global coastal hypoxia area reduced ≥50%; rural drinking water nitrate below MCL across deployed regions.
