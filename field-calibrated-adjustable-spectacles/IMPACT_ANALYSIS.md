# Impact Analysis — Field-Calibrated Adjustable Spectacles

**Author: jayis1. Status: illustrative scenarios, not measured impact or a sales forecast.** Scope: one adult deployment programme in one year, with a six-month use check; eligible users have predominantly spherical distance blur. The factory price in the README is a target, not a supplier quote. Every rate below is an *assumption* to be tested. Not all glasses users need or benefit from adjustable optics.

## Boundary and baseline

WHO reports at least 2.2 billion people worldwide with near or distance vision impairment, including at least 1 billion whose impairment was preventable or remains unaddressed.[1] That is **not** this device's addressable population: it includes cataract, near-only presbyopia and other conditions. A local survey must establish the adult population with distance refractive error, existing spectacle access, cylinder distribution, affordability and referral capacity before projecting coverage. This model assumes **zero measured incremental clinical benefit** over ordinary glasses until a controlled comparison demonstrates one.

## One-year planning inputs

| Input | Conservative | Base | Expansion | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Pairs distributed | 20,000 | 100,000 | 300,000 | Capacity assumption, not a demand estimate; one pair per recipient |
| Eligible for spherical pathway | 50% | 70% | 80% | Assumed after screening; others referred or given better-suited alternatives |
| Successfully fitted among eligible | 60% | 80% | 90% | Hypothetical functional fitting outcome, not proof of disease exclusion |
| Still using successfully fitted pair at 6 months | 50% | 70% | 80% | Assumed follow-up rate; missing follow-up counts as not confirmed |
| Programme cost per distributed pair | US$40 | US$33.20 | US$30 | Includes factory, worker, delivery, follow-up/referral reserve and shared kit; scenario assumptions |

Base cost build-up per distributed pair: US$21 factory (within README's US$18–25 target) + US$5 worker fitting + US$4 distribution + US$3 repairs/follow-up/referral reserve + US$0.20 shared fitting kit = **US$33.20**. Taxes, professional exams, hard cases needing specialist transport, breakage replacement and additional programme overhead are **not fully priced**; use local quotes before procurement. Conservative and expansion totals assume different scale economies and travel burdens without supplier evidence. This is **not** a comparison with local prescription-glasses delivered costs.

## Calculated outputs

Formula: initially fitted = distributed × eligible share × successful-fit share. Confirmed six-month users = initially fitted × retention share. Programme spend = distributed × programme cost per pair. Spend per confirmed six-month user = spend / confirmed users; this is *not* cost per proven health benefit or per person-year.

| Scenario | Initially fitted | Confirmed six-month users | Programme spend | Spend per confirmed six-month user |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Conservative | 6,000 | 3,000 | US$800,000 | US$266.67 |
| Base | 56,000 | 39,200 | US$3,320,000 | US$84.69 |
| Expansion | 216,000 | 172,800 | US$9,000,000 | US$52.08 |

Sensitivity: at base throughput and US$33.20 cost, the 30% ineligible share alone removes 30,000 recipients from the spherical pathway; if eligibility falls from 70% to 50% with fitting and retention unchanged, the formula yields **28,000** confirmed users and **US$118.57** per confirmed user. If repeat fitting misses an excess-minus or disease referral, a counted "successful fitting" can still be harmful; the pilot must separately measure visual acuity versus professional best correction, false-clear referrals, adverse events and referral completion. No visual-acuity, educational or productivity uplift is assigned a dollar value here: those would require controlled evidence and must not be inferred from units shipped.

## Time horizon and residual uncertainty

These are first-year flows, not unique people corrected over multiple years. For a three-year service-life *target*, replacement demand after three years would depend on actual failure, loss and continued need; do not triple six-month users to claim three years of benefit. A 2050 adoption story cannot be extrapolated from these one-year assumptions. Track device mass, replacement parts and end-of-life collection before claiming waste avoided. Fund professional examination and referrals even if this lowers the modelled cost-efficiency: safety is a gate, not a negotiable multiplier.
