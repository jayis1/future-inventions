# Field-Calibrated Adjustable Spectacles

**Author: jayis1. Status: TRL 2 concept; not a clinically validated prescription or substitute for an eye examination.**

## Problem

WHO estimates that at least **2.2 billion** people have near or distance vision impairment, including at least **1 billion** whose impairment could have been prevented or remains unaddressed.[1] That billion includes cataract and other conditions; it is **not** the market for spectacles.[1] WHO estimates that two in three people in low-income countries who need spectacles lack access.[1][2] A prescription, inventory of many lens powers, fitting, and follow-up are difficult to deliver in remote communities. Poor vision can also indicate disease rather than a correctable refractive error.

## Solution

A reusable pair of independently adjustable spectacles uses two injection-moulded **optical-grade polycarbonate Alvarez elements per eye**. Sliding a complementary pair laterally changes its spherical optical power through their matched cubic surfaces; a captive lead screw sets each eye independently, with a mechanical scale and a lock that still works without electricity. Hard coating and replaceable scratch shields protect the optics. This is established geometric optics, not a novel lens material or a claim that self-refraction alone is adequate.[3][4]

A **field-fitting kit** combines a washable, fixed-distance distance-acuity chart, a near-reading card, a monocular occluder, and a high-contrast astigmatic fan. A trained local worker checks each eye separately, guides the wearer to adjust from a deliberately blurred setting to the clearest legible line, repeats after resting the eye, and records the resulting settings **only on a paper card given to the wearer**. The wearer may readjust as needed; no phone, network, battery, biometric storage, or subscription is required. The kit checks *functional acuity*, not the eye's full prescription. Workers refer children, anyone with eye pain/redness, sudden or asymmetric vision change, persistent blur, failed acuity, or significant astigmatic-fan distortion for professional eye care; they do not claim to diagnose disease. Presbyopia users may need a distinct near setting or conventional bifocals.

## Key Innovation

Adjustable Alvarez and fluid-filled glasses already exist. A 2023 trial in 112 children aged 5–11 found good corrected acuity in many participants but a tendency toward more myopic self-selected power than cycloplegic refraction; higher astigmatism and younger age predicted poorer results.[3] A prior review likewise identifies astigmatism, high myopia, affordability and lens quality as limitations of adjustable glasses.[4] The proposed advance is **a serviceable, reversible power-setting mechanism packaged with a repeatable, worker-assisted two-distance fitting and explicit referral gate**, rather than a claim to replace optometrists. Unlike the repository's tactile learning sheet, which presents tactile educational content for blind learners, this is optical correction for selected refractive errors; it is also distinct from the hearing-focused communication implant.

The hard research question is whether the integrated fitting protocol reduces incorrect power and missed referrals versus both unaided self-adjustment and locally available ready-made glasses. If not, distribute conventional glasses and improve access to trained refraction instead. This is a proposed engineering and service model, not a claim of worldwide novelty or patentability.

## Target Cost

**Unverified 2035 factory target at 100,000 pairs/year:** US$18–25 per pair: approximately US$7–10 for four coated moulded lens elements, US$4–6 for frame and captive adjusters, US$2–3 for scratch shields and case, and US$5–6 for assembly, optical inspection and rejects. These are planning allocations, not supplier quotes; their endpoint totals are US$18–25. A shared washable fitting kit is targeted at US$30–50 and 200 fittings before replacement: US$0.15–0.25 per fitting, excluding worker time. For a **three-year** service-life target, one US$21 pair plus US$3/year estimated shields and maintenance is **US$10/person-year** (21/3 + 3), excluding fitting, examination, transport, tax, and replacement after breakage. A locally sourced fixed-power prescription pair could be cheaper or better; procurement must compare delivered cost and optical quality, not only ex-factory price.

## Impact

A planning deployment of **100,000 pairs/year**, if independently fitted and retained, has capacity to serve up to 100,000 wearers that year, not evidence of 100,000 successful corrections. Proposed go/no-go trial targets in *eligible adults* with predominantly spherical error: at least **80%** reach binocular distance acuity of **6/12 or better** and no more than **5%** of participants requiring referral are incorrectly cleared, with uncertainty intervals and subgroup reporting. These are aspirations, not measured performance; separate near-vision and pediatric protocols require validation. A professional refraction and best-corrected acuity examination are the comparison standards. Report unusable/fogged or scratched lenses, discomfort, retained use after six months, and referral completion rather than assuming productivity or health gains.

Polycarbonate is durable but fossil-derived; design for replaceable elements, repairable screws, local take-back and controlled recycling rather than calling the frame biodegradable. Scratches, lens decentration, screw drift, accommodative overminusing, uncorrected astigmatism and missed pathology can all make a cheap pair harmful. Screen with both lenses at their locked setting, reject a frame that fails optical inspection, and refer whenever vision or symptoms fail the gate; never issue a reassuring result after a failed screen. Avoid use as a substitute for clinical care or as a condition of employment. The device has no sensing, surveillance or control function.

**Validation path (10–20 years):** bench-map optical power, off-axis distortion and lock drift through repeated adjustment and abrasion; run supervised adult comparisons against cycloplegic/subjective refraction and conventional glasses, with masked assessment of acuity and false-clear referrals; validate in multiple climates and communities before scaled manufacture. Stop or restrict the design if referral safety, optical standards, cost, or acceptability fail. Longer-term universal access depends on trained eye-care services, not this device alone.
