# Fish-Safe Intake Gate — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**

**Status:** Proposed research milestones; no prototype or approval exists.

## Concept

Survey candidate 10 L/s gravity diversions, local species and smallest protected life stages, seasonal river stages, sweep flow, sediment, flood risk and water rights. With the fishery authority, set aperture, velocity and biological stop criteria; reject sites without enough sweeping current, safe access or an irrigation shutdown contingency. Gate: signed site requirements and a mass-balance/screen-area model, not a purchase order.

## Prototype

Build a serviceable 316L wedge-wire/HDPE module with float limiter, differential-head trip, guarded hydraulic brush and screen-presence latch. In a flume, map local velocities at 10 L/s and low stage, head loss and delivered flow; inject asymmetric blockage, failed springs, seized brush and removed panels. Gate: no unscreened flow after any tested fault and a fishery-approved velocity envelope; redesign if no reliable trip setting exists.

## Pilot

At one permitted reach, compare the instrumented gate with a maintained, approved fish screen at matched flow and stage, then extend to ten eligible diversions only after the single-site gate passes. Log fish contact, entrainment, injury and delayed outcomes by life stage alongside delivery, trips and maintenance over a migration season. Gate: independent biological review and pre-agreed stop thresholds; no claim of superiority based on hydraulics alone.

## Scale

Publish test procedures, installation drawings, spare-part tolerances and operator lockout instructions; gather multi-season data across eligible river types. Quote total installed and lifecycle costs, including civil works and shutdown losses, against accepted screens. Gate: repeatable manufacturing and local permits with no worse verified fish outcome or unacceptable water-delivery penalty.

## Ubiquity

Offer the module only where a site-specific screen plan passes the competent authority's criteria; train maintainers and publish anonymized long-term ecological and uptime data. Update or withdraw designs when protected life stages, climate-driven low flows or new evidence change the safe envelope. Gate: independently audited outcomes and cost over many seasons, not a speculative 2050 adoption figure.
