# Flood-Isolating Wellhead Collar — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1. Concept-stage gates, not achieved milestones.** Each phase can stop in favor of a conventional elevated watertight cap and vent.

## Concept

Define the approved 100–150 mm outside-diameter drilled-casing envelope with well contractors and regulators. Survey actual flood elevations, vent routing, penetrations and existing sanitary caps before freezing the split polypropylene/EPDM/316-stainless design. Exit only with a documented fit/rejection checklist, a vent-flow protocol and a side-by-side comparator design; no household deployment.

## Prototype

Build instrumented samples and matched code-compliant cap/raised-vent controls. Challenge all cap penetrations for 60 minutes at 0.8 m static freshwater head, then repeat after silt exposure and 100 wet/dry cycles; record mL leakage with a stated detection limit, witness false trips/misses and vent pressure drop at pump start/run. Separately test microbial ingress and aging; exit only if independent testing finds no detectable cap-path ingress under the defined method and no worse vent reliability than controls.

## Pilot

After local sanitary/electrical approval, a qualified contractor installs at most 100 eligible wells alongside 100 matched compliant-cap controls, with baseline and post-flood microbial/chemical sampling and documented alternative-water guidance. Track annual inspection adherence, service hours, witness behavior, site-specific flood exposure and actual cap-path ingress. Exit only on demonstrated incremental protection without reduced testing compliance or pump/vent safety; a dry season is inconclusive, not a pass.

## Scale

Seek independent product/material-contact qualification and each jurisdiction's well-code approval before procurement by health departments, utilities or insurers. Validate batch quality, replacement parts, installer training and a full installed-cost comparison; the US$75 ex-factory kit and US$12 service-parts figures remain unverified 2035 volume targets, not quotes. Stop scaling if a raised conventional cap is more reliable or cheaper at a site.

## Ubiquity

Make site surveys, pre-flood inspection and post-event testing routine wherever this design has actually beaten controls; provide a serviceable witness and repair supply chain rather than a one-time gadget. Long-term surveillance must demonstrate intact ventilation, seal durability and owner adherence across climates before any broad flood-resilience claim. Regardless of hardware, suspected flood-affected wells still require qualified inspection, safe alternative water and applicable testing/disinfection.[1]
