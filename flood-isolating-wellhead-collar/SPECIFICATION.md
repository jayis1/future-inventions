# Flood-Isolating Wellhead Collar — Engineering Specification

**Author: jayis1. Concept targets only; no qualification or field performance demonstrated.**

## Boundary and installation

For a contractor-approved 100–150 mm outside-diameter intact drilled-well casing, survey the casing top, flood design elevation, annular seal, water outlet, pump cable and local code before selecting a collar. The design excludes dug wells, pits, damaged/short casing, unsuitable annular grout and locations where a rigid raised vent cannot be supported safely. The casing and annulus themselves are outside the collar's sealing boundary. Compare against a compliant watertight cap and high vent before selecting a retrofit; EPA guidance already recognizes sanitary seals and elevated vents.[3]

The two-piece glass-filled polypropylene shell has a captive EPDM compression gasket, positive compression stops and a replaceable 316 stainless band. Cable and pipe penetrations use individually sized strain-relieved bulkhead glands; no splicing of wet electrical conductors inside the shell. A screened, downward-facing vent rises above the approved flood elevation without a normal-operation check valve. Its pressure drop under the actual pump's starting and running flow must be measured, not guessed. The mechanical flood witness sits outside the inner water seal and latches on exposure; the optional dry contact only reports or inhibits pump operation through a code-approved control. A false trip sacrifices pumping, never silently declares the water safe.

## Proposed qualification protocol

| Test | Measured quantity | Unverified acceptance target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Static head on fitted casing and services | Leakage at cap, bulkheads and vent termination during 60-minute hold | No visible cap-path ingress at 0.8 m head; also culture/test water separately, since visual inspection cannot establish microbiological safety |
| Silt and debris, then 100 wet/dry cycles | Leakage, clamp force, gasket condition, vent pressure drop | Same static-head criterion; no vent blockage at qualified pump duty |
| Size and installation tolerance sweep | Gasket compression, conductor/pipe strain, fit across approved casing sizes | No cut insulation or cracked pipe; reject incompatible penetrations |
| Witness challenge including splash, rain and full immersion | False trips and missed trips, reset state | Define thresholds from field testing before assigning any protective rating |
| Cold/UV/chemical exposure and annual service simulation | Seal aging, corrosion, visibility and time to inspect | Replace or redesign parts whose condition cannot be verified visually |
| Failed contact, loss of power, stuck witness or blocked vent | Pump state, indication, water quality response | A blocked vent or ambiguous flood state triggers manual stop/inspection; never a safe-water indication |

A test must include a conventional code-compliant cap and elevated vent as comparator on matched casings. Independently sample the water before and after challenge for applicable microbial and chemical hazards; a pass of the mechanical test is not a drinking-water clearance. Obtain the locally required sanitary and electrical approvals before any household trial. CDC advises safe alternative water after suspected flood contamination and contractor-led well work.[1]

## Maintenance, disposal and decision gates

Inspect the exposed vent screen and witness before each flood season and after storms; a well contractor gauges the seal and checks the casing/annulus, and a qualified electrician checks wet electrics. Never reset a tripped witness just to restart a pump. Replace EPDM and damaged glands as inspected, retain the main shell only if it passes dimensional and leak checks, and segregate metal from polymer at disposal.

A 10–20-year development program proceeds from bench pressure/vent tests to accelerated weathering, independent code review, controlled field trials and paired flood-exposure data. Kill criteria: worse vent reliability or maintenance burden than a raised conventional cap; undetected ingress; inaccessible parts; or installations that make post-flood testing less likely. No claims of patent novelty or prevented illness follow from a passing bench test.
