# Flood-Safe Egg Sink — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1** · Illustrative planning arithmetic, not a measured vector-control or health impact. Design inputs come from [README.md](./README.md) and [SPECIFICATION.md](./SPECIFICATION.md); there are no prototype or field results.

## What can be counted

The device is a 0.75 L nominal-water *Aedes* ovitrap that must be inspected weekly and after heavy rain. It is not safe for submerged outlets, missed service or unverified adult retention. The potential incremental benefit is **fewer adult-producing failed traps than a conventional screened ovitrap**, not eggs collected in isolation. No dengue cases, infections or lives saved can be estimated from the present evidence.

## Assumptions (all unverified)

- Time horizon: one 52-week operating year; three alternative deployment sizes, not cumulative cohorts. Installed units include withdrawn or out-of-service units; a functioning fraction is assumed solely to explore capacity and must be measured in a pilot. It is unrelated to the ≥90% bench rainfall acceptance target.
- Limited pilot: 500 installed, assumed 90% functioning on average; regional illustration: 10,000 at 80%; large illustration: 100,000 at 70%. These availability fractions are **hypothetical**, not forecasts or safety thresholds. Failed units must be removed, not left wet.
- Planned service: one scheduled visit per installed unit per week (52 annually), regardless of operational fraction, plus uncosted extra post-rain visits. Actual completed visits and labor time require field logs. A functioning trap-week is one functioning unit operating for one week, not one household protected or one case prevented.
- Annualized hardware: unverified US$18/unit ex-factory divided by assumed five-year life = US$3.60/unit-year. Weekly strip/sticky-liner pair target US$0.35 × 52 = US$18.20/unit-year; screens/gasket allowance US$2/year. Total **US$23.80 per installed unit-year** in target materials (including annualized housing); actual replacement, freight, disposal, taxes and financing may differ.
- An added **US$1 per scheduled visit** is only a sensitivity input, not a wage quote. It adds US$52/unit-year before extra storm visits, training or management. If staff time costs more, this estimate rises proportionally.

## Attribution and decision gates

A flood-test pass rate is not a field functioning fraction; neither shows mosquito suppression. In paired lab enclosures, measure eggs captured, adult emergence/escape from each *complete* unit and both trap types' non-target captures. In the 500-unit, 12-month matched-neighborhood pilot, log completed visits, flooded/withdrawn units, failures per trap-month, costs and nearby unmanaged containers. Both arms need the same source-reduction education. The incremental adult emergence difference must be measured against conventional serviced traps before extrapolation; converting egg counts to disease outcomes is invalid. For health effects, a separately powered cluster-randomized evaluation and local public-health review are needed.

The scale scenario would require 5.2 million scheduled visits annually before storms. If weekly access, safe disposal or a dry overflow cannot be funded, withdraw the traps and favor source removal or a proven simpler option. If adult escape occurs in a complete unit, halt deployment; if the conventional trap performs equally well at lower cost, do not scale this design. UV/heat aging, screen corrosion, adhesive waste, replacement housings and ethical disposal remain environmental and cost uncertainties, not net benefits counted here.
