# Flood-Safe Egg Sink — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Research gates, not launch commitments. Advance only with local vector-control authority approval; see [SPECIFICATION.md](./SPECIFICATION.md).

## Concept

Fix the operating envelope (1.5 L body, 0.75 L nominal water, no submerged entrance or outlet) and compare drawings and bill of materials with a conventional screened ovitrap and habitat removal. Exit only when an authority and prospective servicing team agree on safe siting, weekly visits and insect-proof waste handling; otherwise stop before placing water outdoors.

## Prototype

Build at least 30 complete units with dual ≤0.4 mm 316-stainless screens, EPDM lid gasket, baffled entry, replaceable liner and mechanical fault indications. Run two full local *Aedes* development cycles, 100 mm/day rain including a 50 mm/hour pulse with leaf/silt obstruction, and 1,000 gasket/float cycles plus aging tests. Require zero adult escape from intact units, ≥90% sealed/draining and visibly identified or withdrawn failures; any escape requires redesign and repeat testing, not a field trial.

## Pilot

Subject to bench gates and authority/ethics review, compare 500 serviced units for 12 months with ordinary screened traps under matched source-reduction education. Log weekly service, post-rain inspections, adult escape, egg capture, non-target impacts and cost per functioning trap-month; withdraw failed units. Exit only if the extra overflow and indicators improve containment without material loss of attraction or unmanageable service labor; do not infer dengue incidence from trap counts.

## Scale

If the pilot establishes incremental benefit, validate local manufacturing, replaceable parts and the unverified US$18 ex-factory target at 100,000 units/year; audit screen fit, liner aging and safe disposal across suppliers. Test regional routes before increasing unit counts. Stop expansion if servicing cannot keep up or a simpler maintained trap performs as well.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, consider inclusion in authority-approved flood-season kits only where each trap has a funded weekly service and withdrawal route; inspect actual performance and replace housings only after demonstrated durability. The exit condition is a sustained, auditable reduction in hazardous trap failures against the simpler comparator—not mere distribution volume or an unmeasured health claim.
