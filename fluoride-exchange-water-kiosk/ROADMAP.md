# Roadmap — Fluoride Exchange Water Kiosk

**Author: jayis1.** This is a research and service-development plan, not a certification schedule. All gates refer to the screened 5 mg/L design source and ≤1.0 mg/L proposed tap criterion in [README.md](README.md); applicable local standards and source hazards may require stricter gates.

## Phase 1 — Concept

Characterize at least three representative seasonal source waters for fluoride, pH, competing ions, metals and microbiology. Measure dynamic breakthrough and aluminum leaching with 12 kg lead/lag beds rather than relying on equilibrium capacity. Exit only if a 2,000 L lead exchange interval has a documented margin and source-screening rules exclude unsuitable wells; otherwise redesign or choose an existing safe supply.

## Phase 2 — Prototype

Build one locked, sampleable kiosk and a sealed cartridge-return loop; log flow, lockouts and independent split tests over at least six months, including power loss and wrong-cartridge fault injection. Exit only if every observed failed or overdue release check closes the tap, the ≤1.0 mg/L criterion is met at every authorized sampled dispense, and hub waste has a documented lawful destination.

## Phase 3 — Pilot

Run a 12-month audited comparison at multiple sites against a maintained local alternative, including safe backup water during shutdown. Publish compliant litres, sampled breakthrough, actual uptime, media exchanges, operator hours, hub waste and fully loaded cost per m³. Exit only with independent water-quality approval for the intended use, no known unsafe release after a failed test and a viable paid maintenance contract.

## Phase 4 — Scale

Qualify regional suppliers and hubs with cartridge traceability, measured regeneration-cycle life, permitted waste treatment and spare-part inventory. Expand only where audits reproduce the pilot's safety and economic outcomes across seasonal water changes; suspend any site with unresolved contamination, missed sampling or missing residual custody.

## Phase 5 — Ubiquity

Where public programs or cooperatives adopt the service, make independent sampling, accessible quality records, safe alternatives during lockout and funded end-of-life disposal standard procurement terms. Success is sustained verified compliant water and accountable residual handling across years—not a kiosk count or an inferred disease-prevention statistic.
