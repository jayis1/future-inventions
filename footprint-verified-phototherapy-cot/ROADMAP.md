# Roadmap — Footprint-Verified Phototherapy Cot

**Author: jayis1** · Concept only; no clearance or clinical validation. Each phase is conditional on its exit gate.

## Concept

Define the cot's qualified mattress-plane rectangle, applicable infant sizes, nine tile coordinates and acceptance logic with neonatal clinicians and metrology staff. Build an optical budget for 460–490 nm LEDs, diffuser and polycarbonate cradle, then compare a paper nine-point map against dense independent measurements. Stop if plausible occlusions or sensor uncertainty make a reassuring false pass unavoidable.

## Prototype

Build serviceable LED, driver, battery, placard and tile assemblies; bench-test spectral irradiance, minimum and variation across the intended plane, LED/cradle aging, thermal behavior, cleaning, power interruption and alarm faults against calibrated reference instruments. Target ≥30 µW/cm²/nm at all nine defined points, not proof of skin dose. Progress only after pre-compliance review against applicable IEC 60601-1 and IEC 60601-2-50 requirements, plus an agreed false-pass limit and usable backup workflow.

## Pilot

Subject to ethics approval and the applicable authorization pathway, compare supervised use against correctly maintained conventional LED phototherapy at sites able to test bilirubin and escalate care. Measure valid-map starts, false passes, interruptions, time to backup, bilirubin trajectory and adverse events; keep independent clinical oversight. Stop or redesign if the map adds delays or fails to improve operational reliability without harm.

## Scale

Only after favorable clinical and human-factors results and jurisdiction-specific clearance, qualify suppliers and field service for modular LED/driver replacements, meter calibration, cradle cleaning and battery replacement. Track reliability and full installed cost rather than treating the US$180 ex-factory cot target as a selling price. Procurement requires trained staff, replacement parts, routine bilirubin testing and a credible referral route.

## Ubiquity

Aim for repeatable, locally serviceable footprint checks in supervised newborn wards, not universal unsupervised treatment. A deployment counts only while its meter is in calibration, alarms work and clinical follow-up is available; audit those measures against maintained standard units and discontinue the added workflow where it provides no measurable benefit. The 2050 vision is conditional, not an adoption forecast.
