# Ghost-Net Recovery Float — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** All gates are proposals; no phase has been demonstrated. The device is a timed visual backup marker, not an automated net lifter or locator.

## Concept

Agree with licensed fixed-net fishers, a fisheries authority and marine-safety reviewers on an eligible ≤20 m operating envelope, lawful soak-time/72-hour release setting, ownership records and abort rules. Draw the retained cage, enclosed 25 m spool and ~1.5 L float, and calculate reserve buoyancy and line loads; exit only when the group approves a test specification that does not replace legal marks.

## Prototype

Build serviceable polypropylene-cage, spring-timer and cork/PE-float units and test ≥100 timed wet releases at 0–20 m, 100 spool cycles, 30-day salt immersion, pressure/fouling and 0.5 kN fixture proof loads. Seek ≥90% timely surfacing and ≥95% tangle-free payout without net displacement or new free loops. Stop and redesign if any break, entanglement hazard or uncontrolled detachment appears; these are laboratory gates, not sea certification.

## Pilot

With permits and recovery capacity, compare 100 fitted nets with comparable legally marked controls over one fishing season. Record all sets, lost-net incidents, surfaced and missing modules, false releases, complete net retrievals, crew/vessel hours, wildlife interactions and port disposal. Advance only if incremental fully recovered nets, device recovery and total cost show net benefit with no device-caused entanglement or navigation harm; a small pilot may be statistically inconclusive.

## Scale

After replicated pilots in multiple fisheries and an independent ecological/safety review, qualify suppliers and local repair/collection chains. Test the US$45 ex-factory target at 100,000 units/year and ~US$10 annual servicing against actual quotes; include vessel, search, installation and recycling costs in procurement decisions. No production expansion if effective recovery, compliance or lost-module controls deteriorate.

## Ubiquity

Only where long-term monitoring supports it, offer an interoperable marked backup module as an optional complement to legally required marking and lost-gear reporting. Each serial has a custodian, inspection history and return path; retire the design where gear types, depth, currents, habitat or local economics make it unsafe or ineffective. Widespread adoption is a contingent 2050 vision, not a scheduled outcome.
