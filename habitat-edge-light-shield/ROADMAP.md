# Habitat-Edge Light Shield — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Research concept; dates, costs and performance below are planning assumptions, not commitments or achieved results. Each phase requires road-authority and manufacturer approval where applicable.

## Concept

**2026–2027:** Survey candidate pole and habitat geometries, define compatible host-luminaire families, and model the silicone TIR segments and aluminum vanes against unchanged compliant LED fixtures. Exit only when ray-tracing identifies configurations with a credible ≥50% reduction in boundary vertical illuminance without breaching applicable road-visibility and glare limits; simulations alone do not authorize field use.

## Prototype

**2028–2030:** Fabricate indexed optical cartridges and test each approved host family by goniophotometry, thermal cycling, ingress, UV, salt and vibration protocols. Exit when independent bench measurements support the road-safety and boundary-light targets and the qualified manufacturer and road authority authorize a reversible installation; otherwise redesign or retire that host family.

## Pilot

**2031–2033:** Install a 40-luminaire paired, controlled pilot with unchanged compliant lamps on matched road segments. Record boundary and road photometry, glare, input watts, upkeep and seasonal taxon-specific insect observations before and after installation. Exit only if every treated site meets safety requirements, the boundary-light target is met at eligible sites, and the 30% insect-observation hypothesis is assessed transparently with uncertainty rather than treated as proven recovery.

## Scale

**2034–2038:** If pilot evidence holds, qualify additional host families, publish the eligible-pole denominator and provide repairable spare optics through approved installers. The unquoted cost goal is US$65 ex-factory per compatible unit at 10,000/year by 2038, excluding survey/installation (US$90–180 per site). Exit when independent audits confirm repeatable road and habitat acceptance, serviceability and a defensible lifecycle cost at up to 10,000 installations; noncompliant sites remain unchanged.

## Ubiquity

**2039–2050:** Offer surveyed boundary-light criteria as an optional procurement specification wherever validated habitat sensitivity justifies them, alongside—not instead of—road-light standards. Exit only if multi-region monitoring demonstrates durable optical compliance and no unacceptable road-safety or taxon trade-off; broad adoption is a conditional scenario, not a target to fit every light point.
