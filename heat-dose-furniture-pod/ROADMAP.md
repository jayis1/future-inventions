# Heat-Dose Furniture Pod — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**

All dates are conditional; this is an unvalidated concept, not a certified treatment device.

## 1. Concept

Define the eligible item list, exclusion card, probe placement and failure-state specification around a 230 V, ≤1.5 kW dry-heat chamber. Model warm-up and thermal gradients without claiming lethality from air temperature alone. **Exit:** independent pest-control, electrical and fire-safety reviewers approve a test protocol and explicit stop criteria.

## 2. Prototype

Build an instrumented laboratory unit with guarded heater, four contact probes and an independent manual-reset overtemperature cutoff. Map slow points using reference probes; inject disconnected/displaced probes, door and fan faults, and power loss. **Exit:** zero false-completed records in 200 deliberate fault cycles, plus documented thermal and material damage maps; these tests alone do not validate insect control.

## 3. Pilot

After electrical/fire review, run supervised trials on defined eligible articles against matched air-timed commercial-chamber treatments. Use concealed adult and egg sentinels, two-week hatch observation, calibrated traces, damage logs and kWh meters; retain an integrated pest-management follow-up pathway.[1][2] **Exit:** independent review of survival, false passes, user safety and cost per successfully treated item, with a predefined decision to narrow eligibility or stop if efficacy fails.

## 4. Scale

If pilots succeed, qualify replaceable fans, seals and probes, certify the applicable regional product and train housing/reuse operators. Monitor failures, annual calibration, maintenance and comparable treatment outcomes across sites. **Exit:** third-party safety approval and repeatable site audits with no unresolved critical hazards; the US$900 ex-workshop target remains a quote-dependent goal.

## 5. Ubiquity

By 2050, *if* field outcomes and economics support it, shared repairable pods could be a standard option at reuse centers alongside inspection and other IPM methods. **Exit:** independently published longitudinal evidence of safe item-level outcomes and net waste/energy effects across multiple regions; broad adoption is a scenario, not an entitlement.
