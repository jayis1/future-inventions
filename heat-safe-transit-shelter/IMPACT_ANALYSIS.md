# Heat-Safe Transit Shelter — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1. Status: illustrative scenario model, not measured benefit or a deployment forecast.** The design is an unpowered, water-free 3 m × 2 m shelter. The [README.md](README.md) defines experimental targets against a same-footprint conventional open shelter; no pilot has demonstrated them. The Guangzhou and Houston field studies cited there establish the need to compare actual stops, not an effect size for this kit.

## Assumptions and calculation

Each row is an alternative steady-state network, not successive years. Assume each installed stop is available for the stated fraction of hot-day encounters (weather, repairs and actual use); a person may be counted on multiple days. Assume a **10-minute wait** per eligible encounter; this is an illustrative input, not measured ridership. Exposure opportunity = sites × eligible encounters/site/hot day × hot days/year × availability × 10/60 hours. It measures time spent at a functioning shelter, **not** hours of heat illness prevented or a verified cooling dose. Only a site-specific paired trial can establish any MRT difference or whether a given encounter benefits.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Conservative | 100 | 50 | 60 | 0.50 | 150,000 | 25,000 | 600 m² |
| Base | 1,000 | 100 | 90 | 0.70 | 6,300,000 | 1,050,000 | 6,000 m² |
| Optimistic | 10,000 | 200 | 120 | 0.90 | 216,000,000 | 36,000,000 | 60,000 m² |

The canopy area (sites × 6 m²) is **not** the effectively shaded ground area: roof orientation, side sun and adjacent buildings change that. The pilot's ≥70% shaded waiting footprint is a target at three selected solar times, not an all-day network average. No unique-person, avoided-illness, ridership or productivity totals are inferred.

## Cost and resource envelope (unquoted planning assumptions, US dollars)

Installed costs of $3,500–5,500/site and routine maintenance of $80–150/site-year follow the README targets on existing eligible pads; new civil works, emergency repairs, land, financing and inflation are excluded. Frame life is provisionally 10 years and roof coating/liner life 5 years. One midpoint replacement allowance of $350/site during that period is illustrative, **not** a supplier quote; sensitivity uses $0/$350/$700 per site. Straight-line ten-year cost = sites × (installed cost + 10 × annual maintenance + one replacement allowance), with no discounting or residual value.

The labels vary both network scale and unit cost; thus the optimistic reach case is **not** a cheap-cost case. At the README's proposed production ceiling of 1,000 kits/year, 10,000 sites would require at least ten years of output before attrition and replacement; it is not a one-year result. Operational electricity = 0 kWh/site-year and operational water = 0 L/site-year for the passive kit (excluding manufacture, transport and cleaning water). Aluminum and steel extraction, coatings, transport, installation and replacement have embodied impacts that cannot be quantified without masses, supply-chain data and a lifecycle assessment. A conventional shelter, tree planting/maintenance and the no-build option must be compared on matched sites before claiming net environmental benefit.

## Decision thresholds and limits

Proceed only after the paired hot-season study demonstrates the README's MRT, seat and shade targets with reported uncertainty and no worsening heat stress or accessible boarding. Monitor coating reflectance, annual damage and actual availability to replace scenario inputs with observations. A cooler surface does not establish lower morbidity: any avoided-illness estimate needs separately validated exposure-response evidence and a study of actual riders. High humidity, calm air, nighttime heat, blocked ventilation or missing maintenance can erase the modeled opportunity; post heat-response and cooled-refuge guidance regardless of installation.
