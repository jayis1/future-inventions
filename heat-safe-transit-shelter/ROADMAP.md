# Heat-Safe Transit Shelter — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Phases are conditional engineering gates, not promised deployment dates. See [README.md](README.md) for the unverified performance targets and safety exclusions.

## 1. Concept

Survey candidate stops for sun angles, pavement, airflow, existing tree shade, accessible boarding, visibility and drainage. Select a conventional open shelter as the principal comparator; agree on the waiting footprint and test plan with riders and the transit authority. Exit only when a qualified engineer has reviewed the 3 m × 2 m layout, code constraints and a bill of materials without assuming the US$1,800 workshop target is a quote.

## 2. Prototype

Fabricate a replaceable coated-aluminum roof, 80–120 mm vented cavity, matte fire-rated liner, bolted frame, site-specific fins and accessible bench. Bench-test initial reflectance ≥0.75 and emittance ≥0.85, corrosion, flame behavior, anchorage, hot-touch temperatures, glare, blockage and soiled-vent performance; these are procurement/design targets, not certified results. Exit only after structural, fire and accessibility reviewers approve a safely instrumented trial installation.

## 3. Pilot

Compare ten matched candidate/conventional stop pairs over a full hot season including a heatwave, still humid conditions and a nighttime check; include tree-shaded comparators where available. Log five-minute paired air, humidity, wind, irradiance, MRT estimate, seat temperature, shade footprint, glare and wheelchair boarding, with uncertainty and failures reported. Exit only if the README's radiant/seat/shade gates hold without worse heat-stress readings, unsafe sightlines or obstructed boarding; inspect again after storms and deliberate vent obstruction.

## 4. Scale

If the pilot passes, commission independent replication in another climate and solicit actual supplier and civil-work quotes before any volume commitment. Train local crews for twice-yearly cleaning, post-storm inspection and modular replacement; track coating loss, damage, availability, ten-year lifecycle cost and embodied materials. Expand only to surveyed stops where the locally approved kit outperforms or complements maintained trees and ordinary shelter upgrades.

## 5. Ubiquity

By 2050, agencies could make heat-exposure and accessible-boarding audits routine and choose repairable shade kits where their evidence warrants it. A network-wide program remains conditional on reproducible gains, sustainable maintenance funding, procurement access and local approvals; publish negative results, retain cooled-refuge guidance and do not claim prevented illness without a separate health study.
