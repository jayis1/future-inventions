# Insulin Heat-Buffer Sleeve — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**

**Status: proposed development gates, not a clinical or regulatory authorization.** Product-specific storage instructions govern every test and use.[1]

## Concept

Select a sealed near-28 °C paraffin insert and one-pen cradle, calculate the accessible latent-heat budget and whole-assembly resistance, then write a comparison protocol against an ordinary insulated pouch, commercial PCM case and evaporative wallet. Exit when material compatibility, fit, reconditioning and failure-state requirements are specified, including a validated path for rejecting a warm or damaged insert.

## Prototype

Build at least 10 units and log cavity and container temperatures at one-minute resolution in shaded 40 °C air at 30% and 85% relative humidity, from a fully solid 24 °C start, with six 15-second openings. Exit only if repeated runs meet the proposed eight-hour cavity target without freezing, 200-cycle seal tests show no leaks, indicator failures are bounded, and qualified labs assess product-specific potency/degradation after simulated journeys against controls. If measured performance or product labeling does not support the application, redesign or stop; this gate precedes human-use Pilot.

## Pilot

Only after product-specific stability results, safety review, ethics approval where required and jurisdiction-specific regulatory assessment, recruit a small supervised cohort in two climates for one labeled in-use period. Record container exposure, conditioning success, incorrect assembly, indicator false negatives, cleaning incidents and comparator performance—not inferred clinical benefit. Exit on predefined safety and usability criteria and a demonstrated advantage in cost per label-compliant trip; otherwise do not scale.

## Scale

Qualify sealed-insert manufacturing and independent lot sampling, obtain real supplier quotations and confirm whether the proposed US$20 ex-factory cost at 100,000 units/year is achievable including quality control but excluding distribution and refill indicators. Exit only with documented leak/recharge durability, product-specific instructions, recall logistics, and procurement trials that beat an appropriate lower-cost comparator.

## Ubiquity

Offer the sleeve as an optional, repairable short-journey accessory where independent testing and procurement evidence justify it, with training, replacement inserts and take-back for damaged packs. The exit condition is sustained, audited label-compliant transport and affordable per-trip cost across hot and humid settings; it never replaces refrigeration for products requiring it or extends any labeled shelf life.[1]
