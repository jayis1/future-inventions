# Kiln Draft Balancing Retrofit — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1**

**Status: illustrative scenario model, not measured savings, a market forecast or a carbon-credit claim.** Applies only to eligible induced-draft zigzag brick kilns **after** identical operator training and routine cleaning. Published training effects are separate: a Bangladesh trial of 276 zigzag kilns found 10.5% less energy use and 171 t CO₂ and 0.45 t PM₂.₅ lower emissions per kiln-year from treatment assignment; none is a measured effect of this kit.[1]

## Basis and assumptions

| Input | Illustrative value | Evidence / limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Training-only coal use, B | 100 t per kiln-season | Assumed round-number scenario, **not** a measured or typical kiln baseline; replace with weighed fuel by grade |
| Saleable output | Held constant within a matched pair | Needed to convert per-brick targets to seasonal totals; measure actual accepted bricks and rejects |
| Incremental coal reduction, s | 2%, 5%, 8% (low/base/high) | Assumptions; 5% is the untested README pilot target, not a guaranteed result |
| Coal oxidation factor, e | 2.4 t fossil CO₂ per t coal | Illustrative assumption equivalent to ~65.5% carbon by mass if fully oxidized (44/12 × carbon); replace with fuel assay and applicable accounting factor |
| Coal price | US$150/t | Assumed landed price; not a local quotation |
| Kit cost | US$1,500 ex-factory at 10,000 kits/year by 2038 | Design estimate from README; volume and date are targets |
| Survey, installation, training, testing | US$800–2,000/site | README estimate; assumes up-front payment and excludes freight/tax/permitting |
| Added service | US$300/season | Scenario allowance, not a quotation; actual cleaning, replacement and testing costs are unknown |
| Sites in scenarios | 100, 1,000, 10,000 | Hypothetical eligible adoption, **not** counts of available kilns |
| Seasons | One per year | Simplifying assumption; no multiyear persistence or discounting modeled |

Arithmetic per site: saved coal = B × s; gross fuel value = saved coal × US$150/t; net seasonal cash before electricity and other omitted costs = gross value − US$300 service; direct fossil CO₂ avoided = saved coal × 2.4 t/t. This CO₂ estimate excludes changed fan electricity, other greenhouse gases, embodied hardware, transport and production rebound. Do not add published training savings to these *incremental* kit scenarios.

| Scenario | Sites | Coal saved/site (t/season) | Total coal saved (t/season) | Direct CO₂ avoided (t/season) | Net cash/site before omissions (US$/season) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low, 2% | 100 | 2 | 200 | 480 | 0 |
| Base, 5% | 1,000 | 5 | 5,000 | 12,000 | 450 |
| High, 8% | 10,000 | 8 | 80,000 | 192,000 | 900 |

Example base: 1,000 × 100 × 0.05 = 5,000 t coal; 5,000 × 2.4 = 12,000 t direct CO₂. Base up-front cost is US$2,300–3,500/site (kit plus site budget), yielding a simple **5.1–7.8 seasons** payback at US$450/site-season before omitted costs. High case is **2.6–3.9 seasons**; low case has no positive net cash and no finite payback. These are not investment returns: discounting, downtime, repairs, electricity, taxes and financing may eliminate payback entirely.

## Emissions and human outcomes

The separate **≥10% stack PM₂.₅ mass per saleable brick** pilot target cannot be converted into tonnes avoided without a measured training-only PM mass baseline, throughput and valid stack sampling across operating states. If that baseline is P t/site-season at unchanged output, the *target-only* arithmetic is 0.10 × P × participating sites; do not substitute the published training trial's 0.45 t reduction for P.[1] CO must **not increase** at stack or occupied areas. Pressure taps cannot measure PM, black carbon or worker dose. There is no supported estimate of avoided illness, deaths or carbon-credit income; exposure studies and independent additionality verification would be required.

## Sensitivity and boundaries

At a lower coal price or increased service expense, the net cash case worsens linearly: each US$50/t reduction in assumed price removes US$250/site-season from the base case's 5 t saved; net falls from US$450 to US$200 before omitted costs. At fixed output, per-brick and total savings align; if production expands, report **absolute** fuel, CO₂, PM and worker exposure as well as per-brick values to reveal rebound. If fan kWh rises, account for its local grid emission factor and cost before claiming a net climate or economic benefit. Embodied refractory/steel emissions and end-of-life soot management also require a measured lifecycle inventory. All site eligibility, safe installation and regulatory compliance remain gating conditions, not model outputs.
