# Impact Analysis — Lead-Dust Capture Shroud

**Author: jayis1. Illustrative scenario arithmetic, not a forecast or evidence of protection.** The shroud is an untested adjunct for qualified manual paint-removal work on suitable flat surfaces. See [README.md](README.md), [SPECIFICATION.md](SPECIFICATION.md) and [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). EPA work-area containment, cleanup and verification remain necessary.[2]

## Boundary and evidence

The CDC estimate of roughly 29 million US housing units with lead-based paint hazards in the README[1] is *not* an estimate of suitable scraping jobs. This model instead defines an arbitrary, fixed cohort of **1,000 eligible qualified crews** for one illustrative year after successful validation. There is no measured product adoption, correct-use rate, field reduction in lead exposure or health outcome. No claim about blood-lead levels, illness prevented or regulatory compliance follows from the arithmetic.

## Inputs (explicit assumptions)

| Input | Assumption, not observed fact |
| --- | --- |
| Eligible crews | 1,000 with at least one suitable flat-surface job/year; scenario denominator only |
| Adoption | 10%, 25%, 50% of eligible crews purchase one unit (low, central, high) |
| Correct sustained use | 50%, 80%, 90% of purchasers respectively; requires fitted skirt, qualified vacuum, containment and maintenance |
| Shroud manufacturing cost | US$120/unit ex-factory target at 10,000/year in 2038 from the specification; applying it to smaller cohorts is a simplifying assumption, not a supplier quote |
| Annual replacement/servicing allowance | US$40 per purchased unit, invented budgeting assumption; no vendor estimate |
| Annual refresher/training allowance | US$100 per correctly using crew, invented budgeting assumption |
| Time horizon | One modeled year; first-year purchase plus annual allowances, undiscounted USD |

## Conditional throughput and cost

Purchased units = 1,000 × adoption. Correct-use crews = purchased units × correct-use fraction. Modeled first-year spend = purchased units × (US$120 + US$40) + correct-use crews × US$100.

| Scenario | Purchased units | Correct-use crews | Purchase | Service allowance | Training allowance | Modeled first-year spend |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 100 | 50 | US$12,000 | US$4,000 | US$5,000 | US$21,000 |
| Central | 250 | 200 | US$30,000 | US$10,000 | US$20,000 | US$60,000 |
| High | 500 | 450 | US$60,000 | US$20,000 | US$45,000 | US$125,000 |

Sensitivity: at central adoption but only 10% correct sustained use, **25** crews, not 200, would use the control correctly; modeled spend would be **US$42,500** (= 250 × US$160 + 25 × US$100). None of these figures includes the qualified HEPA vacuum, hose, professional labor, containment, PPE, testing, regulatory review, sealed waste collection, transport, disposal, taxes or distribution; real total program cost will be higher and may change adoption materially.

## What would constitute impact

The specification's **≥95%** lower airborne lead *outside the shroud* versus dry unshrouded laboratory scraping is a development target, not an observed field reduction and not a multiplier for health benefit. The dry baseline is a controlled laboratory comparator, never a recommended work practice. The relevant field comparator is established compliant wet/manual or wet-plus-HEPA work. Independent testing must separately establish capture mass balance, personal breathing-zone lead (including filter service), post-cleaning surface contamination and human use errors at matched removal rates. Only then could a measured exposure difference be modeled alongside baseline exposure distribution, occupancy and other lead sources. Do not turn units sold, dust trapped or short-task samples into avoided disease cases. At every phase, stop scaling if proper controls outperform the device or its service chain creates secondary exposure.
