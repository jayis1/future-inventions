# Roadmap — Lead-Dust Capture Shroud

**Author: jayis1. Research concept; no field-use authorization.** Phase gates refer to [SPECIFICATION.md](SPECIFICATION.md). Existing compliant work practices remain the baseline.[2]

## Concept

Specify the flat, sound substrate and manual-scraper operating envelope with renovation professionals and an independent industrial hygienist. Map failure modes from skirt contact to sealed vacuum disposal; predeclare comparisons against wet/manual and wet-plus-HEPA work and define a practical non-worsening margin before tests. Exit only with a reviewed protocol, approved vacuum interfaces and safe waste plan.

## Prototype

Build a transparent polycarbonate/silicone, 38 mm-neck unit with two independently checked pressure signals and a spring-return blade. Calibrate the tentative 60 L/min and 10 Pa settings against actual leakage, then run the specification's randomized fault tests and controlled, qualified laboratory lead-panel comparisons; measure personal air, deposition, maintenance release and removal rate. Exit only if every development gate passes; otherwise redesign or abandon the interlock claim.

## Pilot

After laboratory success and local occupational/regulatory review, compare 20 qualified crews over 12 months with established compliant practice. Log full-shift personal lead exposure where indicated, post-cleaning wipes, false stops, ergonomic workarounds, time, consumables and service exposures. Exit only on independently reviewed non-worsening exposure and clearance outcomes, with demonstrated benefit worth the added burden; a green indicator is not clearance.

## Scale

Qualify specific vacuum/hose combinations, production calibration, skirt tolerances, service training and a sealed consumable collection/disposal channel. Independently repeat the failure and exposure tests on production units and realistic filter-loading states. Exit only when a funded service chain, field monitoring and applicable approvals keep pace with sales; the US$120 ex-factory 2038 target excludes most deployment costs.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, *if* comparative safety, affordability and the waste chain hold, offer a documented capture-verified option to qualified renovators for suitable surfaces. Periodically audit exposure, faults, servicing and post-work verification against current practice, retire failing configurations and keep conventional containment, hygiene and regulatory compliance intact. Adoption alone is never the success metric.
