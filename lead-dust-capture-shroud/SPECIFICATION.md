# Engineering Specification — Lead-Dust Capture Shroud

**Author: jayis1. Research design only; all numeric performance and cost values are unverified targets.** Regulatory work practices and manufacturer instructions take precedence. See [README.md](README.md) and its cited CDC/EPA sources.

## Boundary and mechanism

For qualified renovation crews on **reasonably flat, sound substrates** whose paint treatment has been professionally planned. No powered abrasion, no household use, no attempt to make a room lead-safe by tool attachment alone. A clear polycarbonate cover surrounds a hand scraper with a captive replaceable stainless blade. A removable silicone skirt is compressed against the work surface; a 38 mm sealed hose takes debris to a purpose-designed HEPA renovation vacuum, not a retrofitted shop vacuum. EPA describes shrouds and HEPA attachments as existing controls and requires containment and cleanup independently of them.[2][3]

An orifice/restriction in the **cleanable extraction neck** has upstream/downstream pressure taps. A differential sensor estimates airflow after calibration with each approved hose/vacuum combination. A second pressure channel measures shroud interior relative to ambient near the skirt. High flow with an open seal and strong static suction with a clogged hose can each be misleading; both signals must be in their tested operating bands. The restriction cannot be allowed to reduce capture below qualification flow. Prototype targets are **at least 60 L/min through the neck and at least 10 Pa shroud depression** during use, subject to empirical tuning; these are *not* published safety thresholds and should be rejected if high-leak trials still pass. A spring-return blade retracts if either channel, sensor self-test, cell voltage or actuator fails. A battery-powered controller provides visible red/green status; green only means those checks passed. Calibrate zero and restriction response before each shift using a traceable manometer/flow reference according to an approved service protocol, not by scraping a test patch. Record the service interval and test the actual battery runtime; **500 hours is a housing-life target, not a single-battery promise**.

## Engineering and handling gates

- Assemble to keep chips inside the shroud when the vacuum stops; check that a stalled or reversed vacuum does not expel retained chips. Provide captive fasteners and a wipe-clean neck; no porous foam in the contaminated path.
- Pre-use inspection by a qualified worker: intact skirt, housing and hose, seated HEPA vacuum components, functional red/fault state and blade return. Any paint too loose to hold skirt contact or any visible dust outside the shroud means reject the task and use an approved alternative.
- A failed indication is a stop-work signal, **not** permission to continue with manual dry scraping. Do not claim the spring guard is a certified safety interlock until tamper, wear and human-factors tests pass.
- Keep the vacuum connected while withdrawing the blade; within work-area containment, wet-clean the outside, seal contaminated blades/skirts and service vacuum bags/filters under manufacturer and local requirements. Avoid dry brushing, compressed-air cleaning, or opening loaded parts in occupied space. EPA's RRP work-area containment, cleanup and verification requirements still apply.[2][3]
- Housing designed for repair and eventual qualified decontamination; contaminated replaceables follow local disposal rules. No wireless link, image capture, location log or worker identifier.

## Comparative validation (before any deployment claim)

1. An independent industrial-hygiene laboratory prepares identical sealed test panels of a **characterized lead-paint coating** on flat wood, matched for lead loading, adhesion, humidity and age. Work occurs only in a controlled enclosure with qualified personnel, appropriate protections and approved waste handling. Measure baseline airborne and surface lead, plus paint mass removed, for each panel. Never run an unshrouded dry-scraping comparator in an occupied building.
2. Randomize replicate panels and operators among: (a) shroud plus purpose-designed HEPA vacuum, (b) professionally specified wet/manual or wet-plus-HEPA practice, and (c) unshrouded dry manual scraping **in the laboratory only**. Match exposed area, nominal scraping duration (**10 minutes**), blade geometry and removal mass as closely as possible; report deviations and normalize mass release per gram of paint removed, as well as per task. Record actual flow, shroud pressure, hose length, vacuum model and filter state continuously.
3. Collect calibrated personal breathing-zone and enclosure/background air samples with filter-based **laboratory lead analysis**, plus standardized surface wipes at the shroud perimeter and outside the work zone before/after work and after cleanup. Real-time particle counts may help locate leaks but **are not lead-specific**. Include filter changes and skirt/blade removal in exposure sampling. Use laboratory blanks, duplicate samples, recovery checks and a reporting limit below the planned comparison endpoint; report uncertainty and censored results explicitly.
4. Introduce at least **100 randomized fault trials** spanning vacuum disconnect, progressively clogged filters, pinched hose, lifted/missing skirt, warped substrate, cracked cover, low/removed battery, failed pressure tap and unplugged sensor. Predeclare the permissible flow/pressure bands from pilot data, then lock them for blinded tests. Target **100/100 blade retractions** for gross disconnect/power-loss faults, and **≥99% detection across all induced faults** without a false green; report each fault class, not just an aggregate. If a plausible hazardous leak passes the sensors, redesign or abandon the interlock claim.
5. Development pass criteria: **≥95% reduction in airborne lead mass outside the shroud** versus dry laboratory control under equal removed-paint mass; **no statistically or practically material worsening** of breathing-zone lead, post-cleaning surface loading or service exposure versus compliant wet/HEPA comparator; no visible escaped dust in the intended operating envelope; and fault gates above met. Predefine sampling power and material-worsening margins with an independent hygienist before trials. Failure to meet *any* gate prevents deployment claim; a dust percentage is not a certification or a blood-lead outcome.

## Field decision

Only after laboratory success, seek local regulatory and occupational review and run a 20-crew, 12-month pilot against established compliant methods, recording false stops, removal rate, maintenance burden, actual installed cost, personal air and wipe results, and occupant exclusion/clearance outcomes where required. If benefit disappears against well-maintained wet/HEPA work, the added electronics and consumables are not justified. Lead exposure is a multi-source problem; the prototype does not replace professional hazard assessment, containment or post-work verification.[1][2]
