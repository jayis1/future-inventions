# Lead-Line Release Profiler — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Research concept, not an approved lead assay or compliance method. Each gate can stop the project; follow the receiving laboratory and regulator's requirements.[1][3]

## Phase 1 — Concept

Specify an open-to-air five-bottle index rack, lab-approved container interface, cleaning instructions and an observer-scored loose-bottle comparator. Consult utility workers and accredited laboratories; exit only when a receiving lab accepts a research collection protocol and a blinded fault-test plan is preregistered.

## Phase 2 — Prototype

Build a repairable polypropylene/isolated-stainless demonstrator and test with clean water and inert tracer particles, not lead at households. Across 0.5–2.0 L/min, weigh each fraction and tray, challenge the detent with deliberate swaps, and compare fraction recovery with ordinary bottles. Gate: ≤10 mL loss per changeover, ±20 mL fill indication and no unflagged swaps in the planned fault trials; reject or redesign if the chute changes recovery.

## Phase 3 — Pilot

After independent bench validation and ethics/operator approval, recruit a proposed 50-site research cohort with consent and accredited laboratory analysis. Randomize rack versus loose-bottle order on separate visits, because an initial draw depletes stagnant water; capture blanks, duplicates, invalid runs, staff time and full cost. Gate: ≥99% correctly ordered and sealed fractions across 500 supervised five-bottle sessions, a statistically interpretable comparator difference, and no unexplained contamination or biased lead recovery.

## Phase 4 — Scale

Only if the pilot beats ordinary bottles without sampling bias, test replacement parts, lab-container compatibility, worker training and anonymized custody records across multiple utilities. Seek program and regulator acceptance before any official use; publish independent error rates and reject a cost claim until procurement, transport and accredited-analysis quotes are obtained.

## Phase 5 — Ubiquity

Conditionally make the offline rack a locally repairable option wherever a trained sampling program actually benefits from it. Audit drift and operator errors, periodically revalidate against loose bottles, and retire it if the advantage disappears. Neither widespread adoption nor a low profile replaces lead-line replacement, corrosion control or appropriate public-health action.
