# Locally Repairable Terrain Wheelchair — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**
**Status:** Proposed gates, not achieved milestones. No occupied testing before engineering, service, ethics, and local regulatory approvals.

## Concept

Co-design the fitting and repair workflow with wheelchair users, technicians, and three independent workshops. Survey tire, spoke, steel and fastener availability in three prospective service regions; draft controlled interface drawings, gauges, material specifications and failure-mode analysis. Exit only when the three shops can review a common drawing and a clinical service has defined fitting and follow-up requirements; otherwise narrow the interface ambition.

## Prototype

Build a small set of differently configured chairs and replacement modules, including modules made by independent shops. Test dimensions and fit, then conduct unoccupied static stability, brake, impact and fatigue evaluation under applicable ISO 7176 parts at worst-case approved settings, including wet-tire and post-swap checks. Exit only after documented strength, retention and braking acceptance, a resolved failure log, and usable service instructions; passing a gauge alone is insufficient.

## Pilot

After local approvals and independent safety review, compare at least 50 individually fitted candidates with 50 appropriately fitted existing chairs over 12 months, as in the README's proposed evaluation. Record repairs, downtime, costs, transfers, user-reported participation, pressure injuries and falls with trained follow-up and alternative mobility on failure. Exit only with zero unresolved critical brake, weld, axle-retention or tip failures and a reviewed comparison; do not infer benefit from delivery counts.

## Scale

Qualify material suppliers and production shops against the same drawings and destructive-test samples, audit incoming modules, and fund fitting, cushions, training and parts distribution separately from hardware. Expand by region only after consistent safety and service data and an auditable delivered-cost estimate; the $180 ex-workshop and $220 delivered hardware figures remain unquoted targets, not a procurement ceiling for clinical needs.

## Ubiquity

Publish a stable interface specification and qualified supplier registry so a trained service can source parts from multiple local workshops without guessing compatibility. Refurbish and refit chairs for subsequent users only after structural inspection and personal assessment. The exit condition is independently verified cross-workshop interchange, sustained service access, and documented user outcomes across multiple regions—not a particular production volume or a guaranteed 2050 date.
