# Mercury-Free Gold Concentrate Station

**Author: jayis1. Status: research concept (TRL 2); not a certified mineral-processing plant.**

## Problem

UNEP reports that up to 20 million people work in artisanal and small-scale gold mining and that the sector is a major source of mercury pollution.[1] Mercury amalgamation exposes miners and nearby communities through vapor and contaminated tailings. Gravity concentration can avoid adding mercury when the ore contains liberated gold, but existing processing centers may retain miners' tailings, use hazardous downstream chemistry, or give miners no way to check whether a mercury-free batch is economically viable.[2][3] This concept is for voluntary miner cooperatives processing **assayed, gravity-recoverable ore**, not for every deposit.

## Solution

A cooperative-operated, enclosed wet-processing station accepts a weighed batch of previously characterized ore. A replaceable stainless-steel sizing screen separates coarse material; a guarded, adjustable mini shaking table concentrates dense liberated particles from a narrow size fraction using recirculated water. Gold's density (~19.3 g/cm³) differs from common quartz gangue (~2.65 g/cm³), but particle liberation, size and hydraulic conditions determine actual separation.[2] The concentrate, middlings and tailings each enter **separately numbered, sealable HDPE tubs** on a bunded floor. A settle-and-filter loop returns clarified process water to a covered tank; captured solids remain in the batch inventory. There is no mercury, cyanide, acid leach, furnace, or open tailings discharge in the station.

The differentiating feature is a **batch-accounting return gate**: a mechanical feed latch is released with three captive tags returned from the closed output tubs; the operator then weighs those tubs and logs make-up water before starting the next batch. Tags enforce container return, **not** honest weights or recovery. The record belongs to the cooperative and contains batch masses, not worker identifiers. Periodic independent assays of representative feed, concentrate and tailings samples estimate gold recovery; weighing alone cannot measure it. If assays show poor liberation, excessive fine-gold loss, contaminated legacy material or no acceptable local downstream route, stop processing and return the sealed fractions to their owners for licensed handling. This station does not prescribe a smelting or chemical extraction route.

## Key Innovation

Relative to existing mercury-free shaking-table plants and gravity training guides,[2] the proposed advance is a **closed-loop, batch-segregated cooperative station that pairs a captive-tag container-return gate with periodic independent recovery checks**, instead of treating an attractive concentrate as proof that mercury replacement worked. The innovation is the testable service and material-accounting interface, not gravity separation itself.

**Unverified engineering targets:** 25 kg dry feed/hour for ore within a laboratory-established liberation window; ≤50 L fresh-water makeup per 100 kg feed after settling and filtration; ≥90% solids mass accounted for across separately captured output streams during a supervised run (the balance allows wet solids and sampling uncertainty, not missing mercury); and a study-specific gold-recovery target of ≥70% of assayed gravity-recoverable gold, **not 70% of total ore gold**. Test these with paired fire-assay or accredited equivalent samples and dry-mass measurements across at least three ore types. Fines below about 0.1 mm, sulfide-bound gold and highly variable feed can defeat the table; no recovery target applies without ore characterization.[2][3]

## Target Cost

**Unquoted 2038 design goal at 1,000 stations/year:** US$3,800 ex-factory, comprising guarded table/motor US$1,700, screen and slurry feed US$450, settling/filter/tank loop US$650, bunding and reusable sealed tubs US$600, and controls/guards US$400. Estimate US$600/year for wear parts, tub seals, water-loop maintenance and calibration, excluding power, installation, ore crushing/milling, independent assays, transport, permits, labor and safe tailings storage; these exclusions may exceed hardware cost. A cooperative or publicly supported processing center pays. At 25 kg/h × 4 h/day × 200 days/year, nominal capacity is 20 tonnes/year; US$3,800 over five years plus US$600/year is **US$68 per tonne** before those exclusions. This is a planning scenario, not a demonstrated price or a claim of profitable gold recovery.

## Impact

A ten-year **capacity scenario** of 1,000 fully utilized stations would process at most 20,000 tonnes/year of eligible ore; it does not establish how many miners would switch from mercury or how much mercury release would be prevented. The primary pilot metric is **kilograms of mercury introduced per tonne of ore processed at participating sites**, measured against matched local practice, alongside assayed gold recovery, worker exposure and final tailings custody. The station design prohibits mercury input, but legacy contamination and off-site handling could still cause exposure. Benefits must be verified without pressuring miners to surrender ownership of ore, concentrates or tailings.

The hardest development problem is achieving acceptable recovery *and* transparent custody at a cost that miners choose over amalgamation. In a 10–20-year pathway, first characterize ore and compare table performance with local practice under independent supervision; then pilot 10 stations for a season with cooperative consent, blinded assay reconciliation, mercury-exposure monitoring and auditable tailings destinations. Expand only if recovery and livelihood are not worse, discharge meets local limits, and safe disposal or licensed downstream processing exists. Dust from upstream milling, water-borne sediment, mechanical entanglement, legacy mercury and theft remain hazards: require machine guards, wet housekeeping, closed containers, protective procedures and a secured, legally approved tailings route. No worker tracking, remote control or surveillance is required.

## How It Works

A cooperative accepts only a characterized, gravity-recoverable ore batch with an agreed owner, downstream destination and sampling plan. Record dry-equivalent input mass; take a representative feed sample for an independent assay, with chain-of-custody seals. Wet sizing sends out-of-envelope coarse material to a labeled return container rather than silently grinding it; the guarded table then divides the eligible slurry into concentrate, middlings and tailings. Gold's density helps only where gold is liberated and the feed is sized appropriately.[2][3] Reprocess middlings only as a separately logged pass, never as an invisible addition to the next owner's batch.

Drain and seal the three numbered output tubs on a bunded floor. Return clarified water from settling and filtration to the covered tank; measure makeup, collect filter solids with the same batch, and retain any purge for tested, permitted handling. The three captive tags from returned tubs release the mechanical feed latch; the operator still has to log weights and reconcile dry solids. A tag proves container return, not honest mass, gold content or safe disposal. Independent feed/output assays estimate recovery; the cooperative and miner can compare the receipt and dispute results before transfer. The station delivers **concentrate, not doré**: refining, sale and any thermal treatment are outside its boundary.

## Technical Architecture

- **Physical circuit:** intake scale and sample seal → stainless sizing screen → guarded wet shaking table → numbered concentrate/middlings/tailings tubs. Settler, filter and covered tank recirculate water; sampled purge and captured solids remain accounted-for outputs, not a claimed zero-discharge stream.
- **Custody circuit:** captive tub tags interlock the next-feed latch; a paper or offline-first batch ledger records incoming and output dry masses, samples, water makeup, custody transfers and assay references. The cooperative retains the record; a second witness countersigns disputes. No identity tracking or network service is required.
- **Protection and shutdown:** splash enclosure, electrical isolation near water, guards and a bunded floor protect operators. A missing tub, damaged seal, anomalous mass balance, contaminated feed or unavailable licensed tailings route stops new intake. Isolation and cleaning require trained staff; legacy mercury can be present even though this circuit adds none.

## Performance Benchmarks

These are **unverified design targets**, not prototype results or claims of superiority. Comparator values must be measured at the *same site* on split, characterized feed and include ore preparation and downstream handling where applicable. Existing gravimetric plants are established practice,[2] so there is no defensible universal state-of-art recovery number for unlike ores.

| Metric (common basis) | Station design target | Current-practice comparator / validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible feed throughput | 25 kg dry ore/h for a defined liberation and size window | Measure kg dry ore/h at local gravity or amalgamation workflow over a supervised shift; include downtime. |
| Assayed gold recovery | ≥70% of **gravity-recoverable** gold on at least three characterized feeds | Paired feed/concentrate/middlings/tailings assays plus duplicate samples; measure local practice on matched feed. This is not total-ore recovery. |
| Dry solids accounted for | ≥90% across all captured outputs during a supervised run | Dry, sample-corrected mass balance; investigate any gap rather than treating the remaining 10% as permission to lose tailings. |
| Fresh-water makeup | ≤50 L per 100 kg dry feed, excluding initial fill | Meter makeup and retained purge over the same campaign; compare local measured liters/100 kg, not a generic plant. |
| Equipment-only cost and utilization | US$3,800 ex-factory goal; US$68/t at 20 t/year, five-year allocation plus US$600/year service | Obtain supplier quotes and independently cost installation, assays, labor, power, water, permits and safe residue handling before comparing full costs. |
| Mercury introduced inside station | 0 kg/t by design prohibition, **not** proof of mercury-free feed or zero exposure | Measure kg/t introduced in the displaced local workflow, plus incoming contamination and worker exposure; do not equate use avoided with emissions avoided. |

## Deployment Scenarios

1. **Cooperative shared hub:** a group with assayed alluvial or milled ore books separate batches; at the design duty of four operating hours per day and 200 days/year, one station could accept 20 t/year. Cooperative dues cover custody, assay and safe-tailings services only if the full cost is affordable to members.
2. **Licensed hard-rock processing center:** screened, liberated material from an existing mill enters the table while coarse and refractory fractions remain outside its recovery claim. The center pays per documented batch, retains separately sealed residues and benchmarks gold returned against its current process before asking miners to switch.
3. **Supervised mobile pilot:** a public or NGO formalization program transports a station among consenting sites with a qualified residue transporter. Throughput is reduced by setup and transit; a measured seasonal capacity replaces the nominal 20 t/year, and the program funds independent assays rather than promising an unproven premium.

## Risks & Mitigations

- **Fine or sulfide-bound gold:** a table may recover less than the current method. Screen mineralogy and liberation first, assay all streams, offer the miner a no-penalty refusal, and stop a site whose paired recovery or net income worsens. Borax is not a substitute for liberation; no chemical finishing is offered here.[3]
- **Contaminated feed and residuals:** old mercury, arsenic or lead can enter with ore; recirculation is not treatment. Pre-screen under local rules, quarantine suspect tubs, test purge/sludge and transfer to a licensed handler; do not heat unknown concentrate on site.
- **Loss, theft and false confidence in the latch:** tags cannot validate weighing or assay. Use independently witnessed sealed samples, a reconciled paper ledger, owner-accessible results and a dispute/return procedure; audit failed mass balances.
- **Worker and community hazards:** upstream silica dust, wet electrical equipment, moving table parts, slips and retained contaminated water require guarding, isolation, wet housekeeping, trained operators, containment and exposure monitoring. Confirm permitting and legal sourcing; do not use traceability to surveil workers or legitimize harmful mining.
- **Adoption and service:** assay fees, repairs, transport and storage may dominate the hardware budget, and delays may drive a return to amalgamation. Stock replaceable screens/seals, publish actual turnaround and full per-tonne charges, and scale only when independently observed recovery and take-home earnings match or improve on local practice.

## Vision for 2050

If independently validated stations prove economical for eligible ores, cooperative processing could make custody and tailings accounting routine rather than optional. A miner could retain ownership of every fraction, see an independently checked recovery estimate, and select a licensed downstream buyer without adding mercury at the station. This is a conditional pathway, not a forecast: refractory ores, weak residue governance and unaffordable assays may keep many sites outside the model. Success would be measured in verified substitution of mercury-using workflows, safer exposure and sustained miner income—not station counts alone.
