# Mercury-Free Gold Concentrate Station — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Research concept, not an operating manual. Phases are evidence gates, not delivery dates; stop or redesign if recovery, safety, custody or miner income fail.

## 1. Concept

With consenting cooperatives, map the complete local ore-to-sale workflow, mercury baseline, legal residue route and who owns each fraction. Independently characterize at least three representative ore types for liberation, size and legacy contamination; specify batch samples, dry-mass accounting, water balance, access to results and dispute rights. Exit only when a qualified handler and accredited assay service are available and at least one ore class is plausibly gravity-recoverable; otherwise do not build the station for that site.

## 2. Prototype

Build the guarded wet screen/table, bunded settling loop, three numbered tubs and captive-tag feed latch. On three characterized feed classes, independently weigh and assay all fractions, measure water makeup and purge, demonstrate that a missing tub prevents a new batch, and measure throughput against the 25 kg/h dry-feed target. Exit only if the eligible feeds reach ≥70% of independently assayed gravity-recoverable gold, ≥90% accounted dry solids and ≤50 L makeup per 100 kg dry feed with no uncontrolled discharge; fail the gate if unsafe residues cannot be handled.

## 3. Pilot

With explicit cooperative consent, operate up to ten supervised stations for a season on characterized eligible feed. Compare split-feed recovery, actual miner take-home returns, queue time, mercury introduced per tonne in the displaced local practice, worker exposure, complete residue custody and full installed operating cost. Independent auditors sample contested batches; stop or redesign a site's service if recovery or income is worse, exposure rises, or permitted tailings transfer fails. No mercury-avoidance claim follows from equipment deployment alone.

## 4. Scale

Standardize replaceable screens and seals, local service training and paper/offline records only after multiple pilots reproduce the safety and economic gates. Obtain supplier quotes against the unverified US$3,800 ex-factory goal, publish *fully loaded* costs including assays and residue handling, and maintain repair turnaround and independent assay access across sites. Expansion requires independently documented replacement of mercury-using processing on comparable ore, not just hardware sales.

## 5. Ubiquity

By 2050, a conditional network of cooperative-owned stations could make independently witnessed recovery and tailings custody commonplace for suitable ores. The exit criterion is sustained, audited substitution at participating sites with maintained or improved miner earnings, measured reductions in mercury input and worker exposure, and lawful handling of every output. Refractory ore and places without safe downstream services remain outside this model; ubiquity is an aspiration, not a forecast or license to weaken oversight.
