# Milk-Can Thermal Shuttle — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1. Status: scenario arithmetic, not observed results or a food-safety certification.** The [README](README.md) defines the 20 L, 12 kg ice, 35°C test and the limits of cooling. All monetary, loss and energy inputs below are illustrative assumptions to be replaced with paired-trial data and local quotations.

## Functional unit and baseline

One full 20 L can per farm-day, collected for 60 days, versus the same milk in an ordinary insulated can on a matched route. A 100-farm pilot therefore handles at most **100 × 60 × 20 = 120,000 L** (6,000 can-trips); a smaller fill reduces every litre figure proportionally. There is no measured spoilage baseline or validated safety benefit. The comparator is uncooled insulated transport without shared ice; if ice is supplied to the comparator, allocate its recharge energy and labor equally and retest the incremental advantage. Rejected volume is an accounting proxy, not proof that the accepted milk is pathogen-free.

## Thermal and energy accounting

Cooling 20 L from 30°C to 8°C needs roughly **1.77 MJ** of milk sensible heat at 1.03 kg/L and 3.9 kJ/(kg·K). If returned packs are water at 8°C, cooling and freezing 12 kg to −5°C needs about **4.54 MJ = 1.26 kWh of thermal extraction**, using 4.18 kJ/(kg·K) for water, 334 kJ/kg latent heat and 2.1 kJ/(kg·K) for ice. At an *assumed* system coefficient of performance of 2, idealized electrical input is **0.63 kWh** before cabinet, door-opening and distribution losses; budget **0.8 kWh per trip** provisionally. Meter actual cycles, including standby and failed batches: 6,000 trips at 0.8 kWh would consume **4,800 kWh**. Grid emissions cannot be estimated without a local electricity mix and lifecycle comparison to the baseline. Avoided milk loss is not automatically avoided emissions: disposal, replacement production and equipment manufacture need measurement.

## Illustrative 60-day rejection scenarios

Assume identical incoming milk, a full can each day and that percentage-point changes are caused by the intervention; none is a measured effect. Retained litres = 120,000 × (baseline rejection % − shuttle rejection %)/100. The illustrative value uses **US$0.40/L**, not a verified farm-gate price. Do not add retained-litre value to another estimate of avoided spoilage for the same milk.

| Scenario | Baseline rejected | Shuttle rejected | Additional retained milk | Illustrative gross value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 2% | 1% | 1,200 L | US$480 |
| Base | 5% | 3% | 2,400 L | US$960 |
| High | 8% | 4% | 4,800 L | US$1,920 |

A null effect (equal rates) yields **0 L**; worse handling could increase rejection. The rows are not probabilities or a forecast. In particular, lower rejection is not automatically safer: microbial-count and temperature criteria must be reported independently.

## Incremental operating and ownership cost

Use the README's **unquoted US$140 kit** (can excluded). If one kit makes 300 trips/year for five years, straight-line allocation is US$140/1,500 = **US$0.093/trip**. Assumed annual upkeep US$15/300 = **US$0.050/trip**; assumed 0.8 kWh × US$0.20/kWh = **US$0.160/trip**; assumed extra pack-exchange and cleaning labor **US$0.100/trip**. Total incremental illustrative cost is **US$0.403/trip**, or about **US$2,420 for 6,000 trips**; the US$45 food can is excluded because the baseline also needs one. This excludes freezer capital, pack inventory beyond the kit, sanitation consumables, taxes, financing, transport changes, certification and disposal, so it is a lower-bound-style ledger, not a price quote. At US$0.40/L the base row's US$960 gross retained value **does not cover** US$2,420 assumed incremental costs; even the high row's US$1,920 falls short. Using only the listed costs would require about **6,050 additional retained litres**, or **5.04 percentage points of 120,000 L**, to break even at that milk price. Do not scale until real prices, freezer amortization and quality premiums justify it.

## Sensitivity, evidence and downside

At 40°C ambient, greater heat ingress and faster ice depletion may void the five-hour target; a 25°C start reduces the milk cooling load but does not excuse missing records. Partly frozen modules, low fill or long loading delays alter both thermal and economic results. For each route report distribution of starting and peak milk temperature, time above local limits, microbial counts, ice mass, metered energy, labor minutes, cleaning failures and actual rejected litres. Examine cracked packs, freezer outages, packaging waste, ergonomics and unequal access to a cooperative freezer. The go/no-go test is paired field evidence of compliant milk handling and positive net value after *all* lifecycle and operating costs, not any one scenario in this document.
