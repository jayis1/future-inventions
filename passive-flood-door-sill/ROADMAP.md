# Passive Flood Door Sill — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Five conditional gates for a manually installed, pressure-seated, load-limiting panel. No product is certified or safe to install without a building-specific engineering review; failure at any gate means redesign or stop.

## Concept

Survey representative above-grade masonry and reinforced-concrete doorways, alternate ingress routes and required exits. Model the panel load, surrounding structure and relief-flow requirement for a specified slow-rise hydrograph, then commission independent floodplain, accessibility and egress reviews. Exit with a bounded eligible-site specification and a bench test plan—not a universal installation drawing.

## Prototype

Build a full-width 0.9 m test opening with replaceable EPDM seals, cam anchors and an independently actuated differential-pressure hatch. At 0.3 m static head for 60 minutes after 100 wet/dry cycles, target <1 L/min per metre of sealed perimeter; at up to 0.6 m test structural deflection and relief opening/closing under site-defined rise rates, silt, blockage and power loss (which must have no effect). Exit only if measured flow actually limits differential load without uncontrolled failure.

## Pilot

Subject the design to applicable third-party product testing and local approvals, then enroll up to 100 engineer-screened, above-grade buildings with safe alternate exits and consent. Record fit time, real ingress, nuisance releases, blocked hatches, inspection adherence and post-flood contamination over a flood season; non-occurrence of a flood is not proof of effectiveness. Stop deployment after any unsafe structural or egress finding and publish adverse results as well as successes.

## Scale

Only after verified pilot evidence, validate traceable manufacturing and replacement-part quality, train installers and surveyors, and audit installed costs against the unverified US$90–140 factory-only panel target. Independently verify anchors and hatch calibration on each substrate type; standardize annual pre-season inspection and a replacement/recall route. Exit when certification, local permission, accessibility compliance and full lifecycle economics support procurement.

## Ubiquity

Offer the panel only as one option within property-level resilience plans where warnings permit manual fitting and the building can tolerate both retained water and deliberate relief ingress. Update eligibility and maintenance rules from measured failure rates; insurers or codes should recognize it only after independent evidence. Prefer elevation, relocation or wet floodproofing when those make the building safer, and retire the device if field outcomes do not justify its cost and risk.
