# Passive Storm-Stow Solar Mount — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Concept plan; gates are targets, not achieved milestones.

## Concept

Build a force-path model for one eligible elevated rooftop module and determine which roof geometries are out of scope. Compare fixed, deployed, moving and candidate stowed positions at multiple wind directions; establish an engineer-reviewed test matrix and site-specific stop-angle selection rule. Exit only with a documented fail-safe and a rejection criterion for anchors, clamps and waterproofing.

## Prototype

Fabricate a galvanized or recycled-aluminum rail with stainless spring/pawls, replaceable vane, sealed damper and mechanical flag. Bench the proposed 12–20 m/s vane-height trigger band, ≤15 s stow after sustained actuation, 30-minute chatter test and 2,000 cycles without a missed catch; wind-tunnel the complete roof/parapet assembly and deliberately jam or break the mechanism. Exit only if no loose or free-swinging parts occur and engineers accept the full operating-to-stowed load envelope.

## Pilot

Instrument matched engineered fixed and retrofit rows at qualifying clinic, cooperative or commercial roofs across multiple storm seasons; a hypothetical 1,000-module cohort might qualify at most 900 modules if 90% pass screening. Record real trip success, false trips, roof-anchor forces, leaks, detachment, lost energy and technician reset time. Obtain independent structural/electrical review and local approval before use; stop if the retrofit worsens the governing site load or waterproofing.

## Scale

With pilot evidence, commission third-party testing and applicable product/site approvals, then negotiate replaceable-part supply and installer training with a racking manufacturer. Audit cost against the unquoted US$170 ex-workshop per-module allowance at 10,000 units/year by 2038, excluding installation and engineering. Exit only with documented whole-system economics and a service network for annual inspections and year-five damper replacement.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, offer a qualified, repairable option in storm-exposed roof designs only where independent engineers and owners find it better than simpler fixed mounting. Publish matched-site outcomes, failure reports and maintenance intervals; keep retraining and replacement parts available. Success is repeatable site approvals and independently observed net benefit, not an arbitrary installed-base number or a promise that all storms are survivable.
