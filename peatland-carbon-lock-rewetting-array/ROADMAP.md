# Roadmap — Peatland Carbon-Lock Rewetting Array

## Phase 1 — Concept & Component Validation (2026–2028)

Laboratory and 1-ha field validation of the three core subsystems: (a) peatcrete MICP formulation achieving 0.5–1.5 MPa compressive strength from site peat + biochar + CaCO₃ + ureolytic inoculum; (b) engineered *Methylocystis*/*Methylobacter* strains with upregulated pMMO + sMMO knock-in demonstrating 8–25 mg CH₄/m²/h oxidation at 10–200 ppmv CH₄ in bench-scale peat mesocosms; (c) cellulose-acetate biodegradable sensor prototypes with 5–7 yr dissolution profile and functional TDLAS CH₄ / NDIR CO₂ flux measurement. First integrated 1-ha pilot on a damaged UK raised bog (e.g., Manchester Mosses or Thorne Moor) to verify weir emplacement, water-table response, and sensor-mesh telemetry. Regulatory dossiers for engineered methanotrophs initiated in EU and Singapore. $5–10M funding from climate-tech investors and EU Horizon grants.

## Phase 2 — Integrated Prototype Pilot (2028–2031)

Scale to 100–500 ha integrated pilots in two biomes simultaneously: Central Kalimantan tropical peat (200 ha, canal-dense) and boreal Canada (100 ha, cold-climate). Deploy 2,000–5,000 smart weirs via amphibious drones, broadcast methanotroph-Sphagnum pellets, and operate the full closed-loop sensor-mesh MRV for 24 months. Primary success metric: demonstrate 70–95% methane-spike suppression and net-cooling within 1–3 years (vs. 10–30 yr baseline) — the pivotal proof point. First carbon credits issued via blockchain-verifiable ledger (Verra or Plan Vivo pathway). Cold-tolerant methanotroph strains validated at 2–10 °C. Drone throughput confirmed at 50–200 weirs/day. $30–60M Series A funding; first commercial carbon-credit revenue.

## Phase 3 — Regional Scale-Up (2031–2037)

Deploy across 50K–500K ha regions: Central Kalimantan (2M ha target), Western Siberia (1M ha pilot), and EU temperate peatlands (200K ha). Fleet grows to 10,000+ drones and 5–20M smart weirs. Cost per ha driven below $500 via manufacturing scale-up and autonomous-drone fleet optimization. Regional rewetting cooperatives established in Indonesia, Russia, EU, and Canada — mixing government, NGO, and community-led operators. Carbon-credit revenue at $15–30/t finances ongoing expansion. Methane suppression confirmed at scale (>80% across 10K+ ha). Peat-fire events in pilot regions drop 80%+ — headline results attract policy commitments from IPCC, UNFCCC, and ASEAN. $200–500M Series B / project finance.

## Phase 4 — Planetary Scale (2037–2045)

50M ha rewetted globally — the single largest ecosystem restoration in human history. Fleet of 50,000+ drones and 50M smart weirs operating across tropical (Indonesia, Congo Basin, Peru), boreal (Siberia, Canada, Scandinavia), and temperate (UK, Ireland, Germany, Baltics) peatlands. Annual impact: 1.5–2.3 Gt CO₂-eq/yr avoided from halted oxidation + 0.3–0.8 Gt CO₂-eq/yr from methane suppression. Peat fires become rare events. Subsidence halted across 5–15M ha of low-lying farmland. Carbon-credit market matures to $30–70B/yr — peatland restoration becomes the most profitable natural climate solution. System transitions from active deployment to maintenance and expansion. $1–3B annually from blended carbon finance, sovereign green bonds, and multilateral climate funds.

## Phase 5 — Ubiquity & Stewardship (2045–2055)

The Carbon-Lock Array transitions from active restoration to long-term stewardship. Weir networks self-renew (peatcrete self-heals and new weirs are deployed only where needed). Sphagnum-methanotroph consortia are self-sustaining — engineered strains have either stabilized in the ecosystem or been replaced by naturally adapted descendants. Sensor mesh is replaced by satellite + sparse in-situ validation. 50M ha of restored peatland is sealed and expanding (peat accumulates at 1–3 mm/yr, locking 3–9 t CO₂-eq/ha/yr into new peat). The system has avoided 15–25 Gt CO₂-eq cumulative, prevented millions of haze deaths, saved millions of hectares from subsidence inundation, and restored habitat for thousands of peatland-endemic species. The technology is open-source, locally fabricated, and community-managed — a planetary public good. The next frontier: protecting and expanding the remaining 100M ha of intact peatland and restoring coastal blue-carbon ecosystems (mangroves, salt marshes) using adapted versions of the same autonomous rewetting + MRV platform.
