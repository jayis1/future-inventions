# Post-Fire Runoff Cassette — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · All gates below are proposed, not achieved.

## Concept

Map permitted, accessible outfalls and measure pre-installation head–flow and seasonal ash/SRP loads against no-device and maintained-basin controls. Reject any geometry that cannot leave the entire original culvert capacity independent of the side-cell; agree on waste classification, worker lifting and sampling protocols before procurement.

## Prototype

Build the 200 L settling chamber and ten parallel 50 L iron-coated-sand trays. Run clean-water tracer and blocked-rack/ice hydraulic trials at site-relevant design flows; measure head loss, TSS by size, SRP breakthrough, Fe/Mn leaching and tray handling across at least ten representative runoff matrices. Proceed only if the original drain remains fully capable during total side-cell obstruction and proposed treated-flow gates (≥80% TSS, ≥50% SRP at up to 1 L/s, 5–25 °C) can be tested reproducibly over a declared volume.

## Pilot

Install only at engineered, permitted outfalls with paired control sites for multiple storm seasons. Record continuous inlet, treated and bypass volumes, flow-weighted pollutant loads, servicing hours, head loss and spent-media disposition; suspend or remove failed installations. Advance only on statistically defensible whole-event improvement over maintained controls without increased road, stream or worker risk.

## Scale

Standardize interfaces, assembly checks, depot reconditioning and an authorized waste pathway after pilots show a favorable full installed cost per treated cubic meter. Train road and watershed crews, audit safety and performance across climates, and publish negative results as well as successes; keep source control and restoration as alternatives.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, conditionally stock recoverable side-cells in regional emergency depots for the subset of burned watersheds where repeated monitoring proves net benefit. Retire or redesign any unit whose bypass losses, labor, waste burden or hydraulic hazard exceed the gains; widespread adoption is a scenario, not a promised milestone.
