# Postpartum Loss-Continuity Mat — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1**
**Status:** Illustrative scenario, not observed performance, forecast, or evidence of lives saved.

## Scope and baseline

Model 10,000 eligible births in one hypothetical program year. The existing comparator is a calibrated drape with an ordinary written handoff, not visual estimation. WHO reports roughly 14 million postpartum-haemorrhage cases and around 70,000 related deaths annually worldwide, but those global figures cannot be used as this mat's attributable impact.[1] The prevalence of missing cumulative readings at handoff is unknown; the rates below are sensitivity assumptions, not measured baselines.

## Calculation

Each scenario assumes one mat per covered birth and at most one relevant handoff per birth. Corrected incomplete handoffs = eligible births × coverage × fraction with a handoff × baseline incomplete-reading rate × relative reduction in incomplete readings. This counts documented handoffs, **not** haemorrhages detected, treatments delivered, transfusions avoided, or deaths prevented. Spending = covered births × assumed US$3.50 delivered cost per mat (US$2.50 hypothetical ex-factory target from the README plus US$1.00 assumed combined freight, training, and disposal allocation). No supplier quotes or validated waste estimates exist; staffing time and clinical treatment costs are excluded.

| Scenario (all inputs assumed) | Coverage | Births with handoff, among covered | Baseline incomplete reading at handoff | Relative reduction | Modelled corrected handoffs | Modelled spending |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 40% | 50% | 10% | 25% | 50 | US$14,000 |
| Base | 70% | 60% | 20% | 50% | 420 | US$24,500 |
| High | 90% | 80% | 30% | 70% | 1,512 | US$31,500 |

For example, the base case uses 10,000 × 0.70 × 0.60 × 0.20 × 0.50 = 420 corrected handoffs, and 10,000 × 0.70 × US$3.50 = US$24,500. Prices are assumed constant across scenarios; no scale discount is inferred. These figures do not subtract the cost of comparator drapes, so they are gross program spending rather than incremental cost or cost-effectiveness. A lower actual baseline incomplete-reading rate sharply reduces the possible incremental benefit.

## What must be measured

A prospective comparison needs a denominator of eligible births, actual stock availability and use, proportion transferred, blinded verification of carried totals against gravimetric/reference records, invalid readings, treatment delay, adverse events, and patient acceptability. Report intention-to-treat and per-use results separately. Clinical outcomes require their own adequately powered, ethically reviewed evaluation; the WHO E-MOTIVE bundle result is not an effect size for this mat.[1] Without a demonstrated causal link from corrected handoffs to timely effective treatment, the model assigns **zero quantified deaths, transfusions, or DALYs averted** rather than fabricating them.

## Sensitivity and failure conditions

The concept fails its impact premise if ordinary drape-plus-chart handoffs are already reliable, contamination or spill exceeds gains, use delays treatment, patients or facilities reject sealed-pouch transfers, total delivered costs exceed local budgets, or replacement and waste systems cannot support use. Benefit may be concentrated where transfers are frequent; equitable procurement must not displace basic obstetric supplies. Re-estimate the table using measured local parameters before any funding or health-outcome claim.
