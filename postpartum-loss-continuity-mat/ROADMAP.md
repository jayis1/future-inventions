# Postpartum Loss-Continuity Mat — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**
**Status:** Proposed development gates, not an approved clinical program. All clinical use requires applicable ethics, regulatory, and infection-control review.

## Concept

Interview patients and maternity teams about transfer, privacy, and current calibrated-drape/chart practice. Map fluid and information paths, then test pouch closures and graduations with measured water and blood analogues. Gate: demonstrate that the handoff failure exists and that a pouch-based solution is worth testing against the simpler chart workflow.

## Prototype

Build heat-sealed polyethylene drapes and pouches with polypropylene outlets; test seam failure, tilt, clots, transport, storage, and waste handling. Run blinded readings against gravimetric reference and simulated two-pouch handoffs. Gate: meet the README's unverified measurement and ≥95% handoff targets without slowing emergency care; otherwise redesign or stop.

## Pilot

After regulatory and ethics approval, compare against calibrated drape plus written handoff in a staged maternity-service study; 10,000 eligible births is a planning ceiling, not an enrollment commitment. Measure missing cumulative totals, spills, time to appropriate care, discomfort, infection-control events, and costs with prespecified stopping rules. Gate: independently reviewed evidence of incremental benefit without increased harm or treatment delay.

## Scale

Qualify multiple film and closure suppliers, validate production seams and printed graduations by lot, and establish training, procurement, and regulated clinical-waste channels. Compare fully delivered cost with existing drapes and reusable trays, not just the hypothetical US$2.50 ex-factory target. Gate: regulatory clearance where required, reproducible quality, affordable delivery, and sustained handoff benefit.

## Ubiquity

If evidence warrants, integrate the method into local postpartum protocols and train teams to carry the cumulative reading through every transfer, including when a different approved device replaces this mat. Audit safety, patient acceptability, waste, and equity periodically. Gate: continued independent evidence that adoption improves handoff reliability without displacing effective haemorrhage detection and treatment.[1]
