# PPD-Free Tread Laminate

**Author: jayis1**

**Status:** Research concept (TRL 2); not a road-approved tire compound.

## Problem

Tire antidegradant 6PPD protects rubber against ozone and oxygen, but its ozonation product 6PPD-quinone can reach waterways in road runoff and is acutely toxic to some fish.[1] Simply omitting 6PPD risks cracked tires and reduced road safety. A substitute that wears faster could also increase particle emissions. The problem spans a global vehicle fleet and aquatic ecosystems; this proposal makes no population-level benefit claim.

## Solution

Develop a **replaceable/retreadable tread cap**, not an entire tire, whose ozone-exposed rubber is an EPDM-rich, silica-reinforced elastomer blend with fine, dispersed solution-SBR and high-cis polybutadiene phases for traction and abrasion. EPDM's largely saturated backbone is the proposed primary ozone barrier; a non-PPD hindered-phenol antioxidant supplies supplemental thermal oxidation protection. Use a thin, compatible, sulfur-cured SBR/BR bonding underlayer designed for conventional retread buffing and adhesion. Candidate silica/silane coupling and sulfur/peroxide hybrid cure schedules must be screened for interlayer adhesion, scorch, grip and cure incompatibility; neither the formulation nor its performance has been demonstrated.

**All inputs to the tread cap and bonding layer must be screened for PPD content**, including reclaimed rubber and processing oils. A PPD-free cap alone does not make the *whole tire* 6PPD-free: sidewalls, carcass and factory contamination are separate emission sources. The first application is an independently qualified, limited-duty retread trial, not consumer installation or mixing instructions.

## Key Innovation

The research hypothesis is a **renewing ozone-resistant tread surface**: if the exposed compound remains EPDM-rich as it abrades, it may avoid reliance on sacrificial, mobile PPD antiozonants while retaining SBR/BR grip and wear. A static surface wax coating alone would be lost during dynamic flexing; a rubber-phase architecture is meant to renew with wear. EPDM blending, silica-filled treads and retreading already exist; the proposed differentiator is the combined PPD-exclusion, full-depth phase-continuity and measured *whole-tire* runoff-toxicity gate, not a claim of patent novelty.

Unlike [Storm-Drain Tire-Toxin Trap](../storm-drain-tire-toxin-trap/), which sorbs contaminants **after** release at a curb inlet, this tests a **source-material change** before particles are created. [Rail Brake Dust Cassette](../rail-brake-dust-cassette/) concerns friction-brake airborne metal particles, not roadway tire chemistry.

**Provisional acceptance targets, not results:** relative to a matched 6PPD-containing tire at the same axle load, inflation and test cycles, ≥90% lower *mass of 6PPD-quinone emitted per tire-kilometre* in captured wear plus standardized runoff; wet-braking distance no more than 5% longer; abrasion mass loss no more than 10% higher; and no earlier ozone crack initiation under matched static **and dynamic** ozone/flex aging. If residual tire sections contain 6PPD, the whole-tire emission target must include them. These arbitrary research gates are not regulatory or safety limits; independent road qualification is mandatory.

## Target Cost

**Unquoted planning target for 2038 at 100,000 compatible retreads/year:** ≤US$20 incremental materials and process cost per tread cap versus a matched conventional retread, with no reduction in certified service life. This is an **incremental** target, not a price for a tire. Illustrative allowances are US$7 for EPDM/blend and specialty compounding, US$6 for silica/coupling/cure optimization, US$4 for additional lamination and adhesion QA, US$3 for environmental batch testing allocation; these are estimates, not supplier quotations. Tire casing, buffing, labor, logistics, road certification, testing programme, failed batches and end-of-life handling are excluded. Cost per kilometre must be compared with a matched compliant retread after actual wear and failure rates are known.

## Impact

If a formulation passes both road-safety and aquatic-toxicity gates, source substitution could reduce 6PPD-quinone formation in the tread fraction and complement stormwater controls; it cannot automatically prevent fish mortality or remove other tire-wear contaminants. A planning pilot of 1,000 qualified retreads would test 1,000 tread caps, not prove a catchment-scale benefit. Report tire-kilometres, tire-wide 6PPD-quinone mass, total wear mass and toxicity of runoff **per kilometre**, plus sidewall/carcass contribution. Compare with matched conventional tires and existing stormwater treatment, including energy/material inputs and end-of-life recovery.

**Safety and falsification:** the hardest unknown is whether an EPDM-rich surface can preserve wet traction, abrasion life, interlayer adhesion and dynamic ozone resistance simultaneously. First run small, contained compound-screening tests; reject candidates that crack, delaminate, lose grip, or shed more particles, regardless of 6PPD-quinone reduction. Analyze ozonated wear particles and leachate by targeted 6PPD/6PPD-quinone assays **and** non-target screening for new transformation products; compare toxicity in independently approved aquatic assays, not chemistry alone. Evaluate cure emissions and production energy, ingredient provenance and recyclability. Progress through benchtop, closed-track, independent road-safety, then paired catchment studies over 10–20 years; no public-road use before tire-maker and regulatory approval. No sensors, identity collection or military application are needed.
