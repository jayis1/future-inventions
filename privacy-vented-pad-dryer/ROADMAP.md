# Privacy-Vented Pad Dryer — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**
**Status: proposed gates, not achieved milestones**

## 1. Concept

Interview consenting reusable-pad users and facility operators about privacy, washing access, climate, and product choice; sketch the collector, opaque airflow baffles, and removable wash-contact parts. Exit when a materials list, vent/heat hazard review, cleaning protocol, and matched-control test plan specify the 30 g dry-mass/30 g water pad load without making disinfection claims.

## 2. Prototype

Build instrumented passive cabinets and optional separately protected PV-fan variants; weigh each pad through drying cycles at specified sun, temperature, humidity, and blocked-vent/rain/fan-failure conditions. Compare 30 units with shaded lines and screened racks over two seasons; advance only if ≥90% of qualified batches meet ≤1.5 g additional water per pad in ≤6 h, pad-contact air stays below 45 °C and fabric-care limits, and ingress and electrical faults are resolved.

## 3. Pilot

With independent hygiene review and informed consent, offer 100 users cabinets and alternatives across wet and dry seasons. Record completion failures, privacy and access preferences, maintenance time, washing-water availability, and actual usage without identifying users electronically; advance only if the product is acceptable, repairable, and clearly labeled as drying rather than sterilization.

## 4. Scale

Obtain supplier quotations and test batches for recycled aluminium, opaque PP, stainless clips, cards, and replaceable screens; verify weathering, assembly quality, cleaning instructions, local repairs, and safe disposal. Set a realistic installed-and-serviced price instead of assuming the unquoted US$18 ex-workshop target, then scale only where washing facilities and trained local maintenance exist.

## 5. Ubiquity

By 2050, distribute open test procedures and locally serviceable variants only if long-run evidence shows reliable drying, voluntary adoption, privacy, and affordable upkeep in different climates. Maintain product choice and no-ready weather guidance; measure waste and any health outcomes separately before claiming broad social benefit.
