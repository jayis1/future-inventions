# Rail Brake Dust Cassette — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**

**Status:** Research gates, not approved deployment. Each phase depends on brake-OEM and operator safety review; failure at a gate ends or redesigns the program.

## Concept

Survey one low-speed urban disc-brake geometry, rotor thermal envelope, cooling path and clearance through full suspension travel. Build a mass/flow and hazard model for 316L pickup, ferrite baffle and heat-resistant cartridge; compare existing brake-source controls and identify sampling locations. Exit only with an OEM-approved test plan, explicitly including blocked, detached, wet and emergency-stop faults.

## Prototype

Build an instrumented dynamometer fixture for the chosen lining and brake. Run matched cassette/no-cassette stop cycles, fresh and loaded, reporting brake-origin PM10/PM2.5 mass at the same sampling boundary, particle number limits, temperature, stopping, flow and leakage. Proposed performance gates are ≥50% PM10 and ≥30% PM2.5 mass reduction, ≤2 kg added mass and ≤50 Pa clean-filter drop at documented flow; no loss of the manufacturer's certified braking/cooling margin is non-negotiable. Exit only after independent repeatability and fault tests, not on a single attractive run.

## Pilot

After rolling-stock safety review, supervise a limited compatible disc-brake fitment and sealed depot exchanges. Record cartridge dry mass, service exposure and downtime; compare matched vehicles, ventilation and season for platform/worker PM while measuring other rail sources separately. Exit only if safety and maintenance limits hold over a qualified loading interval and a net exposure benefit can be demonstrated with uncertainty bounds.

## Scale

Specify fitment variants only for brake families that pass separate clearance, thermal, vibration, ingress and fire review. Cost real units, technician time, waste handling and optional fan energy against low-emission linings and ventilation; seek operator and authority approval before passenger-service procurement. Exit when independently replicated safety, fleet economics and controlled exposure results support a contract, not merely a cartridge sales target.

## Ubiquity

If several operators reproduce the benefit, standardize a service interface, cartridge ID and sealed return route for compatible new rolling stock while maintaining opt-out where regenerative braking or alternate controls are better. Publish fleet-level brake-origin emissions and lifecycle accounting; the 2050 goal is an evidence-backed maintenance option, not universal retrofitting or guaranteed station-air parity.
