# Refrigerant Service Recapture Coupler — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1**
**Status:** Research milestones, not a product release schedule. See [README](README.md) for scope and safety boundaries.

## Concept

Choose one manufacturer-approved nonflammable conventional-pressure refrigerant/port/oil family, document incumbent low-loss fittings and the pressure/temperature envelope, and commission an independent hazard review of liquid trapping, valve sequencing and relief-to-recovery. Exit when a qualified pressure-equipment team approves a test specification, materials shortlist and safe failure-state design; no field use.

## Prototype

Machine a brass/stainless matched pair with replaceable qualified seals, ≤2 cm³ interstitial chamber and captive fail-closed sleeve. On a controlled rig, use independent gravimetry to compare complete cycles against a maintained low-loss fitting under matched conditions; aim for ≤0.5 g total release and ≥0.2 g incremental reduction per cycle, and run ≥5,000 durability cycles with inspection. These are unverified gates, not measured results. Stop on unintended unlock, unsafe pressure excursion or material incompatibility.

## Pilot

Only after required approvals, equip a small licensed stationary-cooling service group with the specified variant and a segregated rated recovery path. Audit at least 500 paired eligible service cycles against the same documented comparator; record releases, failures, extra technician time and recovered-fluid disposition. Exit only if independent data show a statistically defensible incremental reduction without extra safety incidents or unmanageable service delay.

## Scale

Seek jurisdiction-appropriate component/equipment certification, supplier quality controls, lot pressure/leak tests and a replaceable-seal service program. Test whether the unquoted US$45 ex-factory goal at 100,000 pairs/year and US$4 annual seal allowance can survive real certification, labor and recovery-system costs; do not expand to other refrigerants without separate qualification.

## Ubiquity

If independently validated and economically workable, distribute approved variants through trained service networks, with privacy-preserving local mass-balance records and end-of-life metal recycling. Track annual eligible cycles, actual incremental release reduction and safe recovery rates; withdraw a variant if the maintained low-loss baseline catches up. The 2050 vision is conditional, not a forecast or permission for universal connection.
