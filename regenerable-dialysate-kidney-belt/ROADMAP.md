# Regenerable Dialysate Kidney Belt — Roadmap

- **Author:** jayis1
- **Status:** Concept development plan

> This roadmap describes an investigational life-support device. Exit criteria are development gates, not evidence of clinical safety or efficacy.

## Phase 1 — Concept

Close the nitrogen, water, electrolyte, heat, and energy balances in simulation and benchtop rigs using synthetic dialysate at 5–20 g/day urea loads. The phase exits only when ammonia transfer and guard capture remain inside a documented safe envelope, mass balances close to 95–105%, candidate materials pass initial extractables and sterilization screening, and an ISO 14971-aligned hazard analysis identifies independent controls for every catastrophic failure.

## Phase 2 — Prototype

Build a 5–7 kg engineering prototype with the intended dialyzer, pumps, 350–450 Wh LiFePO4 battery, reusable regeneration cassette, disposable blood path, and dock. Run at least 1,000 hours of accelerated closed-loop testing plus ex-vivo blood-loop studies, deliberately inducing occlusion, leaks, bubbles, membrane wetting, sensor drift, depleted sorbent, power loss, and software faults. Exit requires <300 Wh per 12-hour run, validated controlled shutdown, target ultrafiltration accuracy, and no single fault that permits unsafe patient exposure.

## Phase 3 — Pilot

After biocompatibility, electrical, EMC, sterilization, animal, and regulatory gates, progress from stationary first-in-human feasibility to seated mobility and supervised ambulatory use. Compare delivered clearance, fluid control, ammonia exposure, access complications, haemodynamics, usability, and serious adverse events with each participant’s prescribed standard care. Exit requires independent safety review, non-inferior prescribed treatment delivery within the study design, reliable fallback to conventional dialysis, and evidence that trained users can respond correctly to critical alarms.

## Phase 4 — Scale

Deploy clinic-managed docks and several hundred belts across at least three climates and health-system settings while qualifying dual-source suppliers for membranes, sensors, batteries, carbons, and zirconium media. Demonstrate at least 95% completed-session availability over 12 months, auditable sanitation and waste chains, field replacement of wear components, manufacturing yield above 95%, consumables below 150 g/day, and a total service model that does not transfer hidden maintenance or data costs to patients.

## Phase 5 — Ubiquity

Expand only after multi-year post-market evidence supports home, rural, and disaster-continuity use. Standardize interoperable cassettes and clinical records, finance cooperative access, establish battery and sorbent take-back, and publish outcomes by geography and income so inequitable adoption is visible. Ubiquity means dependable local service, universal clinical backup, and continuously monitored real-world safety—not merely one million units shipped.
