# Roadmap — Repairable Iron-Air Outage Bank

**Author: jayis1** · Research concept; all gates are proposed, not completed.

## 1. Concept

Model iron/oxygen reaction inventory, electrolyte and air management, 48 V-class electrical isolation, ventilation and loss budget; compare a serviceable cassette against maintained LFP and generator alternatives. Exit only with a reviewed hazard analysis and a component bill of materials consistent with a 5 kWh DC/1 kW DC target. Redesign if hydrogen/alkaline handling cannot be safely contained.

## 2. Prototype

Build a contained, instrumented module; test 5 kWh usable DC at 1 kW for five hours at 20–35 °C, ≥40% DC-bus round-trip efficiency including auxiliaries/conversion, seven-day idle retention and 1,000 equivalent full cycles to 80% capacity. Inject sensor, leak, airflow and short-circuit faults; verify contactor isolation and technician-only cassette replacement under lockout. Do not claim all gates from a handful of cycles.

## 3. Pilot

After independent safety and electrical review, trial at a noncritical telecom or shelter load for at least six months with a separately maintained backup. Log actual outages, recharge time, usable energy, false trips, electrolyte service, repair duration and cost; compare matched LFP/generator service. Stop if any safety fault cannot be isolated locally or readiness is unreliable.

## 4. Scale

If pilots pass, qualify repeatable manufacturing, spare-parts distribution and locally trained servicing; independently validate certification and disposal pathways in each jurisdiction. Audit the full installed bill of materials and service expense against the unquoted $2,400/module assumption at 10,000 units/year; do not scale if lifecycle cost or safety is inferior for the chosen duty cycle.

## 5. Ubiquity

Only as a conditional 2050 scenario, make published service manuals, interchangeability and material recovery routine across community backup fleets. Audit field performance and equity annually; retain certified alternatives for critical care, sustained multi-day demand and sites where ventilation, charging or repair logistics make iron-air unsuitable.
