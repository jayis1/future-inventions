# Repairable Roadside Noise Baffle — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1. Illustrative scenario model, not a measured impact or a sales forecast.** See [README.md](README.md) for the device and cited acoustic context. All monetary amounts are illustrative US dollars; the US$115/m² manufacturing target is an unquoted **2038** target, not a current supplier price. No avoided illness, carbon savings or beneficiary total is claimed.

## Boundary and baseline

One continuous 100 m × 1.5 m installation (100 replaceable 1 m bays; 150 m² of panel). Compare a same-height, same-length, same-open-area slat screen at identical receiver locations. A permitted solid barrier is a third comparator, *not* assumed to have equal openness or price. The site's existing sound level, traffic distribution, posts, foundations and safe setback must be measured and quoted; no universal bare-road reduction can be inferred. The horizon below is an **illustrative 10 years** after installation, conditional on maintenance and approvals.

| Service life / weather degradation | Unknown | Ten-year accounting horizon is **not** a ten-year warranted service life. |

The 2 dB(A) target is an *incremental, traffic-spectrum-weighted level difference* versus the ventilated slat control, not a claim that the bare roadside exposure or individual health risk falls by 2 dB. At equal spectrum and location, the corresponding A-weighted acoustic-energy ratio is `10^(-2/10) ≈ 0.63` for the target case; it is **not** a 37% reduction in perceived loudness or health burden. Do not add frequency-band decibel values or combine this increment with a solid barrier's general published attenuation range.[3]

| Case | Eligible receivers with line of sight screened | Incremental vs slats | Panel-only 10-year outlay if no replacements | With one complete insert replacement |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Geometry or acoustic failure | 0 | 0 dB(A) | US$17,250 sunk | US$19,950 sunk if replacement attempted |
| Pilot target | 20 (illustrative) | 2 dB(A), unvalidated | US$17,250 | US$19,950 |
| Upside site | 50 (illustrative) | 3 dB(A), unvalidated | US$17,250 | US$19,950 |

The last column is `17,250 + 2,700` **before labor, posts, land, inspection, discounting and replacement of damaged panels**. Straight-line panel-only allocation over ten years would be US$1,725/year (or US$1,995/year including the one insert allowance), which is an accounting illustration, not a quote. Receiver counts must not be treated as unique beneficiaries without an occupancy survey; night and day occupants may overlap. Site coverage must not be extrapolated to WHO's Europe-wide exposed population.[1]

## Decision gates and tradeoffs

The pilot must pre-register fixed near-side and opposite-side microphones, heights, weather exclusions, traffic composition, baseline slat measurements and a wet-season repeat. Record the full distribution of receiver results rather than selecting only improved points. An open screen may provide less attenuation than a solid barrier despite its airflow benefit; an elevated facade, cross-street gap or truck-dominated spectrum may erase even the incremental target.[3]

Aluminum production, anchoring, replacement travel and scrap handling create impacts whose magnitude cannot be projected without a bill of materials, supplier recycled-content evidence and lifecycle inventory. Ventilation can reduce wind pressure but openings also transmit sound and collect debris. Opposite-side reflection, safety clearance, crash exposure and maintenance labor can outweigh the near-side benefit. No health, climate or monetary return-on-investment calculation is justified until site-specific exposure and full installed costs are measured.
