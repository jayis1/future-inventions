# Sealed Window Friction Cassette — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1** · Scenario calculations, not measured outcomes or a forecast. The device addresses one source of lead-bearing window dust; it does not remove lead paint or certify a dwelling safe. See [README.md](README.md) for the mechanism and exclusions.

## Assumptions and model

| Input | Scenario value | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible installed windows | Low 1,000; central 10,000; high 100,000 | Hypothetical adoption, **not** estimated eligible stock |
| Window opening/closing cycles | 1,000 per window-year | Illustration; field-use distribution unknown |
| Trough lead-mass change vs paired untreated control | 90% reduction per 1,000 cycles | Prototype **target**, unmeasured; not a reduction in blood lead |
| Floor and airborne lead | Must not increase | Independent gate; no modeled benefit assigned |
| Installed price | US$120–200 per eligible window | 2038 target, excludes stabilization, repair, testing, taxes, disposal |
| Manufacturing price | US$45 per pair at 100,000 sets/year | 2038 design target, not a quote or installed cost |
| Sealed replacement insert | US$5–10 each, plus qualified service | Design target; assume one per window-year for illustration |
| Service interval | 10,000 cycles **or 12 months**, whichever first | Proposed maximum interval, subject to wear testing |

For measured untreated trough lead mass B per window per 1,000 cycles under matched conditions, the **target** annual reduction with the illustrative duty cycle is 0.90 × B. For N suitable windows, target aggregate difference is 0.90 × N × B, *only if* controls are comparable, the target persists in field use and measured floor/airborne lead does not increase. B has not been measured here: reporting kilograms of lead avoided would manufacture a baseline. The relevant counterfactual for procurement is qualified lead-safe repair, whose incremental dust performance and cost remain to be tested.

## Adoption scenarios (one illustrative operating year)

| Scenario | Windows | Cycles/year | Installation spend at target | Insert parts/year at target |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 1,000 | 1,000,000 | US$120,000–200,000 | US$5,000–10,000 |
| Central | 10,000 | 10,000,000 | US$1.2–2.0 million | US$50,000–100,000 |
| High | 100,000 | 100,000,000 | US$12–20 million | US$500,000–1 million |

Formulae: installation spend = N × US$120–200; annual insert parts = N × US$5–10. These figures exclude qualified annual labor, containment, wipes, disposal, paint stabilization and damaged-window replacement, so they are **not** lifecycle cost or savings. If actual service frequency exceeds once per year, parts and labor rise accordingly. No energy or carbon savings are credited: the EPDM edge shield is not a certified weather seal, and materials, delivery and disposal impose unquantified burdens.

## Sensitivity and decision threshold

At half the proposed dust-reduction target (45%), modeled trough-mass reduction becomes 0.45 × N × B; costs above do not fall. At half the assumed eligible adoption, install and insert-part spending halve, but there is no evidence that health benefits scale linearly. A positive trough result is insufficient if room-side deposition rises or if an established lead-safe repair performs as well at lower lifetime cost. Pilot decisions should use paired measurements of trough **and adjacent floor** loading, airborne dust, installation exposure, egress and window function—not sales volume or assumed child blood-lead reduction.

No numeric health, energy, CO₂, avoided-abatement or landfill benefit can responsibly be projected without measured baseline dust, exposure transfer, comparator effectiveness, component mass and disposal practice. Those are required pilot outputs, not omissions to fill with invented constants.
