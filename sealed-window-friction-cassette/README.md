# Sealed Window Friction Cassette

**Author: jayis1**

**Category:** Healthy Housing & Universal Basic Services / Lead-Dust Prevention

**Stage:** Concept; no safety or performance certification

## Problem

Old painted sliding windows can generate lead-bearing dust when sash and jamb rub. Children can ingest that dust from hands and window troughs. The [CDC identifies friction surfaces, especially windows, as a lead-dust source](https://www.cdc.gov/lead-prevention/prevention/paint.html); the [US EPA reports that roughly three-quarters of pre-1978 US homes still contain some lead paint](https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-renovation-repair-and-painting-program). This is a housing-health problem affecting millions of households, not just a window-operability nuisance. Merely wiping the sill does not prevent new dust, while disturbing an old painted window during uncontained repair can increase exposure.

## Solution

A **professional-installed, removable cassette for eligible double-hung windows** separates the sash's moving bearing interface from painted jamb surfaces. A paired, dimension-matched **polypropylene or recycled-HDPE jamb liner** and **replaceable acetal (POM) sliding shoe** create a polymer-on-polymer track; thin captive **EPDM lips** shield exposed painted edges without becoming load-bearing rubbing surfaces. The installer measures clearances first and rejects windows needing dry sanding, force-fitting, or interference with sash cords, locks, ventilation, or emergency egress. Attachment uses reversible compression against verified sound, non-flaking substrate where geometry permits; any fastener or paint disturbance requires the applicable lead-safe work practice and qualified installer. No resident should attempt to fit this over deteriorating paint.

A snap-closed trough insert under the track catches residual loose debris without being opened during routine sash operation. The cassette is removed inside containment, wetted and sealed in a labeled bag for locally compliant disposal; it is **not** emptied into household waste by the occupant. Existing sill/trough dust must be cleaned by a qualified worker before installation and checked afterward. The insert does not purify air, treat lead in pipes, or make other painted surfaces safe.

## Key Innovation

Ordinary weatherstripping and [commercial sash/jamb liners](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/window-sash-retrofit) improve operation and air sealing; [HUD interim controls](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/lbph-13.pdf) already call for eliminating lead-painted friction surfaces. The proposal is **not** the invention of a liner or lead-safe renovation. Its testable integration is a tolerance-graded, reversible **paired bearing-and-edge shield** plus a contained service path for residual dust, with a measured post-install dust gate: if paint remains in the rubbing path or dust rises, the window is taken out of service pending professional remediation. Unlike the repository's **Electromineral Pipe Regenerator**, which targets dissolved lead at the plumbing-water interface, this prevents one specific indoor lead-dust generation pathway at a moving window.

Prototype targets, **not demonstrated performance**: at least **90% lower lead mass released into the interior trough per 1,000 opening/closing cycles** than a paired untreated window; normal hand operation without painted-surface contact; cassette service after **10,000 cycles or 12 months**, whichever comes first. Measure lead mass with paired pre/post surface wipes and contained dust collection under the same sash, opening force, humidity, paint condition, and cleaning history. Also measure airborne dust and adjacent floor deposition; a lower trough result alone is insufficient if dust escapes elsewhere. Pilot only after independent lead-safe installation and egress review.

## Target Cost

**2038 design target at 100,000 window sets/year:** **US$45 ex-factory per cassette pair**, modeled as US$12 extruded liner, US$8 acetal shoes, US$6 EPDM lips, US$5 closed trough insert, US$4 captive clamps, and US$10 cutting/inspection/packaging. **US$120–200 installed per eligible window** is an unverified target that includes a trained installer and basic containment but excludes paint stabilization, window repair, testing, taxes and disposal fees; those can dominate actual cost. Replacement sealed insert target: **US$5–10 plus qualified service**. Where fit or lifetime costs fail this gate, established lead-safe repair or replacement takes precedence.

## Impact

A proposed pilot would randomize **100 eligible windows** across comparable homes to cassette versus established lead-safe window repair, with independent baseline and repeated dust-wipe measurements, sash-force/egress checks, and condensation/mold inspections over **12 months**. The primary endpoint is the **incremental change in lead loading on interior trough and adjacent floor**, not the number of cassettes sold. At a hypothetical 100,000 qualified installations, no more than 100,000 windows receive this one-path intervention; child blood-lead reduction cannot be inferred without controlled exposure and clinical follow-up. A 10–20-year development path is plausible for extrusion, fit qualification and field trials, but superiority and affordability remain unproven.

**Failure and exclusions:** Gasket detachment, worn shoes, jammed sash, trapped moisture, mold, tampering, or paint flakes outside the track require suspension of use and professional inspection. Maintain drainage and inspect for condensation; never seal drainage weeps or obstruct an escape window. No DIY scraping, sanding, or dry vacuuming. This is an interim control requiring inspection, cleaning, and locally required lead-safe renovation/abatement practice—not certification, a substitute for removing hazards elsewhere, or a guarantee that a home is lead-safe.

## How It Works

1. A qualified lead-safe installer documents paint condition, sash travel, opening force, locks, cords/balances, drainage and required escape opening. Flaking substrate, insufficient track clearance or any required paint disturbance without a compliant work plan means no cassette installation. Existing dust is cleaned and assessed under applicable local practice first.
2. After contained fitting, each polypropylene/recycled-HDPE liner bears against sound jamb geometry. Captive clamps provide reversible retention only where the substrate withstands them; the sash's original supports and locks still carry their designed loads. An acetal shoe on the moving sash slides on the liner rather than abrading painted wood. Actual contact pressure and clamp force require profile-specific measurement, not a universal setting.
3. EPDM lips cover adjacent paint edges but are not friction bearings or a certified air seal. During travel, residual particulate falls toward the snap-closed trough insert; the insert is a secondary containment path, not a filter or a promise of zero airborne lead. A worn shoe, displaced lip or saturated insert triggers a stop-use inspection.
4. The installer checks full travel, closing and escape operation, then obtains baseline and follow-up trough *and floor* wipes. Service is due at 10,000 cycles or 12 months, whichever comes first; a qualified worker removes and bags the sealed insert under containment and replaces worn parts. Unexpected dust loading prompts investigation and established remediation, not repeated operation to meet a target.

## Technical Architecture

The passive assembly has five interfaces: measured jamb-to-liner fit; captive compression clamps to sound substrate; sash-to-acetal shoe; shoe-to-polymer bearing track; and non-load-bearing EPDM edge shield plus removable closed trough. Force runs from the moving sash through its existing support and new shoe/liner contact into the jamb; the retrofit must not alter the primary sash support, latch or egress path. Potential particles run from an exposed paint defect toward the contained trough, while some can escape to the room—hence independent floor and airborne measurements. There is no motor, sensor, battery or data stream; inspections and laboratory wipe results form the human feedback loop. Profile-specific spacers and a rejection gauge are proposed design choices, not validated universal-fit parts.

## Performance Benchmarks

Commercial sash/jamb liners and qualified lead-safe repair are the relevant *current alternatives*, not untreated painted friction alone. [PNNL's sash-retrofit guide](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/window-sash-retrofit) documents the existing liner approach; [HUD's lead-paint guidance](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/lbph-13.pdf) describes interim controls. Neither source supplies a like-for-like lead mass, cost or cycle-life figure for this proposed cassette. The paired untreated sash is an experimental control, **not** a claim that established repair performs worse.

| Metric and comparable conditions | Current evidence/baseline | Proposed acceptance target (unmeasured) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interior trough lead mass per 1,000 cycles, same sash and pre-cleaning | Measure untreated control and established lead-safe repair in pilot | At least 90% below paired untreated control; also no increase on adjacent floor or in airborne sampling |
| Sash operating force and egress, same window before/after fitting | Measure both states; no assumed industry figure | No painted contact or new obstruction; pass applicable local egress and operating-force review |
| Bearing service interval, recorded cycles and calendar time | No comparable measured lifetime available | Inspect/replace by 10,000 cycles or 12 months, whichever comes first |
| Installed cost per eligible window, including stated exclusions | Obtain actual qualified repair/replacement bids for each site | US$120–200 target excluding stabilization, repairs, testing, taxes and disposal |
| Air leakage or heating energy | No demonstrated improvement; EPDM is an edge shield | No energy-saving claim; verify drainage and condensation do not worsen |

These are development gates rather than certified product ratings. Paired windows must have documented paint condition, cleaning history, travel, humidity and sampling area; report mass per cycle block and surface loading separately. A result confined to the trough cannot establish reduced occupant exposure.

## Deployment Scenarios

- **Older rental housing:** A manager screens 100 painted double-hung windows, excludes unsuitable units, and offers qualified lead-safe repairs as the control. If all 100 qualify, the installation target is US$12,000–20,000 before the listed exclusions; the 12-month comparison must measure floor dust as well as trough dust.
- **School or childcare retrofit:** Inspect 20 eligible classroom windows during a scheduled lead-safe maintenance closure. At target pricing, installation would be US$2,400–4,000 before testing and containment extras. Occupancy resumes only under applicable clearance rules; a cassette cannot substitute for whole-room dust control or window fall protection.
- **Heritage building:** Trial 10 reversible fits only after preservation and egress review; installation target US$1,200–2,000 before specialized labor and testing. Reject any sash requiring paint removal or a clamp that marks fragile joinery; use established professional remediation instead.

## Risks & Mitigations

| Failure mode | Control and evidence gate |
| --- | --- |
| Hidden loose lead paint or installation-generated dust | Reject unstable surfaces; use qualified lead-safe containment, documented cleaning and post-work clearance as locally required. |
| Shoe wear, clamp creep or a detached lip | Cycle and humidity-condition each profile; inspect at service, stop use on looseness and verify floor/airborne dust as well as trough mass. |
| Jammed sash, pinched fingers or lost emergency escape | Check opening force and clear opening before and after installation; prohibit use where escape, child-safety or accessibility requirements cannot be maintained. |
| Water trapping, mold or blocked ventilation | Retain weep drainage, inspect condensation and keep a separate compliant ventilation strategy; do not mistake tighter contact for a weather rating. |
| Fragile frames, nonstandard balances and warranty conflicts | Use a profile-specific fit gauge and owner/manufacturer approval; reject rather than drill, sand or force-fit. |
| Dust released at replacement or disposal | Only qualified workers open/remove the closed insert inside containment; seal, label and dispose under local rules. |

No lead-safe, child-safety, fire-egress or durability certification has been obtained. A laboratory dust reduction would not by itself establish lower blood-lead levels.

## Vision for 2050

If controlled trials show a genuine reduction in room-side lead loading, maintenance teams could specify a reversible wear interface for a *subset* of old double-hung windows while replacing or remediating the rest. Standardized profile gauges, replaceable shoes and contained collection would make repeated professional inspection more predictable than ad hoc friction repairs. The goal is fewer newly abraded lead particles at one source—not a lead-safe building by default, and not a reason to delay comprehensive abatement. Deployment remains contingent on occupant-exposure evidence, code review and lifetime cost against established lead-safe alternatives.
