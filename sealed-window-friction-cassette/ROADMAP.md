# Sealed Window Friction Cassette — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · All dates, costs and gates below are plans, not achieved results. Progress requires qualified lead-safe practice and applicable local approvals; failure at any gate favors established repair or replacement.

## Concept

**Years 0–2.** Map common double-hung profiles and exclude unstable paint, obstructed balances, insufficient clearance and escape-window conflicts. Make dimensional gauges and a lead-safe installation/removal protocol. Exit only with a documented fit/rejection matrix and an independent safety review; the unknown is how many real windows can be fitted without disturbing paint.

## Prototype

**Years 2–5.** Extrude polypropylene or recycled-HDPE liners and mold acetal shoes, EPDM lips and captive closed inserts; compare against untreated matched windows and established lead-safe repair. Under matched cleaning, humidity and 1,000-cycle test blocks, seek at least 90% less interior-trough lead mass than untreated controls **without higher floor or airborne deposition**; demonstrate no painted contact, preserved egress and an inspection/replacement interval no longer than 10,000 cycles or 12 months. Exit only after independent repeat testing across several accepted profiles; no efficacy is presumed.

## Pilot

**Years 5–8.** Randomize 100 eligible windows across comparable homes to the cassette or established lead-safe repair, with independent baseline and repeated trough/floor dust wipes, airborne sampling, opening-force and egress checks, and condensation inspections over 12 months. Use qualified installers and local clearance requirements. Exit only if room-side exposure proxies and failures compare favorably with repair; blood-lead benefits require a separate appropriately designed study.

## Scale

**Years 8–15.** If the pilot passes, qualify tooling and professional service networks while collecting real installed and disposal costs; the 2038 design target at 100,000 window sets per year is US$45 ex-factory and US$120–200 installed per eligible window, excluding stabilization, repairs, testing, taxes and disposal. Exit only if recurrent maintenance and replacement can be funded, fit rejection is transparent, and field surveillance detects no egress or dust-regression pattern. The main unknown is lifetime economics versus established remedies.

## Ubiquity

**Years 15–20 and beyond, conditional.** Offer interoperable profile-specific service parts wherever independent evidence supports them; never present a cassette as general lead abatement. The hypothetical ceiling of 100,000 qualified installations is a scenario, not a market forecast. Continue 12-month or 10,000-cycle inspection (whichever comes first), audit contained disposal and retire profiles that fail the dust, safety or cost gates. Broad adoption is justified only if occupant-side evidence and local regulatory review persist through long-term use.
