# Seasonal Pond Access Barrier — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1. Status: illustrative scenario model, not measured outcomes.** The device is a concept. No claim of reduced drowning, certified compliance or proven cost-effectiveness follows from these calculations.

## Scope and method

Model candidate dry play areas near seasonal water over **one 120-day wet season**. One kit serves at most one surveyed area. A functioning area is one with an intact perimeter, working gate, named daily checker and no flood-triggered out-of-service condition. An installed kit that fails any of those tests is not counted as functioning. The model assumes one supervised child using each functioning area for two hours per day, purely to size observation effort; it does not assume the child would otherwise spend those hours near water.

`Functioning areas = kits deployed × functioning fraction`.

`Potential supervised child-hours in functioning areas = functioning areas × 120 days × 2 hours/day`.

All counts, fractions, usage hours and prices here are **scenario inputs, not observations or forecasts**. The US$180 ex-factory figure is the README's unquoted 2038 target. The extra US$120 per kit for survey/installation and US$40 for first-season inspection/parts are invented budgeting assumptions to expose excluded costs; obtain local quotations before any procurement. Land, permitting, taxes, training, site-specific footings, flood replacement and independent evaluation are excluded and may dominate the budget. For an unchanged site, a purely illustrative five-year arithmetic cost is `(180 + 120 + 5 × 40) / 5 = US$100 per kit-year`, excluding financing and replacement. Seasonal relocation would require another survey/installation and raise this figure.

## What must be measured

- Site eligibility and refusal rate; full installed and maintenance costs; user retention and reasons for propping gates open.
- Operating days from daily inspections; anchor motion, flood exposure, gate failures and emergency-access delays. The README's 90% operating-availability target is **not** the model's 60% base-case functioning fraction: the latter includes sites never installed safely and abandoned installations.
- Observed child access attempts reaching the water side per 1,000 observed child-hours, with matched usual-care and existing-barrier comparisons and independently recorded adverse events. Hours in a play area do **not** equal water-exposure hours prevented.
- Follow-up over multiple seasons, separately recording supervision behavior and displaced water access. An ethically designed, adequately powered study would be needed to estimate any injury or mortality effect; a 100-household pilot cannot do so.

## Sensitivity and boundaries

Holding the base-case kit count and observation schedule fixed, halving the functioning fraction from 60% to 30% halves functioning areas from 6,000 to 3,000 and potential observed child-hours from 1,440,000 to 720,000. Even the high case is not a prediction of lives saved: there is no defensible device-specific efficacy or local baseline drowning rate to multiply into these hours. Do not calculate cost per life or DALY averted until a controlled outcome study supplies those inputs.

For an end-of-life planning placeholder only, **assume** 8 kg HDPE and 6 kg steel per kit. The base case would purchase 80 tonnes HDPE and 60 tonnes steel, before replacement parts; actual bills of materials, recycled content, UV lifetime and local recovery rates remain unverified. Reuse serviceable modules and budget collection rather than treating recycling as guaranteed. Flood damage, wildlife entanglement, reduced supervision and exclusion of poorer households can make net impact negative; stop deployment when monitoring finds those harms.
