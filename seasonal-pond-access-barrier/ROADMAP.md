# Seasonal Pond Access Barrier — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** All milestones are proposed, not completed. A barrier supplements supervision and must pass local safety review before public use.

## Concept

Interview caregivers and water users, map dry/wet-season routes and flood levels, and compare existing barriers and supervised childcare at candidate sites. Define who checks the gate daily, who pays for repairs and when a site is ineligible. Exit only with a reviewed hazard log and feasible adult access and emergency-egress layouts; no field protection claim.

## Prototype

Fabricate several bolted HDPE/steel layouts and test opening probes, supervised climb/bypass behavior, saturated-soil anchor retention, flood-debris exposure, gate cycles and red-flag failure modes. Obtain supplier quotations including site work, and reject unsafe geometries rather than relaxing criteria. Exit only after independent child-safety, accessibility and ecological review of a prototype against applicable local rules.

## Pilot

Install only at surveyed eligible sites with consent and active supervision; an illustrative design is 100 households over one 120-day wet season, with a comparable usual-care group and a named repairer. Log observed water-side access attempts per 1,000 child-hours, operating days, gate-open time, egress failures and any adverse events. Stop on entrapment, blocked escape or reduced supervision; advance only if the preregistered safety and usability thresholds are met. This pilot cannot establish mortality benefit.

## Scale

If the pilot is safe and a larger independent study supports benefit, qualify local moulders and metal workshops and audit installation/repair crews. Seek competing quotations for the unverified US$180 ex-factory kit target at 10,000 kits/year, plus full installed and seasonal service costs. Exit only if functioning-installation rates and total cost compare favorably with existing locally compliant barriers or childcare without transferring maintenance burden to households unable to pay.

## Ubiquity

Conditional on replicated effectiveness and public procurement review, offer surveyed, repairable enclosures within local water-safety programmes rather than mandating them at unsuitable ponds. Fund annual inspections, replacement parts and end-of-life polymer collection; publicly report out-of-service sites and adverse events. Exit criterion is sustained independently audited safety and maintenance across multiple wet seasons, never kit shipments alone.
