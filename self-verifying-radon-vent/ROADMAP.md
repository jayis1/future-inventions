# Self-Verifying Radon Vent — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** All dates and figures are planning targets, not achieved performance. A qualified installer and independent radon testing remain necessary at every field stage.

## Concept (years 1–2)

Map eligible slab and basement foundations, inspect existing mitigation standards and specify gas path, pressure taps, fixed-speed failover and power-outage indication. Exit when a safety review defines measurable fault states, installation exclusions and a reproducible independent radon-test protocol.

## Prototype (years 2–3)

Build serviceable bench assemblies from code-appropriate gas-tight riser materials, a replaceable centrifugal fan, pressure/flow sensors and local controls. Inject fan stall, disconnected tap, blocked exhaust, stale indoor detector and mains/controller failure; exit only if persistent induced extraction faults are locally indicated within the proposed 60-second bench target and no single controller fault silently stops a powered fan.

## Pilot (years 4–8)

Commission up to 100 eligible homes with qualified contractors and a maintained conventional active-vent comparison group. Record pressure-field coverage, electricity and service costs, accredited radon measurements for at least three months in each warm and cold period, and combustion-safety checks. Exit only after independent analysis demonstrates acceptable indoor levels under local rules, reliable fault detection and no safety regression; publish failures as well as successes.

## Scale (years 9–15)

Pursue applicable product and installation approvals, independent replication across climates and audited repair supply chains before approaching the 10,000-unit/year cost-planning volume. Exit when multi-site results support the existing $350–600 ex-factory target without hiding accredited tests, electricity, replacements or technician labor.

## Ubiquity (years 16–20 and beyond)

If evidence warrants broader use, offer local-first verification records to housing providers with funded inspections and replacement parts; do not deploy indiscriminately to crawlspaces or unsuitable foundations. The exit condition is sustained independently measured exposure reduction and maintained fault visibility across diverse buildings, not an installation count or a claim of zero radon risk.
