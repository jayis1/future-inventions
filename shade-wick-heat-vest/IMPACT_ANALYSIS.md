# Shade-Wick Heat Vest — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1.** All quantities below are **illustrative planning scenarios, not measured outcomes or forecasts**. The baseline is breathable clothing plus existing shade, hydration, acclimatization and work/rest protections; the vest must be tested as an *addition*, not a replacement.[1] The global heat-exposure population in the main README is not the addressable market or a count of beneficiaries.

## Inputs and accounting boundary

| Variable | Low / base / high scenario assumption | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New units deployed and used in one year | 1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000; one per worker, no stockpiling | Hypothetical uptake; high equals proposed annual factory capacity, not demand |
| Utilization | 100 two-hour bouts per unit-year (200 wearing hours); same across scenarios | Planning input, subject to site weather and use logs |
| Water drawn | Up to 250 mL clean refill per bout, separate from drinking supply | Reservoir capacity; runoff/retained water measured rather than assumed evaporated |
| Dry-condition evaporation | 200 g per two-hour bout at 35°C/35% RH/1 m/s | Unverified target, not useful cooling delivered to a person |
| Garment life and price | Two years, US$12 ex-factory only at 100,000 units/year in 2038; US$3/panel set | Design/cost targets, not quotes; no low-volume price extrapolation |

Annual water [m³] = deployed units × 100 bouts × 0.25 L ÷ 1,000 L/m³. Annual wearing hours = units × 100 bouts × 2 h. If and only if the dry-condition evaporation target holds on every bout, evaporated water [m³] = units × 100 × 0.20 L ÷ 1,000; the balance may remain in the garment or run off. At 2.4 MJ/kg latent heat, the 200 g target corresponds to approximately 67 W averaged over two hours; the 250 g full-charge upper bound is about 83 W. Neither measures body cooling.

## Conditional annual scale

| Scenario | Workers equipped (maximum) | Wearing hours (potential) | Refill water (maximum, m³) | Evaporated if every bout meets dry target (m³) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 1,000 | 200,000 | 25 | 20 |
| Base | 10,000 | 2,000,000 | 250 | 200 |
| High | 100,000 | 20,000,000 | 2,500 | 2,000 |

At the high scenario only, 100,000 × US$12 = **US$1.2 million ex-factory garment spend**. At two years and 100 bouts/year, US$12 ÷ 200 = **US$0.06 garment amortization per bout** before panels, refill water, wash energy, labor, distribution, taxes and repairs. This is not an employer saving or an affordable delivered price. The number of panel replacements is unknown and must be measured.

## Health, productivity and environment

| Outcome | Quantified claim today | Evidence needed before claiming benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat injuries, core temperature, safe work time | **Zero proven avoided cases or added safe hours**; no injury-reduction rate can be inferred from latent heat | Controlled thermal and worker studies, then adequately powered prospective occupational outcome study |
| Productivity and employer payback | **No estimate**: a vest must not authorize longer work or fewer breaks | Compare matched shifts and total delivered lifecycle cost without changing safety schedule |
| Electrical demand | **0 kWh of onboard electricity per use** by design; net system energy unknown | Measure textile manufacture, laundering, water provision and avoided cooling if any |
| Water | Up to **0.25 L/bout** for the vest, additional to drinking water | Verify source and wash water, runoff and local water stress; do not displace hydration |
| Material flows | Garment and replaceable wick panels; no verified recycling or composting rate | Weigh fabric, spacer, packaging, wash loss and waste by material at end of life |

**Sensitivity:** if only 100 g evaporates per bout rather than 200 g, calculated latent transfer falls to about 33 W over two hours, and conditional annual evaporated volume in the high scenario falls from 2,000 to 1,000 m³. In still humid air it may approach zero; even full evaporation does not prove net wearer benefit. If utilization halves, the wearing-hour and refill-water rows halve at unchanged unit counts. If service life halves from two years to one year, garment amortization at 100 bouts/year doubles from US$0.06 to US$0.12 per bout, before other costs. All these sensitivities are arithmetic, not trial results.

**Decision rule:** adopt only where controlled evidence shows net benefit over shade, breathable clothing and an existing evaporative vest under site-specific conditions. Do not claim prevented heat illness, emissions savings or labor gains from production capacity or theoretical heat transfer alone.[1][2]
