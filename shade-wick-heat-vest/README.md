# Shade-Wick Heat Vest

**Author: jayis1. Status: unvalidated research concept, not certified heat-protection equipment.**

## Problem

The ILO estimates that **2.41 billion workers** are exposed to excessive heat at work and attributes approximately **22.85 million occupational injuries annually** to that exposure.[1] This is the global problem, not the number this vest could help. Farm, construction and street workers often work in sun without powered cooling; a worker who cannot stop work, access shade and drink water remains at risk even in cooling clothing. The incumbent is loose, breathable light clothing, shade and a work/rest plan, sometimes supplemented by existing cooling vests. Those protections must remain in place.[2]

## Solution

A worker fills a detachable **250 mL** reservoir with clean water and wears a loose, open-sided vest over breathable work clothes. Gravity wets narrow cellulose-cotton wicks sewn into removable panels across the upper back and sides; a washable white, reflective textile canopy stands off the wicks on a molded recycled-polypropylene spacer mesh. Open vertical air channels allow moving air to carry vapor away, while a hydrophobic drip edge and leak-visible seam keep water out of the waistband. A manual pinch valve stops flow; the reservoir can be detached and washed. No battery, app, camera, identity system or network is required. A visible fill scale only shows water remaining, **not** a safe body temperature or permission to keep working.

The initial target is under **0.6 kg dry** and under **0.9 kg filled**, in adjustable sizes; these are design goals. Replace contaminated water, wash and dry the panels daily, and inspect the seams and valve before each shift. Do not use the vest beneath impermeable protective clothing or where required PPE fit, visibility, electrical safety or food hygiene would be compromised. When humidity is high or air is still, evaporation and therefore cooling can collapse; workers must stop and follow the site's heat plan regardless of how the vest feels.

## Key Innovation

Evaporative vests and reflective garments already exist: this is a **proposed integration**, not a claim to invent evaporative cooling or to have proven superiority.[3] The research question is whether a low-water, replaceable **segmented wick + air-gap canopy** keeps the evaporating water separated from the skin and clothing while shielding the sun-facing panels, so that moisture can leave through deliberately unobstructed channels instead of turning a soaked vest into insulation. The modular fabric assembly could be cut and stitched locally, rather than requiring proprietary refrigerant packs. Both the radiant shield and spacer may reduce air flow, so the combination may fail against a plain damp cloth or an existing vest.

**Unverified test targets:** with a 250 mL charge at **35°C, 35% relative humidity and 1 m/s air speed**, evaporate at least **200 g over two hours** while showing a measured reduction in heat strain against a breathable-clothing control; record actual heat flux, core temperature, heart rate and comfort rather than equating evaporation with physiological benefit. At **35°C, 70% relative humidity and 0.2 m/s**, require no worse physiological strain than the control or reject that operating condition. The ideal latent-heat ceiling of a full 250 g charge at approximately 2.4 MJ/kg is **600 kJ**, equivalent to **about 83 W averaged over two hours** if all water evaporates; this is an energy-budget upper bound, **not** heat removed from a person. Sun, wind, humidity, garment wetting and sweat competition all change the real result.[4]

## Target Cost

**Unvalidated 2038 target at 100,000 units/year:** **US$12 ex-factory** per reusable vest: US$3 stitched cellulose-cotton panels, US$2 white canopy fabric, US$2 molded polypropylene spacer and buckles, US$2 reservoir/valve, US$3 assembly and quality checks. A US$3 replaceable wick-panel set and approximately 250 mL clean refill water per two-hour use are planning inputs, not supplier quotes. Target a **two-year garment life** with replaceable panels; at 100 uses/year this is US$0.06/use hardware amortization before panels, water, laundering, transport, taxes, wages, repairs and distribution. Local procurement must compare fully delivered cost and worker acceptance against ordinary breathable clothing, shade provision and existing cooling vests; do not shift safety costs to workers.

## Impact

At **100,000 units/year**, production could equip at most **100,000 workers/year** if one vest is issued per worker; this is capacity, not avoided injuries. Start with a randomized, independently monitored crossover trial against ordinary breathable clothing **and** a commercially available evaporative vest in dry and humid field conditions. Prespecify comparable workload, hydration, sun exposure and work/rest schedule; measure core-temperature trends under clinical oversight, heart rate, skin wetness, evaporated and leaked water, PPE interference, skin irritation, cleaning time and adverse events. If shade or work-rest changes outperform the vest, prioritize those measures. No injury or mortality reduction is claimed without a much larger prospective study.

A 10–20-year development path is bench calorimetry and wash-cycle testing, then supervised worker trials, locally manufactured variants and occupational-safety assessment. The physics is ordinary evaporation and radiation; achieving safe, affordable improvement under real workloads remains unproven. Failure modes include high humidity, blocked channels, stale water, mold, valve leaks, extra clothing insulation and an employer using the vest to deny breaks. An open-sided fit and removable panels help inspection, but cannot prevent misuse: employers must supply shade, drinking water, acclimatization and appropriate rest regardless of the device.[2] Retire cracked reservoirs, recycle polypropylene where facilities exist and compost only confirmed uncontaminated natural-fiber panels; verify dye, washing-water and textile lifecycle impacts. No military, surveillance or coercive use is intended.

Unlike this repository's [Thermoregulatory Metamaterial Habitation](../thermoregulatory-metamaterial-habitation/), a proposed building envelope, this is an individual, removable water-fed garment for outdoor work; unlike the [Radiative-Adsorption Refrigerator](../radiative-adsorption-refrigerator/), it does not refrigerate stored goods. Existing personal evaporative vests are the real performance comparator, not those unrelated inventions.
