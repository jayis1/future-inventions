# Shade-Wick Heat Vest — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Research concept; no certified protective or medical benefit is established. Phases are evidence gates, not promised launch dates. Preserve existing workplace heat controls throughout.[1]

## Concept

Build a gravity-fed 250 mL reservoir, segmented cellulose-cotton wick, washable white canopy and recycled-polypropylene spacer. On a thermal manikin, separately measure water mass balance, solar absorption, airflow and useful heat flux at 35°C/35% RH/1 m/s and 35°C/70% RH/0.2 m/s against breathable clothing and a damp cloth. Exit only if the dry-condition ≥200 g/two-hour evaporation target is reproducible without leaking onto clothing and humid-condition insulation does not worsen manikin heat load.

## Prototype

Make adjustable garments targeting <0.6 kg dry and <0.9 kg filled; run wash, seam, valve and blocked-vent failure tests. With independent safety review and appropriate human-subject oversight, conduct a small 10–20-person crossover feasibility study against breathable clothing and a commercial evaporative vest, matching workload, solar exposure, hydration and rest. Exit only if monitored physiological strain is not worse than controls, wearers can move safely in their actual PPE, and stop criteria are workable; a small study cannot establish injury reduction.

## Pilot

At one consenting outdoor-work site over a full hot season, provide separate drinking and refill water, shade, rest breaks, cleaning and spare panels at employer expense. Prespecify temperature/humidity limits, adverse-event reporting, fit, laundry, water use and worker acceptance; compare heat-strain measures with usual practice without extending shifts. Exit only on independently reviewed safety and usability data in the stated climate envelope.

## Scale

Obtain supplier quotes and durability data for a two-year garment life and US$3 replacement wick set. Test whether the 2038 **US$12 ex-factory target at 100,000 units/year** is achievable without cutting quality or transferring laundering and refill costs to workers. Exit only if audited manufacturing, delivered cost, material recovery and site-specific benefit justify purchase over simpler controls.

## Ubiquity

If replicated controlled studies and occupational procurement reviews demonstrate benefit, publish an open test protocol and practical climate/PPE exclusion labels for locally maintainable variants. Exit is repeatable evidence and sustained safe use across multiple employers—not an arbitrary sales quota. Maintain shade, hydration, acclimatization and rest as primary protections even if vests become common.[1]
