# Silica Wetwork Recovery Bench

**Author: jayis1. Status: research concept, not a certified silica exposure control.**

## Problem

Millions of workers worldwide are exposed to respirable crystalline silica, and silicosis is irreversible; small stone-fabrication workplaces face particular exposure risks.[3] A review of engineered-stone fabrication found that wet tools alone can still leave excessive airborne silica exposure.[2] Drying slurry can also create secondary dust. A bench that wets a cut but lets contaminated runoff escape is an incomplete control.

## Solution

A repairable **sheet-wetting and slurry-capture workbench** for small, stationary stone finishing tasks. A 12 V low-voltage pump feeds clean water from a covered 30 L HDPE reservoir through a perforated stainless-steel rail across the work face. A removable flexible polyurethane splash skirt and sloped stainless tray direct runoff into a sealed settling pail; a mesh screen retains chips. The pail is exchanged and the silica-bearing slurry is handled wet by trained staff under a workplace disposal plan. A visible rotameter and low-flow float switch flag loss of wetting; they do **not** measure airborne silica or certify that work is safe. The bench leaves the tool's original guards intact.

This is for **bench-supported finishing**, not hand-held overhead cutting, dry machining, large slabs, or explosive/electrical environments. Use only with electrically suitable wet-rated tools, appropriate ground-fault protection, local exhaust where required, workplace respiratory protection, and qualified safety oversight. Do not retrofit water into a dry-only tool. Site-specific exposure assessment and applicable regulatory controls remain mandatory.[1][2]

## Key Innovation

Wet cutting, sheet-flow wetting, shrouds and settling tanks already exist. The proposed advance is a **locally repairable, single-pass sheet-flow bench that keeps its contaminated water in a closed-handling path and makes flow failure obvious at the point of work**. Unlike this repository's [Particulate Depuration Metamaterial Facade](../particulate-depuration-metamaterial-facade/), it targets dust at a stone-work source rather than outdoor urban air; unlike the [Straw Board Micropress](../straw-board-micropress/), it is an exposure-control research platform rather than a board-making process. Its value over a commercial wet workstation plus local exhaust must be demonstrated, not assumed.

A 10–20 year development horizon is for validated modular fitment, inexpensive service, and exposure qualification, not discovery of new physics. The key falsifiable hypothesis is that integrated wet capture and maintenance checks lower full-shift worker exposure and secondary dried-slurry dust versus an otherwise comparable maintained wet-tool station.

## Target Cost

**Unvalidated ex-factory goal: US$650 per bench at 10,000 units/year in 2038.** Planning bill of materials: stainless tray and frame $220; pump, 30 L HDPE reservoir, hose and rail $105; removable skirt and screen $65; sealed settling pail and lid $55; low-flow indicator/switch and low-voltage supply $75; assembly, electrical tests and leak tests $130. These sum to $650. Excludes wet-rated tools, GFCI/RCD installation, local exhaust, fitment, personal protection, industrial-hygiene sampling, labour, taxes, sludge disposal and replacement parts. A prototype will likely cost more; supplier quotes and field servicing must test affordability. No disposable proprietary cartridge is required.

## Impact

**Pilot targets, not measured results:** sustain an adjustable **0.5 L/min** sheet flow across a specified small workpiece (30 L/hour; a 30 L reservoir lasts at most one hour before refill), capture **at least 90% of discharged liquid by measured volume** into the pail under the defined bench task, and correctly flag every imposed no-flow fault in a 100-cycle bench test. Liquid capture is not respirable-dust capture. Test a 20-workshop, 12-month paired pilot using personal full-shift respirable crystalline silica samples, task-based aerosol measurements, runoff mass balance and dried-residue inspection; compare the bench to existing compliant wet methods and wet-plus-exhaust controls. Do not claim a percentage reduction in disease, compliance, or the OSHA limit without sampling and independent assessment.[1][2]

**Failure and maintenance:** A blocked rail, low water, damaged skirt, overflow, pump failure or loss of electrical isolation requires stopping the task, isolating power and repairing the fault; a flow indicator cannot detect every badly placed jet. Keep pails lidded and wet; never sweep or blow dried residue. Settled water is **not automatically reusable**: fine silica can remain suspended, so reuse would require separate filtration, microbial/electrical suitability checks and measurement of nozzle fouling. Qualified staff decide whether the slurry is regulated waste locally. At end of life, separate stainless steel, HDPE, pump and electronics for repair/recycling and dispose of residues under local rules. Worker consultation and independent safety review precede deployment.
