# Silica Wetwork Recovery Bench — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** All gates are proposed; none has been passed. See [README](README.md) for the concept and [impact analysis](IMPACT_ANALYSIS.md) for scenario assumptions.

## Concept

Specify the supported workpiece and wet-rated tool envelope, runoff path, pail exchange procedure and baseline wet-tool comparator with workers and an industrial hygienist. Exit only after electrical, splash, aerosol, maintenance and waste-handling hazards have a documented review and a test plan; reject applications requiring dry-only tools.

## Prototype

Build a serviceable sheet-flow and collection assembly. Under a defined small-workpiece task, demonstrate adjustable 0.5 L/min coverage, at least 90% liquid collection by metered balance, and indication in 100/100 induced no-flow faults; separately test partial clogging, pail overflow, splash, electrical isolation and wet cleaning. Exit requires measured results, independent safety review and a credible servicing cost—not an exposure-compliance claim.

## Pilot

Run a proposed 20-workshop, 12-month paired comparison with maintained wet-tool stations and, where relevant, wet-plus-exhaust controls. Collect personal full-shift respirable-silica samples, task aerosol and cleaning exposure, runoff volumes, waste characterization, maintenance events and worker feedback. Exit only if an independent reviewer finds a reproducible safety and usability benefit without unacceptable exposure or runoff; meet applicable local requirements before routine deployment.

## Scale

If the pilot supports it, qualify vendors and installers, document spare parts and pail collection, and repeat exposure and waste reviews across workpiece types. Test the US$650 ex-factory goal at 10,000 units/year in 2038 against actual supplier quotes, excluding installation, sampling and disposal. Exit when independent field data and local compliance pathways remain credible at expanded sites; otherwise redesign or stop.

## Ubiquity

By 2050, a possible standard practice is maintainable source wetting with documented slurry containment and exposure assessment in suitable small finishing shops. Continue periodic audits, repairs, worker training and locally approved waste management; withdraw any installation that fails coverage or sampling. Widespread adoption is conditional on measured advantages over existing maintained controls, not a guaranteed milestone.
