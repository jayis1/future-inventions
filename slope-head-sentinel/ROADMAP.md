# Slope-Head Sentinel — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Proposed gates, not completed milestones. This mechanical positive-head indicator supplements, rather than replaces, qualified geotechnical assessment and official warnings.

## Concept (years 0–2)

Map candidate shallow rain-triggered failure modes with local geotechnical teams and define the screened interval, hydraulic reference, safe viewing point and named decision-maker for each proposed site. Exit only when bench drawings, hazard analysis, accessible flag language and a site-selection protocol are independently reviewed; reject any site where positive head is not a relevant local signal.

## Prototype (years 0–2)

Build screened HDPE/float/pawl prototypes and compare both trip thresholds with a reference water column through wet/dry, temperature and blocked-screen tests. Gate: head agreement within a proposed 5 cm at the trip marks, at least 95% successful releases over 1,000 cycles per unit, and documented failure behavior; redesign if clogging or trapped air creates an unobservable false-negative mode.

## Pilot (years 3–5)

Instrument 40 professionally assessed slopes serving approximately 10,000 residents over two wet seasons, initially as **non-alerting** comparisons with reference standpipes, local rainfall data and existing official procedures. Gate: at least 90% of units pass monthly field checks, complete logs of all flags and known failures/near misses, and independent assessment of missed events and false alerts; suspend human-facing use following any dangerous unflagged event until investigated.

## Scale (years 5–10)

Only if prospective event evidence and authority-approved thresholds support it, run supervised drills and procure replaceable components and trained service capacity. Seek an unverified US$65 ex-factory target at 10,000 units/year; obtain real installed and recurring-cost quotes instead of treating the illustrative US$150–400/site services allowance as a guarantee. Gate: funded inspections, an owned alert/evacuation chain and independent review at each deployment, otherwise stop.

## Ubiquity (years 10–20)

Make locally appropriate indicators an optional line item in slope and route maintenance, not a universal mandate. Aim for repeatable training, spare-part recovery, annual trip tests, post-storm inspections and publicly auditable outcomes; expand only to sites with demonstrated head–hazard correlation and continuing response capacity. By 2050, success would mean fewer unobserved local changes and faster informed decisions—not a claim that landslides can be predicted or eliminated.
