# Smoke-Refuge Witness Cassette

**Author: jayis1** · **Category: Disaster Resilience / Clean Air** · **Status: research concept (TRL 2; untested)**

## Problem

Wildfire smoke can enter homes while people shelter indoors. A closed room with a correctly sized portable air cleaner can reduce particle exposure, but a fan that still spins behind a clogged or badly seated filter can create false confidence. EPA advises a cleaner-air room, appropriately sized filtration, frequent filter checks, and relocation if the room cannot stay cool or smoke continues to enter.[1][2] Smoke episodes can coincide with power loss; a maintenance signal and bounded battery reserve should be understandable without a phone or network.

## Solution

A **repairable recirculating room-air cassette** sits entirely inside a closable room. Two common-size, replaceable **MERV 13 pleated mechanical filters** fit into gasketed polypropylene/steel frames upstream of a guarded, fused 24 V brushless centrifugal blower. A spring-loaded seating tab prevents the blower from starting unless both frames latch. A differential-pressure tap across the filters and a downstream airflow flap give *separate* local, mechanical indications of resistance and actual flow. The flap must reach a marked minimum-flow region at the selected speed; a latched red service flag is also raised by a blocked-filter pressure threshold or loss of power. These indicators are **fault witnesses, not PM₂.₅ sensors or proof of clean-air delivery**. A serviceable LiFePO₄ battery and protected mains charger provide a short outage reserve. No outdoor intake, UV lamp, ionizer, network connection or occupant tracking is used.

The room still needs safe temperature and appropriate ventilation under local building and health guidance. Keep doors and windows closed only while safe; never block exits or legally required ventilation. If the room overheats, smoke enters, the cassette flags a fault, or local officials call for evacuation, use a safer shelter or follow their instructions.[1]

## Key Innovation

Mechanical filtration, DIY fan-filter cleaners and portable air cleaners already exist.[1][2] The **proposed, testable integration** is a locally repairable cassette that couples positive filter seating to a *two-variable, no-cloud service witness*: pressure rise detects loading, while low measured airflow catches a weak fan or obstructed grille. Neither reading alone establishes particle-removal efficiency. A paired trial must compare post-service particle CADR and undetected-fault frequency with ordinary low-cost cleaners; superiority and patent novelty are **not established**. Unlike the repository's outdoor [particulate facade](../particulate-depuration-metamaterial-facade/) or [UV air disinfector](../spectral-guard-air-disinfector/), this captures indoor smoke particles during a temporary shelter event, without claiming outdoor remediation or pathogen inactivation.

## Target Cost

**Unquoted 2038 planning target in USD at 50,000 units/year:** US$210 ex-factory: blower/driver US$32; two framed filters/gaskets US$38; recyclable housing and grille US$32; pressure/flow witness and latches US$22; 160 Wh protected LiFePO₄ pack/charger US$66; assembly and bench test US$20. Allow US$30–70 for distribution and setup and US$30–60/year for filters under a hypothetical smoke-season duty cycle; actual loading may require replacement within days or weeks.[1] Battery service, taxes, electrical certification, collection and local labor are excluded. All prices and service intervals are estimates, not supplier quotes or lifetime guarantees; the replaceable pack and soiled filters have separate collection streams.

## Impact

**Unmeasured design targets:** for a 40 m³, well-mixed reference room (about 16 m² floor area at 2.5 m ceiling), independently verify **≥200 m³/h smoke-particle CADR** at full speed with fresh, correctly installed filters, at a proposed **300 m³/h physical airflow**; this requires at least two-thirds effective smoke-particle removal across the assembled unit. Set the red service flag at physical airflow below 240 m³/h, but do not equate that flow threshold to a guaranteed CADR. At 200 m³/h CADR, the idealized equivalent clean-air rate is 5 h⁻¹, *not* a prediction of indoor PM₂.₅ reduction: leakage, room mixing, filter age, indoor sources and outdoor concentration change the result. The target CADR is roughly 118 cfm, near EPA's sizing guidance for this room's ~172 ft² footprint (at least two-thirds the floor area in smoke CADR cfm).[2] Target ≤35 W while running, ≤55 dBA at 1 m in the reference room, and ≥4 hours on a nominal 160 Wh battery with aged-pack and charger losses accounted for in the test. These co-targets may conflict and require independent testing; no prototype, certification, measured protection or health outcome is claimed.

At most, 10,000 deployed units could provision 10,000 *suitable* rooms, not 10,000 verified safe shelters. A 10–20 year path is bench CADR/pressure testing, dust-loading and fault-injection trials, electrical/fire qualification, and matched household studies across smoke and heat conditions. A 2050 vision is locally serviceable smoke-refuge equipment available through community libraries and clinics, conditional on those trials, affordable replacement filters and safe rooms.

## Operating limits, test and safe failure

- **Flow and energy:** 300 m³/h physical airflow is 0.0833 m³/s. At an assumed 100 Pa total pressure rise with two large-area clean filters, air power is about 8.3 W; achieving ≤35 W electrical requires ≥24% overall blower/driver efficiency at that point, including installation losses. Filter loading raises pressure loss and may break the power/flow targets; both need measurement. A 160 Wh nominal pack at 35 W has only 4.6 h *ideal* runtime; the ≥4 h requirement needs ≥140 Wh usable at end-of-life test condition and must be rejected if aged-pack trials fail. The battery is not a cooling or household-power supply.
- **Acceptance trial:** in a 40 m³ chamber, measure standardized smoke-particle CADR, actual volumetric flow, power, acoustic level and multi-point particle concentration with clean, partly loaded and replacement filters. Repeat after reassembly by non-specialist users, with deliberate unseated gasket, taped-over inlet, weak fan and sensor-port blockage. Compare false-normal states against a conventional portable cleaner. Establish the service thresholds only from these trials; neither the pressure tap nor the flap is a substitute for a calibrated CADR test.
- **Power loss / blocked flow / seal failure:** red flag on battery exhaustion or failed airflow; latch inhibit on missing frames. A leaking filter gasket can preserve airflow yet pass particles, so require an independent post-service fit/leak check and periodic room-particle verification where instrumentation exists. If tests find undetectable dangerous bypass, reject the design rather than claim the witness makes it safe. No automatic claim of room safety is ever displayed.
- **Bad parts / humidity / heat / misuse:** keyed, dimension-checked replacement frames and clear airflow-arrow markings; test counterfeit and reversed filters, wet media, dust, rain ingress and motor stalls. Thermal fuse, guarded grille, fused battery and charger protection must pass applicable electrical/fire standards before occupancy use. Keep unit dry, away from children and combustible materials; do not operate with damaged wiring or saturated filters. Clean the pre-grille and check flags daily during an event; replace filters when flagged, visibly soiled or smoky-smelling.[1]
- **What it cannot do:** particle filters do **not** establish protection from carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, all smoke gases, heat or oxygen depletion. Avoid indoor combustion, retain working CO/smoke alarms where required, and do not use the device to justify sealing a room indefinitely or staying near an active fire. A flagged or unverified unit is *out of service*; relocate or use a proven alternative rather than bypassing the interlock.[1][2]
- **Lifecycle:** choose screwed, rather than glued, steel/polypropylene parts; offer locally replaceable standard-size filters and a separately recoverable LiFePO₄ battery. Treat smoke-loaded filters as locally regulated solid waste, not as compost; verify recycling routes rather than promise zero waste.
