# Snakebite Venom Triage Strip — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1.** Scenario model, not observed benefit or a procurement forecast. The device is an unvalidated research concept; a negative result cannot rule out envenoming or delay care.

## Baseline and boundary

WHO estimates 1.8–2.7 million envenomings and 81,000–138,000 deaths annually worldwide.[1] These worldwide burdens are context, **not** the denominator for a particular regional antigen panel. Model one hypothetical region for one year with 100,000 potentially testable suspected-bite encounters already reaching participating facilities; this denominator is an assumption, not a measured regional caseload. A locally relevant two-group panel cannot cover every venom or every care pathway. The model does not infer deaths, disability-adjusted life years, antivenom vials or adverse reactions prevented.

## Transparent one-year scenarios

All inputs below are **illustrative assumptions**, not measured prevalence, uptake, sensitivity or effectiveness. “Actionable” means a *valid* result whose antigen group could potentially distinguish locally stocked products; it does not imply a correct result or a treatment change. The valid-result fraction is a planning assumption, distinct from clinical accuracy.

| Scenario | Facility encounters offered test | Fraction tested | Fraction valid | Fraction of valid results potentially actionable | Planning delivered cost/test |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 100,000 | 20% | 90% | 10% | US$2 |
| Base | 100,000 | 50% | 95% | 20% | US$3 |
| High | 100,000 | 80% | 98% | 30% | US$4 |

Formulas, with the denominator explicit:

- Tests used = 100,000 facility encounters × fraction tested: **20,000 / 50,000 / 80,000** (low / base / high).
- Valid results = tests used × fraction valid: **18,000 / 47,500 / 78,400**. Invalid/indeterminate results = tests used − valid results: **2,000 / 2,500 / 1,600**; those patients follow the ordinary clinical pathway, not a “negative” pathway.
- Potentially actionable results = valid results × assumed actionable fraction: **1,800 / 9,500 / 23,520**. These are *upper opportunity counts* before false positives, false negatives, lack of antivenom, clinician decisions and referral delays; they are not additional treated patients.
- Delivered strip-program spend = tests used × assumed per-test cost: **US$40,000 / US$150,000 / US$320,000**. These cost choices span the README's unverified US$1.50–2.50 factory and US$0.50–1.50 distribution/training/quality/disposal goals, but exclude clinical staff, referrals, treatment, registration and validation. The three rows are sensitivity cases, not statistical confidence bounds.

## What would demonstrate benefit

In a predeclared, regulator-authorized trial, compare strip-assisted care with usual care, reporting the count of independently adjudicated appropriate antivenom selections and the distribution of time to effective treatment for **all randomized encounters**, not just interpretable strips. Disaggregate by immunotype, clinic, specimen quality and post-bite sampling time. Audit false reassurance and inappropriate treatment from false-negative or false-positive readings, and report invalids separately. A model of deaths avoided would additionally require measured causal effects on timely effective treatment, local treatment availability, severity and mortality; none is available for this concept. Do not count avoided vials or costs without local product-use and price data, and do not double-count reassigned patients as additional patients treated.

Equity and implementation matter: include training, storage failures, replacement stock, quality assurance, controlled clinical-waste disposal and access to referral/antivenom in a future full budget. A card that identifies an antigen correctly but delays transport or has no corresponding stocked antivenom can produce no benefit or cause harm. Stop or redesign if prospective evidence shows that risk.
