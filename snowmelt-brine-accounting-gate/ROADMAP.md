# Snowmelt Brine Accounting Gate — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Research concept; each phase requires a documented stop/go decision by the site operator and permitting authority. See [technical blueprint](README.md).

## 1. Concept

Map one lined snow yard's flow, existing permitted containment, snow chemistry and winter access; baseline inlet and outlet chloride with laboratory samples and document where every captured batch can lawfully go. Exit only with a hydraulic review, an identified operator and a plausible mass-balance boundary; reject sites without overflow capacity or lawful disposal.

## 2. Prototype

Bench-build the 1 m³ HDPE tank, accessible screen, calibrated flow/conductivity train and locally interlocked 24 V valve. At 0–5 °C and up to 0.3 L/s, witness sensor drift, ice, blocked inlet, power loss, valve seizure and full-tank tests; proceed only if each routes safely to the existing containment path and recorded samples reconcile within the predeclared ±15% pilot goal.

## 3. Pilot

With permits and a trained operator, run a controlled yard against a pre-installation or matched-yard baseline for at least two melt seasons. Test the ≥60% controlled-inlet chloride-capture target with flow-weighted lab assays, separate bypass and availability reporting, contaminant vetoes and a complete disposal/reuse ledger. Stop reuse immediately on quality, containment or accounting failure; publish uncertainty and independent audit results before expanding.

## 4. Scale

Trial multiple already-lined and permitted yards with distinct runoff chemistries and storage arrangements. Obtain supplier quotes and full costs for labor, sampling, frost protection, collection, transport and disposal; compare source-side salt reduction against the gate. Expand only if site-specific hydraulic safety, permits and verifiable net benefit survive independent review, not merely because tank capture is high.

## 5. Ubiquity

By 2050, if repeatable evidence warrants it, make winter-runoff provenance and destination ledgers a standard option for qualified yards, with auditable calibration, interoperable records and trained servicing. Retire or redesign installations whose receiving-water results, lifecycle burdens or salt-purchase inventories fail to demonstrate a net improvement; universal rollout is a conditional vision, not a forecast.
