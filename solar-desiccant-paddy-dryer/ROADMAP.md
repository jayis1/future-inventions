# Solar-Desiccant Paddy Dryer — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1.** Research milestones, not guaranteed dates or approved food-processing claims. See [README.md](README.md) for the design case and sources.

## Phase 1 — Concept

Build a mass, psychrometric and heat-balance model for 100 kg paddy at 18% → 14% wet-basis moisture; bench-test 18 kg of contained zeolite across realistic 25–35 °C, up to 85% RH air. Exit only when measured usable uptake, filter bypass, pressure drop and regeneration heat reconcile with the 4.65 kg water-removal balance, and a local food-safety reviewer accepts the test plan.

## Phase 2 — Prototype

Instrument one tray, two cassettes and an isolated solar/backup dock; log airflow, grain profiles, bead mass, fan electricity and delivered thermal energy across at least 100 regeneration cycles. Exit only after reference moisture tests find no unsafe wet pockets, no food-side bead carryover is detected under an agreed laboratory limit, and fault drills prove safe intake lockout and prompt wet-grain handoff.

## Phase 3 — Pilot

Run matched trials over two harvest seasons at a cooperative, comparing a maintained solar bubble dryer and heated-air dryer with equivalent grain lots and recorded weather. Independent reviewers assess head-rice yield, mycotoxins, labor, full-cycle energy and cost per tonne of safely dried grain; exit only if the humid-hour benefit survives those comparisons and local approvals are obtained.

## Phase 4 — Scale

If the pilot passes, standardize food-contact trays, replaceable barriers, trained service procedures and traceable cassette inspection; test multiple sites with different humidity and grid reliability. Exit when audits reproduce safety and cost results, spare parts and backup heat are available locally, and life-cycle impacts include collector fabrication and bead replacement.

## Phase 5 — Ubiquity

Offer the design only where independent evidence shows an advantage over maintained alternatives; publish comparable lot-level moisture and energy protocols for local operators. The exit gate is sustained, independently audited safe output and affordable service across varied sites—not a target number of installations or universal adoption.
