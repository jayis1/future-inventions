# Solar-Swing Humidity Vent — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1** · Illustrative ten-year adoption cases, **not forecasts or measured outcomes**. All device performance is a target. Functional unit: one installed unit in one eligible, repaired room during one 120-day wet season; yearly figures below describe the tenth year's installed fleet, not cumulative ten-year benefits.

## Boundary and assumptions

- Baseline: an equal-flow conventional vent delivering the same 20 m³/h for eight hours on operating days. The comparison is **water removed from incoming air**, not total building moisture removed. Baseline desiccant removal = zero; independent ventilation and source repairs must still be provided.
- Eligible conditions: inlet 30 °C/75% RH, four hours at 600 W/m² on the 1 m² collector, working 0.10 kg/kg gel swing for two cycles, and 0.20 kg captured per productive day. No output is assigned to nonproductive days. Suitability prevalence, ambient conditions and duty cycle are unmeasured.
- Auxiliary electricity: conservative ceiling 48 Wh per productive day from PV, battery or grid; no quantified grid saving because the power source and counterfactual differ. This table excludes any bypass fan use on nonproductive days, which must be measured separately to preserve required ventilation. Useful solar heat target ≥1.5 MJ per productive day. Solar collection, PV production and battery round-trip losses require measurement.
- Ex-factory budget US$95/unit at 100,000/year is unvalidated. Installation estimate US$35–80/unit; service allowance US$8–15/year excludes labor and replacement battery. Taxes, distribution and capital for repairs are excluded. These are not consumer quotes.
- Service life: ten years is a scenario assumption, contingent on bed, damper, seals, PV and battery replacement testing; materials, battery, disposal and transport emissions are **not** modeled.

## Sensitivities and limits

If measured throughput is only 0.10 kg per productive day, halve the water quantities; if all units meet 0.20 kg/day but only 30 productive days occur, a 10,000-unit fleet transfers just 60 tonnes. If incoming air is already dry, there is little water to remove; if solar heat cannot regenerate the beds, productive days fall. Installed units are *not* equivalent to rooms achieving acceptable RH, and moisture may re-enter through leakage or exhaust recirculation.

For a 10,000-unit base fleet, installation alone adds an estimated US$350,000–800,000 up front; annual service allowance would be US$80,000–150,000 before excluded labor or battery costs. These budgets do not establish affordability or payback. Compare measured room-level humidity and full installed lifecycle costs against an ordinary vent, appropriate source repair and a powered dehumidifier before any cost-effectiveness claim.

No avoided illness, mold-remediation cost, carbon emissions or electricity savings are quantified: current evidence does not establish causality or a like-for-like energy boundary. Disposal and manufacturing impacts could outweigh operational advantages at small moisture loads. A controlled pilot must establish net room benefit and a lifecycle assessment must establish any environmental benefit before these projections are used in procurement.
