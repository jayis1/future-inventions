# Roadmap — Stabilized Earth Floor Sealing Kit

**Author: jayis1**
**Status:** Conditional development plan, not a deployment schedule. See [SPECIFICATION.md](./SPECIFICATION.md) for test gates.

## Concept

Define the site exclusion card (flooding, damp, contaminated soil, unstable base), source three distinct local soils and characterize graded aggregate, lime and calcined-clay reactivity. Model the 20 mm topping at 32 kg dry mix/m² and obtain local material and installed-cost quotes. **Gate:** a documented safe-source and moisture-screen protocol and at least one trialable mix per accepted soil class; otherwise redesign or reject the site.

## Prototype

Build paired bare-earth, kit and locally available cement-floor bays under the same moisture and traffic conditions. Measure density, shrinkage, wet abrasion, dust, vapor behavior, wet slip, indoor mixing exposure and patchability after at least seven days of protected cure. **Gate:** no connected cracks over 1 mm, no persistent damp or unsafe exposure, and lower abrasion and dust than the paired bare-earth control; set numeric durability thresholds from the measured baseline, not in advance.

## Pilot

Install 100 voluntary, dry-site rooms with trained local masons and independent inspection. Monitor moisture, dust, wear, wet cleaning, user acceptance and repair frequency over two wet seasons; compare against paired untreated and local cement floors. Health endpoints, if studied with consent and appropriate design, are exploratory and cannot be inferred from Mexico's cement-floor study.[2] **Gate:** no safety stop, no widespread softening, and no site needing patches on more than 10% of floor area annually; publish failures as well as successes.

## Scale

Only after pilot evidence, qualify local mineral suppliers and train independent installers in the moisture rejection rule, batch records, PPE, curing and repairs. Test whether the US$5–8/m² *materials-only* target at 10,000 kits/year is achievable alongside acceptable installed cost, local building approval and a regional lifecycle assessment. **Gate:** repeatable performance across soil sources, viable supply economics and no unresolved indoor-air or moisture safety finding; otherwise remain a limited pilot product.

## Ubiquity

If multi-region follow-up supports the ten-year service hypothesis, publish open acceptance tests and propose inclusion as one non-structural dry-room option in local housing and humanitarian guidance. Maintain a repair supply chain and retraining pathway; compare the full life-cycle and service cost against improved-earth floors already on the market, not just bare dirt. **Gate:** independent replication, resident choice, permitted installation and durable maintenance capacity. Flooded or structural sites remain out of scope even in a mature rollout.
