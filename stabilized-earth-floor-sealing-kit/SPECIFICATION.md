# Engineering Specification — Stabilized Earth Floor Sealing Kit

**Author: jayis1**

**Status:** Concept; all performance and price figures below are unverified design targets.

## Materials and mechanism

| Component | Preliminary choice | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Graded mineral fraction | Clean, tested local soil plus 0–4 mm washed sand; reject organic-rich or contaminated feed | Dense, compactable aggregate skeleton |
| Binder | By dry mass of topping, 7.5% hydrated building lime (Ca(OH)₂) and 7.5% calcined kaolinitic clay (approximately 650–850°C processing); optimize per local clay reactivity | Pozzolanic calcium-silicate/aluminate hydrate formation; some carbonation at exposed surfaces |
| Water | Trial mix at approximately 8–12% of dry mix mass, adjusted to compaction optimum | Enables packing and binder reaction; excess water causes shrinkage |
| Finish | Thin limewash, refreshed locally | Replaceable sacrificial cleanable surface; no impermeable film |
| Tool | Steel or timber hand roller, reusable rails, tamping block, sieve and measuring buckets | Repeatable 20 mm compacted layer |

These are trial proportions, not a universal recipe. Use occupational dust controls; wet-mix where possible. Calcining clay consumes energy and lime releases process CO₂; do not claim carbon neutrality without a regional life-cycle assessment. No heavy-metal or asbestos-bearing aggregate may be used.

## Mass and time budget

At a **target dry density of 1,600 kg/m³**, a 1 m² floor at 20 mm thickness needs **32 kg of dry mix**: approximately **27.2 kg graded mineral fraction**, **2.4 kg hydrated lime**, and **2.4 kg calcined clay**. Initial mixing water of roughly **2.6–3.8 kg/m²** follows the 8–12% trial band; it must escape or become chemically bound during curing, not be sealed behind an impermeable coating. These are input quantities, not guaranteed final moisture or strength. For a 12 m² room the target dry mix is 384 kg; check floor bearing and door clearance before use.

Protect from direct sun and rain and maintain moderate moisture for **at least seven days**, then verify surface hardness and moisture before routine wet cleaning. Cold, humid conditions can extend curing substantially. Unlike cement concrete, lime–pozzolan strength develops slowly; do not promise 48-hour occupancy or waterproofing.

## Acceptance tests (proposed)

- Moisture gate: use a simple overnight sealed-patch test as a *screen*, followed by a competent dampness assessment where indicated. Any visible condensation, pooling, capillary rise, or seasonal flood history rejects this kit until the underlying cause is corrected. A negative patch is not proof against seasonal moisture.
- Pilot mixes: sieve and compact local soil variants; measure density, shrinkage and cracking after cure. Reject a mix with connected cracks wider than 1 mm or debonding under ordinary foot traffic.
- Durability target: at least **two wet seasons** without widespread softening, with standardized wet-abrasion mass loss and dust loading lower than a paired bare-earth control. Set numeric abrasion thresholds only after a baseline study; compare with a locally sourced cement-floor control as well.
- Indoor safety: test source soil for site-relevant contaminants, verify dry handling dust exposure, and confirm no significant indoor particulate increase after abrasion. Stop pilot on persistent damp, mold, excessive dust, or burn/irritation complaints.
- Repairs: excise a damaged patch, recompact compatible wet mix, repeat protected cure. If repeated patching exceeds 10% of surface per year, treat the design as failed at that site.

## Costs and service

The README's US$5–8/m² at 10,000 kits/year in 2035 is a **target material-cost envelope**, not a measured BOM. It presumes accessible local soil/sand and shared reusable tools. Labor, drainage, screening tests, building-code approval, transport, lime/clay production emissions, and finance are excluded. The ten-year service life is an explicit field-test hypothesis. Calcined-clay availability and lime prices may invalidate the envelope; compare installed cost and maintenance against locally available concrete and improved-earth floors before adoption.

## Development and safeguards

Bench phase: characterize at least three local soils and binder sources for reactivity, shrinkage, abrasion and water sensitivity. Controlled phase: build side-by-side test bays, then 100 voluntary pilot rooms with independent moisture and dust measurements over two wet seasons. Deployment within 10–20 years requires repeatable local-source quality control, demonstrated indoor-air safety, acceptable installed cost, and approval under applicable local building rules. There is no remote monitoring; residents own the floor and the tools. At end of life, broken topping is mineral rubble: reuse as fill only after contaminant testing and where locally permitted, never assume automatic recyclability.
