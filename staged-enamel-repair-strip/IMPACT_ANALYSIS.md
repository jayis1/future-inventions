# Staged Enamel Repair Strip — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1.** Scenario model, not an efficacy claim or forecast. Only clinician-confirmed, non-cavitated enamel lesions qualify; the WHO oral-disease burden is **not** an eligible-population estimate. The product has no clinical outcome data. Existing preventive care is the counterfactual, not no treatment.

## Annual planning basis

| Input | Assumption and provenance |
| --- | --- |
| Strip volume | 1,000,000/year, hypothetical scale case from the README; not sales or delivered supply. |
| Strip course | Two strips per assessed lesion; maximum 500,000 courses before waste or noncompletion. This is lesion-level capacity, not unique patients. |
| Strip manufacturing cost | US$0.30–0.60 each at this volume, unverified README planning target including rejects; US$300,000–600,000/year in strip-only spend. |
| Completed-course fraction | Low/base/high assumptions of 60% / 80% / 90% of nominal courses; includes missed second applications but assumes no separate manufacturing loss. |
| Incremental 12-month non-progression | Low/base/high **hypotheses** of 0 / 5 / 10 percentage points among completed courses versus fluoride-based standard care; no measured efficacy exists. |
| Pouch + backing mass | Illustrative 2 g/strip **assumption**, subject to weighing prototype parts; 2,000 kg/year before clinical-waste handling. |

## Conditional outcomes at that volume

| Case | Completed lesion courses/year | Additional lesions non-progressing at 12 months versus standard care | Strip-only spend |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low / no incremental efficacy | 300,000 | 0 | US$300,000–600,000 |
| Base hypothesis | 400,000 | 20,000 | US$300,000–600,000 |
| High hypothesis | 450,000 | 45,000 | US$300,000–600,000 |

Formula: completed courses = (1,000,000 strips ÷ 2) × completion fraction; additional non-progressing lesions = completed courses × *incremental* percentage-point difference. The high scenario is not a ceiling on clinical uncertainty: harm or worse outcomes than control remain possible. These counts are **not** cavities, fillings or dental visits prevented. Multiple lesions in one person are counted separately; no global prevalence is multiplied into this model.

Strip-only expenditure excludes screening, worker time, follow-up, shipping, taxes, failures and waste management, so dividing it by the modeled benefit would misrepresent cost-effectiveness. A clinic study must measure those costs and the comparator's costs before claiming savings. If incremental efficacy is zero or negative, the environmental and financial burden has no demonstrated offset. At the illustrative 2 g/strip, 1 million single-use pouches/backings create 2 tonnes of material requiring a locally compliant disposal route; this is a mass estimate, **not** a lifecycle carbon figure.

## Decisions needed before extrapolation

Measure eligibility per clinic population; manufacturing yield; retention and real-time stability; lesion-level 12-month incremental non-progression with confidence intervals; adverse events and missed advanced disease; full delivered cost; and measured packaging mass/disposal impacts. Do not scale the 5–10-point hypotheses or call them population forecasts until independently replicated clinical outcomes justify it. Sugar reduction, fluoride toothpaste and professional diagnosis remain the baseline. See the README's evidence links and the five gates in ROADMAP.md.
