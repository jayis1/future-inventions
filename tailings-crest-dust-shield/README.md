# Tailings Crest Dust Shield

- **Author:** jayis1
- **Status:** Concept (TRL 2; untested assembly)
- **Development horizon:** 10–15 years for site-qualified modular manufacturing and monitored field trials

## Problem

Exposed mine tailings can disperse fine particles into nearby communities, crops and waterways, particularly in dry regions.[2] Wind-tunnel and field observations at some gold-tailings dams identify the upper windward slopes and the zone below the crest as more erodible than the flat top; this pattern is site-dependent and must be measured, not generalized to every impoundment.[1] Repeated watering is difficult to sustain in arid regions and temporary chemical crusts can fail.[2] The population protected by this specific design is **unknown** until sites and exposure are assessed.

## Solution

The **Tailings Crest Dust Shield** is a repairable, segmented surface-armor retrofit for *inactive, geotechnically stable* dry tailings embankments. A qualified engineer first maps wind direction, surface erosion, slope stability, drainage and contaminant leaching. Only eligible upper windward slopes receive overlapping, mechanically retained modules, rather than automatically treating the entire pile.

Each nominal **0.5 m × 0.5 m module** has a textured, noncombustible recycled-glass/ceramic outer tile; a captive stainless-steel mesh edge and replaceable mechanical anchors into an independently designed stable cover layer; a graded mineral drainage layer and filter fabric below to prevent fine migration; and a visible edge-lift/anchor witness mark for inspection. Tile ridges interrupt near-surface wind, overlap joints reduce direct exposure of tailings, and the graded layer directs rainwater to an engineered, sampled collection channel rather than pooling behind the shield. A color-marked sacrificial witness strip at selected joints reveals displacement or undercutting. No chemicals or living organisms are released onto the tailings.

**Design targets, not measured performance:** at a site with a verified stable cover layer, protect a 100 m² pilot strip through one full windy/rainy season; reduce *area-normalized emitted dust mass* by at least 50% versus matched uncovered and conventional rock-armored test plots under comparable wind exposure; keep runoff and leachate analytes below the site's permitted limits, and retain at least 95% of modules without uplift or displacement. Wind-tunnel tests must span the site's measured wind-speed distribution and dry/wet cycling; field monitors record upwind/downwind PM10 plus deposition and meteorology, with independent background correction. A PM10 concentration improvement alone cannot establish source reduction or health benefit.

## Key Innovation

The proposed advance is a **selective, replaceable crest-and-slope armor with a measurable failure witness and controlled runoff path**. Rock armoring, soil caps, drainage layers and erosion controls are established prior art.[1][3] The unproven hypothesis is that site-mapped modular coverage can suppress emissions comparably to full-slope rock armor with less imported material while making local failures visible and repairable. It is not a substitute for an engineered closure cap, structural stabilization, acid-drainage control or compliance monitoring.

## Target Cost

**Unquoted engineering target:** US$35 per 0.25 m² module (US$140/m²) ex-factory at 50,000 modules/year by 2038, including recycled-glass tile, mineral layer, filter fabric and mechanical retainers. Preliminary planning allowance: US$40/m² for installation on an already qualified, accessible stable cover and US$5/m²/year for inspection and spot replacement; no cost reduction is assumed until a site-specific bill of quantities is priced. Excludes geotechnical investigation, cover construction, grading, drainage and leachate treatment, worker protection, permits, access, transport, monitoring and long-term stewardship. Material reuse is conditional on contaminant testing; contaminated modules require licensed disposal.

## Impact

A 100 m² paired pilot could test source-specific dust reduction and material use, **not** health outcomes. At 1,000 eligible sites with 1,000 m² treated each, nominal covered area would be 1 km²; actual dust avoided depends on source strength, wind, failure rates and maintenance. If the comparative target holds, fewer exposed fine particles could reach neighboring settlements, but no avoided illness or universal deployment claim is justified. Local repair and mineral-rich recycled materials could lower maintenance and virgin-rock demand only if a cradle-to-grave assessment confirms them.

## Safety and Validation Gates

1. **No installation on active, unstable or saturated dams.** Independent geotechnical sign-off must establish slope stability and anchor load paths; armor must not hide cracks, seepage, movement or drainage outlets. Stop work and investigate any deformation.
2. **Runoff and contaminants:** bench leach tests on tile and fabric; measure dissolved metals, pH and conductivity upstream/downstream of pilot collection. A barrier that reroutes contaminated water off-site fails even if it stops dust.
3. **Storm and fire:** test anchor pullout, edge uplift, UV and freeze–thaw where relevant, high-intensity rain erosion, and noncombustibility. Do not seal the toe or increase pore pressure; provide an engineered overflow route.
4. **Maintenance:** inspect after storms and at least quarterly during the first year; isolate any lifted modules, wet-clean under dust-control procedures, and replace damaged sections. Workers require site-specific dust and contaminant protection. No public access is enabled by the armor.
5. **Falsification:** compare identical slope/wind exposure plots for at least a season, including bare and standard rock-armor controls; report dust flux by wind bin, water chemistry, displaced tiles, imported mass/m², installed and lifecycle cost. Abandon the design if it increases instability or contaminated runoff, or cannot outperform a conventional cover on a justified material/cost basis.
