# Roadmap — Thermoacoustic Metamaterial Heat Pump

> From concept to ubiquity: a five-phase development plan to replace vapor-compression HVAC with solid-state thermoacoustic heat pumping.

---

## Phase 1 — Concept (Years 0–2)

Establish the scientific foundation. Physics-informed neural networks co-optimize the phononic crystal lens geometry, stack parameters, and bandgap reflector placement using coupled linearized Euler / Rott-equation simulations. Validate the core hypothesis — that metamaterial wave shaping can achieve traveling-wave Stirling phasing in a single compact resonator at 3–8 bar helium — through finite-element acoustic simulation (COMSOL / DeltaEC) and small-scale bench validation of individual phononic crystal elements (acoustic gain, bandgap suppression). Publish peer-reviewed evidence that the integrated concept is thermodynamically and acoustically viable. Target output: a validated digital twin of a 3 kW resonator with predicted COP ≥ 3.0 and a component-level TRL 3 demonstration of the phononic crystal lens (8–15 dB gain) and bandgap reflector (>20 dB suppression).

## Phase 2 — Prototype (Years 2–5)

Build and test the first integrated benchtop prototype. A 1–3 kW resonator is fabricated with 3D-printed metal (AlSi10Mg) and high-temperature polymer (PEEK) metamaterial internals, a moving-magnet linear motor driver, and micro-channel copper heat exchangers. Measure COP across the full temperature range (−20 °C to +45 °C outdoor), map acoustic field distribution inside the resonator with embedded piezo sensors, and iterate the AI-optimized geometry against measured performance. Validate 100,000-hour flexure-bearing life through accelerated wear testing. Demonstrate electronic reversibility (heating ↔ cooling via phase shift). Target: TRL 4–5, measured COP ≥ 3.0 at rated conditions and COP ≥ 2.5 at −20 °C, with a credible path to COP 4.0+ in the next iteration.

## Phase 3 — Pilot (Years 5–8)

Deploy 50–200 field-pilot units in three target niches where conventional heat pumps fail or are inaccessible: (a) cold-climate retrofits in Canada/Scandinavia (−20 °C heating), (b) off-grid solar-direct deployments in Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia (48 V DC operation), and (c) industrial process-heat recovery pilots (150–250 °C lift). Collect 12–24 months of real-world COP, reliability, and acoustic-noise data. Establish manufacturing partnerships for high-volume 3D-printed metamaterial components and linear motors. Develop the installer-free "homeowner installable" packaging and documentation. Pursue safety certification (UL/CE) for a non-pressurized, non-refrigerant device — a simpler regulatory path than conventional AC. Target: TRL 6–7, field-validated COP ≥ 3.5, <45 dB external noise, zero refrigerant-leak incidents, and a certified product ready for limited production.

## Phase 4 — Scale (Years 8–14)

Ramp to mass production. Build a dedicated manufacturing line producing 100,000–1,000,000 units/year, leveraging the TMHP's inherently simpler BOM (no compressor, no valves, no oil, no hermetic seal) to undercut conventional heat pump manufacturing cost by 30–50%. Expand product line: 3 kW wall-mount ($400–800), 10 kW whole-home, 50 kW commercial rooftop, 500 kW industrial. Open-license the installer-free protocol to accelerate adoption in regions without HVAC technician infrastructure. Partner with solar microgrid providers for bundled solar + TMHP + battery packages targeting the 770 million off-grid population. Target: TRL 8–9, 1–10 million units deployed, demonstrated 30+ year service life in the field, $600 residential entry point achieved at scale, and measurable displacement of refrigerant and fossil heating in early-adopter markets.

## Phase 5 — Ubiquity (Years 14–25)

The TMHP becomes the default HVAC technology for new construction and replacement markets worldwide. Vapor-compression units are relegated to legacy installations, driven by a combination of cost advantage, regulatory refrigerant phase-down (Kigali Amendment), and the TMHP's cold-climate superiority. Global deployment reaches hundreds of millions of units, eliminating 1.0–1.5 Gt CO₂-eq/yr of refrigerant leakage and displacing 500–800 TWh/yr of resistive/fossil heating. The 770 million off-grid population gains affordable solar-powered thermal comfort. Industrial process-heat recovery captures 10–30 EJ/yr of fossil gas demand. The compressor — a 19th-century invention — retires from the building sector, joining the incandescent bulb and the internal combustion engine in the museum of superseded technologies. The solid-state HVAC revolution is complete.
