# Tidal Marsh Sediment Ratchet — Conditional Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1. Status: scenario arithmetic, not observed performance or a forecast.** Scope: a hypothetical program of 10,000 sheltered, permitted sites, each with one 20 m² treated cell, over one year of elevation measurement and three years of device ownership. No location is assumed to qualify automatically.

## Inputs and boundary conditions

| Input | Scenario assumption | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treated cell | 20 m²/site | Design illustration, not verified footprint |
| Program size | 10,000 sites | Hypothetical adoption, not committed deployment |
| Pilot advantage | 2 mm/year greater *net surface-elevation gain* than matched fixed coir | Untested target, not absolute gain or guaranteed resilience |
| Dry bulk density | 500 kg/m³ | Illustrative conversion only; sample locally |
| Exchange / suspended solids / capture | 1,000 m³/year/site; 100 mg/L; 20% retained | Illustrative sediment-rich site, no measured capture |
| Hardware | Two 1 m modules/site, each US$57 plus one US$18 slat replacement over three years | Unquoted 2038 mass-manufacturing goal, not installed price |

Eligibility requires suitable tide/flow geometry, clean sediment, open fish/drainage bypass, consent, permits, and measured treated elevation keeping pace with *local* relative sea-level rise without harming neighboring cells. Count a cell as a resilience benefit only after these conditions are verified against untreated and equal-mass fixed-coir controls; two years is a pilot minimum, not proof of permanence. SET–MH separates deposited thickness from net elevation, while tidal benchmarks account for relative sea level.[1]

## Traceable calculations

- Candidate area = 10,000 sites × 20 m²/site = **200,000 m² = 20 ha treated**. This is *not* restored or protected area.
- Per qualifying site, the *difference versus fixed coir* at the target rate = 20 m² × 0.002 m/year = **0.04 m³/year of elevation-equivalent volume**. The same result cannot be assumed in unqualified cells or equated to flood protection.
- If deposition alone explained the difference, an illustrative mineral-equivalent requirement = 0.04 m³/year × 500 kg/m³ = **20 kg dry solids/year/site**. Actual accretion, compaction, root growth and erosion require separate measurement.
- Hypothetical incoming suspended load = 1,000 m³/year × 100 mg/L × 1,000 L/m³ ÷ 1,000,000 mg/kg = **100 kg/year/site**; retaining 20% = **20 kg/year/site**. This ignores competing sinks and is a feasibility check, *not* proof of capture. At 10 mg/L with otherwise unchanged exchange and fraction, only **2 kg/year/site** is retained-equivalent; the design target becomes implausible without other elevation processes or increased supply.

| Scenario | Sites passing every gate | Verified candidate benefit area | Annual elevation-equivalent difference vs fixed coir at target* |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low: sediment-poor or harmful side effects | 0% = 0 | 0 ha | 0 m³/year |
| Central: conditional | 25% = 2,500 | 5 ha | 100 m³/year |
| High: conditional | 50% = 5,000 | 10 ha | 200 m³/year |

*Only if qualifying cells actually achieve the 2 mm/year differential; do not add this differential to a measured absolute elevation trend twice. If local relative sea-level rise exceeds the treated trend, the site fails regardless of its differential. At the illustrative density, central/high volume corresponds to 50/100 tonnes dry mineral-equivalent per year, not verified sediment burial or carbon removal.

## Costs and distribution

Hardware for two modules/site over three years = 2 × (US$57 + US$18) = **US$150/site**. A deliberately explicit *unquoted* planning allowance adds US$350/site for survey, permissions and installation, US$300/site for shared monitoring allocation, and US$150/site for inspections, retrieval and disposal: **US$950/site/three years**, or **US$9.5 million/10,000 sites** before storm repairs, land rights and major lab testing. Actual permits, SET–MH networks and monitoring can cost much more; obtain local bids and disclose payer/maintenance responsibility. Costs are borne by restoration sponsors/cooperatives; habitat and flood-related benefits may accrue elsewhere, so no cost-benefit ratio is asserted. At 25% qualification, program spend divided by verified candidate area is US$1.9 million/ha over three years; this is *not* a competitive price claim against thin-layer placement, which adds sediment and operates at another scale.[2]

No carbon credit, avoided flood loss, fisheries yield or jobs total is estimated: these require site-specific baselines, lifecycle emissions, methane/N₂O accounting, permanence, displacement and beneficiaries. Retained mineral sediment is not automatically additional carbon. Erosion elsewhere can cancel local gains; periodic adjacent-cell surveys and removal costs belong in any net assessment. Storm damage, lower sediment availability or weak maintenance can reduce the qualified fraction to zero.
