# Vector-Sealed Water Lid — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1** · Illustrative planning scenarios only; no prototype or field efficacy measured.

## Boundary and baseline

WHO describes tight-fitting lids as an existing approach to container-source reduction, and CDC recommends tightly covering stored water and weekly emptying/scrubbing.[1][2] The counterfactual is therefore a correctly used affordable conventional tight lid, not an open barrel. This design's incremental effect over that baseline is **unknown**. Count eligible, functioning storage sites, not infections prevented, treated water, people protected, or households reached.

## Five-year conditional model

Assume the program assesses 10,000 / 50,000 / 100,000 candidate sites (low / base / high). For illustration only, 80% have eligible 180–300 mm rigid rims and independently protected outlets; the remainder need different solutions. Assume the fraction of installed sites that stays correctly fitted, maintained and in service averages 70% / 85% / 95% over five years. These fractions are **assumptions**, not observed performance, and may vary by season.

| Scenario | Candidates | Eligible installed (×0.80) | Average functional sites (× uptime) | Functional site-years (×5) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 10,000 | 8,000 | 5,600 | 28,000 |
| Base | 50,000 | 40,000 | 34,000 | 170,000 |
| High | 100,000 | 80,000 | 76,000 | 380,000 |

A functional site-year is one storage site operated with an intact barrier for a year, **not** a measured reduction in mosquito emergence. Multiply the functional sites by 365 only if daily site-days are needed; no claim of uninterrupted individual protection follows from an average. The high scenario installs 80,000 lids, not 100,000: the latter is a candidate-site ceiling.

## Costs and resource burden

Use the README's **unquoted targets** of US$24 ex-factory, US$5–10 fitting and US$3/year parts per installed unit, with five years of service; no discounting or inflation adjustment. Illustrative five-year spend per installation = 24 + (5 to 10) + 5×3 = **US$44–49**. Across the three scenarios that gives **US$352,000–392,000**, **US$1.76–1.96 million**, and **US$3.52–3.92 million** respectively. These are not procurement quotes and omit vessels, taps, water treatment, freight, taxes, training, cleaning labor, tests, breakage, capital tooling and end-of-life fees. A lower uptime shrinks functional site-years without reducing upfront expenditure. At end of service, separate 316L mesh/metal from polymer parts only where local recovery exists; count unrecovered material as waste, not automatically recycled. Mass, energy and carbon footprints await a measured bill of materials and life-cycle assessment.

## What could actually be measured

Pilot against a well-fitted simple lid: site-level adult ingress, larvae, closure and cleaning compliance, time spent opening/refilling, maintenance and water-quality changes across one wet season. Monitor other breeding sites and household practices so any difference is not falsely attributed to one vessel. Disease incidence needs a separate sufficiently powered, ethics-approved study; no numerical infections-averted or lives-saved estimate is supportable from these scenarios. Reject the product if its greater complexity worsens sustained use or safety.
