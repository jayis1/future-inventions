# Vector-Sealed Water Lid

**Author: jayis1** · **Status: research concept (TRL 2)** · **Category: Drinking Water & Public Health / Household Mosquito Source Prevention**

## Problem

Households without continuous piped water often store water in wide-mouth vessels. An uncovered or poorly fitted vessel can become a breeding site for container-breeding *Aedes* mosquitoes; a conventional loose cover may be removed for every refill and left off. Disease prevention also depends on eliminating other breeding sites and established public-health measures. A lid cannot make contaminated water safe to drink or prove a reduction in dengue.

## Solution

A repairable retrofit lid for **eligible 180–300 mm round-mouth, rigid, non-pressurized vessels with a separate protected outlet**. A food-contact polypropylene compression ring and replaceable EPDM gasket clamp to a smooth, structurally sound rim without glue. Water enters through a wide, removable filling funnel and a **normally closed, spring-return silicone flap**: the funnel opens the flap only while seated, and its insect-screened throat remains in place during filling. Withdrawal closes the flap before the funnel's screen leaves the opening. A separately seated 316L stainless-steel screen protects a downward-draining overflow, and another protects the pressure-equalizing air vent. Mesh is captive behind removable service frames (proposed maximum clear aperture **0.4 mm**, subject to local vector-species challenge tests). No pesticide, electronics, network connection or personal data.

The fill path, vent, overflow, rim and outlet must all be screened or sealed. The design is **not** for an open-bucket dipping practice, damaged rims, or submerged/flooded vessels; where users need to dip, a different safe storage method is needed. A large hinged service opening makes cleaning possible but has a visible red seal-seating tab; it is opened only when the vessel is drained for cleaning. A broken tab, stuck flap, torn mesh or inaccessible outlet means remove the vessel from protected service, empty/cover it by an approved alternative, and repair it. This physical closure addresses mosquito access only, not waterborne pathogens; clean containers and independently qualified drinking-water treatment remain necessary.

## Key Innovation

Tight lids and screened vents already exist. The proposed contribution is a **sequenced refill boundary** that keeps an insect barrier across every state of routine filling, paired with individually removable vent/overflow screens and a mechanically visible service-seal fault. Unlike the repository's Flood-Safe Egg Sink, which deliberately attracts mosquitoes into a serviced trap, this design aims to **exclude** them from stored household water; unlike the Acoustic Vector Disruption Array it does not interfere with mating or monitor insects. Its value over a cheap, correctly used tight lid is a testable hypothesis, not an established advance.

## Target Cost

**Unquoted 2038 design target:** US$24 ex-factory per lid at 100,000/year (ring and cover US$8; silicone flap, spring and screened funnel US$6; three captive mesh/frame assemblies US$5; EPDM gasket, seal tab and assembly inspection US$5). Estimated US$5–10 installation/fitting, US$3/year replacement gaskets/screens, and a five-year service life are unvalidated targets. A compatible vessel, protected draw-off tap, cleaning labor, distribution, taxes, training and water-quality treatment/testing are **excluded**. Locally replaceable mesh and gaskets are preferable to sealed proprietary cartridges; recycle metal and polymer separately where collection exists.

## Impact

**Proposed pilot, not measured benefit:** Compare 300 fitted, maintained vessels against 300 matched vessels with the best locally affordable conventional tight lid over one wet season. Measure user-reported and observed lid-off time, filling convenience, adult entry and larval presence, screen condition, bypass attempts and maintenance time; separately monitor drinking-water quality so the lid is not mistaken for disinfection. A planning scale of 100,000 eligible vessels could cover at most 100,000 household storage sites, not 100,000 protected households or infections averted. Any disease-outcome claim requires an appropriately powered, ethics-approved public-health trial accounting for other breeding sources and vector-control interventions.

### Engineering gates and limits

- **Bench targets (unverified):** On 60 complete units spanning tolerance extremes, zero mosquito entry or adult emergence over two full local *Aedes* development cycles, including repeated fills, aged seals and a blocked-overflow challenge. Any entry is a redesign stop, not an acceptable percentage loss. Confirm that a torn screen or unseated service hatch is visually detectable before use; a red tab is not a water-quality indicator.
- **Water balance:** For an illustrative 0.1 m² exposed collection area and 100 mm/hour rain, incoming rain is 10 L/hour (0.17 L/min), before splash; test unobstructed overflow capacity at ≥1 L/min and measure clogging with leaves/silt. A blocked or submerged overflow invalidates the protection claim. Target ≥3 L/min refill at 0.5 m gravity head with the screened funnel, verified on real vessels; do not assume the flow follows from mesh aperture alone.
- **Durability:** Target 2,000 fill/closure cycles, 12 months outdoor UV exposure and weekly cleaning without torn mesh, stuck flap, trapped stagnant pockets or gasket loss. Test a pressure-equalized draw-off tap and any hose attachment as additional mosquito paths. Use materials certified for the applicable potable-water contact standard before drinking-water deployment; screen manufacturing tolerances, spring corrosion and cleaning contamination require independent review.
- **Decision path (10–20 years):** Bench containment and flow tests → co-design with households and local vector authorities → controlled seasonal comparison with conventional lids → independent health and water-safety review before scaled manufacture. If correct use or upkeep is worse than a simple tight lid, retire the design rather than claiming a health benefit.
