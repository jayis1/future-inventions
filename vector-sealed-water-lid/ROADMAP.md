# Vector-Sealed Water Lid — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1** · Concept-stage gates; dates and performance are not validated.

## Concept

Co-design with households, water providers and local vector-control teams; survey rim geometry, refill and tap practices, and compare the cheapest usable tight lid. Produce a dimensioned cutaway proving the screened funnel closes its barrier before the flap opens and the reverse on removal. Exit only if fitting, cleaning and use without dipping are feasible; reject incompatible vessels.

## Prototype

Build 60 units across rim/tolerance extremes. Independently check water-contact material grades and test screened-flow targets (≥3 L/min fill at 0.5 m head; ≥1 L/min clear overflow), flap sequencing, outlet/vent bypasses, 2,000 cycles, UV exposure and locally relevant *Aedes* access over two development cycles with appropriate controls. Any adult ingress, unguarded overflow, stuck flap or unsafe contact material halts progression for redesign.

## Pilot

With ethics and public-health oversight, fit 300 maintained eligible vessels and compare with 300 matched vessels using the best locally affordable conventional tight lid for one wet season. Observe actual lid-off time, larval presence, usability, water quality, servicing labor and dropout; do not infer disease reduction. Advance only if protection, upkeep and acceptance are at least as good as the comparator under the pre-registered protocol.

## Scale

Qualify local molding, screen/frame and gasket suppliers against dimensional and water-contact specifications; audit installation fit and replacement-part availability in several climates. Verify field faults, costs and disposal pathways before a 100,000/year manufacturing target; pause shipments if batch tests or community maintenance fail.

## Ubiquity

Offer interoperable, locally repairable parts where stored water remains necessary, alongside reliable piped water and other vector-control measures. Renew only where surveillance demonstrates sustained closure of eligible sites, safe water practices and favorable cost/use against simple lids; sunset the product when superior infrastructure or cheaper covers make it redundant.
