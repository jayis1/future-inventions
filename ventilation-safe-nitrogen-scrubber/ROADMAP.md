# Ventilation-Safe Nitrogen Scrubber — Roadmap

**Author: jayis1. Research concept; every gate below is prospective, not a completed demonstration.** The governing design point is 10,000 m³/h of 20 ppmv NH₃ exhaust at 25 °C: ≥70% removal during a 30-day service cycle, ≤40 Pa clean-to-service added resistance, and independently verified preservation of required barn airflow. Installed hardware is a US$16,000 volume-cost target (US$12,000 ex-factory plus US$4,000 site work), not a current quote.

## Concept

Close the NH₃–nitrogen–acid–water balance and screen candidate mechanically ventilated barns for concentration, fan headroom, safe chemical handling and a lawful product outlet. Compare the conventional spray scrubber and source-control options before specifying a cassette. Exit only with a reviewed design envelope, a site-specific bypass-flow requirement and an explicit no-go rule for sites that cannot preserve ventilation.

## Prototype

Bench-test PP/FRP spray, demister, bunded HDPE loop and a power-independent full-flow bypass on representative dusty exhaust. The prospective gate is ≥70% NH₃ removal at 20 ppmv and 10,000 m³/h throughout a 30-day cycle, ≤40 Pa clean-to-service rise where the host fan allows it, measured acid-mist control and a witnessed blocked-cassette/power-loss bypass test. Measure pump energy and waste as well as incremental fan power; do not infer them from the 0.185 kW calculated fan point.

## Pilot

Run a 10-site, 12-month paired treatment/bypass trial with independent ventilation and acid-safety oversight. Report seasonal flow-weighted mass flux, downtime, chemical purchases, product assays, pressure, mist, electricity and labor; do not count bypass hours as removal. Exit only if the removal/pressure gates remain true at qualifying inlet conditions, required airflow never falls, no acid escapes containment, and at least one eligible site has a better site-specific net case than its conventional alternative.

## Scale

Qualify multiple fabricators and trained chemical-service partners; independently audit failure tests and batch traceability. Validate rather than assume the 2038 target of US$12,000 ex-factory plus US$4,000 installation at 10,000 modules/year; publish total installed and operating cost alongside a conventional scrubber comparison. Exit only after repeatable independent field performance and compliant fertilizer or disposal routes, without relaxing fan protection.

## Ubiquity

Consider broad deployment by 2050 only at eligible ducted exhaust sites after regulators, farmers and independent engineers can verify outdoor NH₃ reduction without sacrificing barn ventilation. A service network should publish audited captured nitrogen, bypass time, lifecycle inputs and safety incidents. The phase has no automatic adoption quota: if source control or an incumbent scrubber is better, use that instead.
