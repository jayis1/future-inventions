# Verified Abandoned-Well Seal — Impact Analysis

**Author: jayis1** | All scenario figures below are *illustrative assumptions, not observed benefits or a forecast*. No well has yet been treated with this concept. The existing README's U.S. unplugged-well inventory and historic plugging-cost estimates come from [2]; this exercise uses a hypothetical 100-well procurement cohort, **not** a fraction of that national inventory.

## Accounting boundary and equations

- Cohort: 100 candidate retired oil/gas wells. A licensed engineer screens each for access, records, hazards, groundwater protection and legal authority. The treated count excludes sites deferred to other approved methods.
- Count only **verified** treated wells: subsurface regulatory acceptance **and** comparable pad-plus-perimeter methane mass-flux measurements. Inconclusive/failed tests, diverted gas and missing follow-up count as *zero credited reduction* pending remediation and re-verification, not as proven zero emissions.
- One-year modeled methane reduction (tonnes CH₄) = verified wells × assumed baseline per well (kg CH₄/h) × assumed fractional reduction × 8,760 h/year ÷ 1,000 kg/tonne. Continuous operation and year-round persistence are simplifying assumptions; the actual duration, seasonal variance and counterfactual emissions require repeated measurements.[1]
- No CO₂-equivalent conversion or groundwater/health valuation is asserted. Passing a pressure test is a separate safety condition, not a methane-rate measurement.

These cases are deliberately *not* extrapolated to 2.1 million unplugged wells. Emissions are heterogeneous; a previously non-emitting well cannot be credited with these assumed savings. The ≥90% flux reduction in the README is a proposed **per-site pilot target**, not an established cohort outcome; the cautious and middle cases deliberately fail that aspirational threshold and would not justify a performance claim. Neither a below-detection reading nor a short-term success establishes annual persistence.[1]

## Costs, capacity and verification

The README's unquoted hardware/material allowance is US$960 per screened shallow treated well: 25/50/75 deployments imply US$24,000/48,000/72,000 respectively **only for incremental kit and materials**, before contractor labor, rig time, cement placement, permits, waste, restoration, monitoring or rework. The historical US$20,000 plugging-only and US$76,000 with-restoration averages from [2] are broad context from different wells and years—not prices to add mechanically to the allowance or evidence of savings. Actual unit economics must be measured on matched jobs.

For a workload thought experiment, at an assumed 10 screened jobs per licensed crew-year, the three deployment counts would require 2.5/5/7.5 crew-years before rework, travel and exclusions; these are planning ratios, not field productivity measurements. Monitoring at baseline, immediately after, 6 months and 12 months means at least 4 survey occasions per treated site, or 100/200/300 survey occasions in the three cases, plus any retests. The program must budget independent reviewers, calibration, safe access and a named long-term steward. No labor saving, aquifer benefit or 10-year durability is credited without evidence.

## What would change the estimate

A controlled 10-well pilot should publish per-well baseline and post-work rates (with detection limits and conditions), exact integrity-test outcomes, uninterpretable/deferred counts, rework, perimeter migration, total costs and repeat surveys. Stratify by geology, depth and prior emissions rather than averaging only successes. If unexpected gas or water migration appears, pause, secure the site, refer to the qualified engineer and regulator, and withhold impact credit until remedied and independently retested. Independent validation at 6 and 12 months is a first checkpoint, not proof that the full annual projection or lifetime containment holds.
