# Verified Abandoned-Well Seal

**Author: jayis1**

**Status: Research concept (TRL 2); not an approved well-plugging method**

**Category: Climate & Groundwater Protection / Orphaned-Well Remediation**

## Problem

Abandoned oil and gas wells can connect gas-bearing rock to aquifers and the atmosphere through damaged casing, failed cement or the annulus. A 2021 analysis estimates roughly 2.1 million unplugged abandoned wells in the United States, while stressing uncertainty in the inventory; its cost data show that difficult wells can be far more expensive than ordinary ones.[2] The federal orphaned-well guidance distinguishes methane concentration from emissions *rate* and calls for before/after measurement rather than inferring mitigation from a visible cap.[1] A surface cap alone may divert gas into soil or groundwater. This is a climate and local water-integrity problem, not a reason to treat every well as an equal methane emitter.

## Solution

A **licensed-contractor-only, interval-addressed sealing workflow** pairs a temporary, retrievable diagnostic test string with a well-specific permanent cementing program. After record review, gas monitoring and a qualified mechanical-integrity assessment, an inflatable elastomer test packer on a steel service tube isolates one accessible interval at a time. Pressure observations and sampled fluid/gas identify *candidate* communicating intervals; no pressure reading alone proves the location of an external annular leak. Where regulation and the well-specific design permit, a contractor places a compatible, laboratory-qualified swelling elastomer annular element *as an auxiliary barrier* and Class G oil-well cement plugs across mapped intervals and protected formations. Cement—not the elastomer—provides the prescribed permanent isolation. The temporary test string is removed; there is no permanent sensor, network, or new flow connection across aquifers.

The proposed portable kit consists of a corrosion-compatible test tube and packer, mechanical pressure gauges, sample ports, gas-safe fittings and a paper test record. It does **not** replace the licensed plugging crew, workover rig, blowout-prevention equipment where required, cement design or regulator approval. A damaged casing, lost circulation, multiple aquifers, high-pressure flow, inaccessible interval, hydrogen sulfide, unknown ownership or missing records triggers a **do-not-deploy gate** until a qualified well engineer and authority establish a safe alternative. A seal that redirects leakage without isolation is a failed intervention.

## Key Innovation

Packers, swelling elastomers, cement plugs and methane surveys are established practice. The research hypothesis is a **reusable interval-test cassette with a two-channel acceptance record**: one channel checks wellbore/annular pressure integrity at the jurisdiction-approved test conditions; the other independently compares surface methane *mass flux* before and after plugging over the well pad and adjacent soil. It cannot certify subsurface integrity from surface flux or vice versa. Compare against the same contractor's conventional assessment and cement-plug program on similar wells; do not claim novelty or patentability of the components. Unlike the repository's [Atmospheric Methane Oxidation Array](../atmospheric-methane-oxidation-array/) (ambient methane destruction) and [Flood-Isolating Wellhead Collar](../flood-isolating-wellhead-collar/) (flood protection for drinking-water wellheads), this targets leakage paths in retired oil/gas wells.

**Unverified design targets (not measured performance):** On shallow, accessible, low-pressure pilot wells selected by a licensed engineer, resolve a candidate leak interval to within ±5 m *when isolation tests are interpretable*; achieve ≥90% lower measured surface methane mass flux than the same site's baseline, with a stated detection limit, and **pass the applicable regulator's pressure-integrity acceptance test** after plugging. Do not convert a non-detect into a claim of zero emissions. Target 10-year demonstrated integrity under annual review before claiming a longer service life; permanent plugging must meet the jurisdiction's actual lifetime requirements, regardless of this research milestone. These figures are experiment go/no-go thresholds, not established capability.

## Target Cost

**Unquoted 2038 target, at 1,000 kits/year:** US$1,200 per reusable diagnostic kit (US$430 steel tubing/fittings, US$300 replaceable packer elements, US$220 gauges/sampling ports, US$250 assembly and qualification); amortized over 20 qualified jobs with US$100/job cleaning, element replacement and calibration allowance, the diagnostic hardware goal is US$160/job. Cement, auxiliary elastomer and consumables: *planning allowance* US$800/job for a screened shallow well. This yields US$960/job of incremental kit/material allowance, **not** a total installed price; well survey, licensed labor, rig time, gas control, permitting, cement placement, monitoring, site restoration and waste handling are extra and site-dependent. A published multi-state analysis reports roughly US$20,000 per well for plugging only and US$76,000 with restoration in its historic data (not 2038 quotes), with extreme variation.[2] If the kit adds cost without improving verified integrity or prioritization, it should not be adopted.

## Impact

A screened, **10-well comparative pilot** aims to demonstrate independent baseline/after methane measurements (g CH₄/h) at each accessible site and documented pressure-integrity outcomes; no global savings or health benefits are claimed in advance. As an illustrative conditional budget, if a well emitted 100 g CH₄/h continuously before work and 10 g/h afterward, the difference would be 90 g/h **only during comparable measurement conditions**; seasonal persistence and groundwater protection would still be unknown. Federal guidance discusses ground-based direct methods, quality assurance and variability in emission measurements, so repeated, instrument-calibrated surveys matter.[1]

**Validation and safety:** First bench-test the packer and compatible materials in representative brines and pressure/temperature cycles; then obtain an independent engineering review and applicable permits. At each authorized field site, document baseline methane rate, soil/perimeter leakage, pressure history and nearby protected-water intervals. Have an independent specialist repeat post-work rate measurements using the same documented detection limit and comparable conditions, immediately after work and at 6 and 12 months; perform jurisdiction-approved integrity tests separately. Stop work and refer to the licensed engineer for unexpected pressure, gas, fluid migration or failed test. Never trial this on a live/high-pressure well without its own engineered plan. Dispose of contaminated fluids and spent elastomer as characterized regulated waste; recycle cleaned steel and repair gauges. Record data locally without location-sharing or surveillance. Across a 10–20-year path, proceed from bench compatibility to a 10-well pilot, then multi-geology trials and long-duration third-party monitoring before any procurement claim. Methane reduction, isolation durability, labor savings and any aquifer benefit remain unproven for this concept.
